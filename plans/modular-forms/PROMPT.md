# Modular Forms

I want a fourteen-part series from the hyperbolic plane to monstrous moonshine, for a reader who knows complex numbers and matrices but not modular forms. The through-line is that q-expansion coefficients carry information, and what they carry changes as the series goes: divisor sums, then Hecke eigenvalues, then point counts on elliptic curves, then dimensions of Monster representations. Geometry creates symmetry, symmetry makes the surviving functions scarce, scarcity makes their coefficients arithmetic, and one set of coefficients turns out to be representation theory.

By the end a reader should be able to picture the fundamental domain and its tiling, read a q-expansion as arithmetic, apply a Hecke operator, match a point count to a coefficient, and follow why 196884 = 1 + 196883 became a theorem.

## Articles

**Act I · The Symmetry Machine**

### 1. The Upper Half-Plane and Hyperbolic Geometry
The reader drags a point and watches a hyperbolic disk of fixed radius shrink toward the real axis, draws geodesics, and toggles to the disk model.

### 2. SL₂(ℤ), Generators and Relations
The modular group is generated by S and T with only the relations S² = (ST)³ = −I. The reader types any integer matrix and sees it written as a word in S and T and probes the two elliptic points the relations predict.

### 3. The Fundamental Domain
The fundamental domain, its gluings and its tiling of the plane. The reader drags any point and watches the reduction algorithm carry it home, and sees the quotient painted by j as a phase portrait.

### 4. Cusps and the Cusp-Form Condition
All rational boundary points form one cusp, the stabiliser of infinity gives the q-expansion, and cusp forms have zero constant term. The reader picks two cusps and sees the matrix carrying one to the other, then probes E₄, E₆ and Δ up the imaginary axis to see how many terms it takes to get them right.

**Act II · The Functions That Survive**

### 5. Modular Forms and Their Dimensions
The transformation law, the ring generated by E₄ and E₆, and the dimension formula. The reader checks the law numerically at any point and climbs a dimension staircase where exact basis reduction produces identities for free.

### 6. Eisenstein Series and q-Expansions
Eisenstein series are lattice sums whose q-expansions are divisor sums, with 691 appearing at weight 12. The reader watches the complex lattice sum converge to the q-series value.

### 7. The Discriminant and Ramanujan's τ
Δ is the unique normalised weight-12 cusp form; its coefficients τ(n) are multiplicative, congruent to σ₁₁ mod 691, and subject to Lehmer's open question. The reader tests multiplicativity on any coprime pair and checks the congruence across a table.

### 8. Hecke Operators and the Hecke Algebra
Hecke operators commute, and on eigenforms scaled so that a(1) = 1 they make coefficients multiplicative and the L-function an Euler product. The signature interactive is an exact Hecke calculator applying T_p to Δ, E₄, E₆ and a non-eigenform like E₄³.

**Act III · Coefficients Become Curves**

### 9. Weight 2, Modular Curves, and L-Functions
Under Γ₀(N) the quotient becomes a curve of positive genus, weight-2 cusp forms are its differentials, and the Mellin transform gives an L-function with a functional equation. The reader slides the level and sees genus and cusps change, then plots an L-function.

### 10. The Modularity Theorem and Elliptic Curves
Point counts of an elliptic curve over 𝔽_p are fixed by the coefficients of a weight-2 newform (#E(𝔽_p) = p + 1 − a_p at good primes); with Ribet's theorem this settles Fermat. The reader builds a curve, sees its points on a finite-field grid, and compares Frobenius traces with the newform.

**Act IV · Coefficients Become Characters**

### 11. The j-Function
j classifies complex elliptic curves, is the Hauptmodul for SL₂(ℤ), and has fast-growing integer coefficients. The reader drags τ and watches j move, with Heegner points and e^{π√163} as landmarks.

### 12. The Monster and McKay's Coincidence
The largest sporadic simple group, its order and character degrees, and McKay's observation that j's coefficients split into its irreducible dimensions. The reader assembles each coefficient from Monster irreps.

### 13. Vertex Operators and the Moonshine Module
The Conway–Norton conjecture read as a specification, and the Frenkel–Lepowsky–Meurman construction from the Leech lattice that meets it with graded dimension j − 744. The reader sees those dimensions computed from partition counts and a lattice theta series.

### 14. Borcherds' Proof and Beyond
Borcherds builds the Monster Lie algebra with the no-ghost theorem; its denominator identity is the product formula for j, and twisting by Monster elements finishes the proof. The reader multiplies out the product formula exactly and runs the replication cascade.

## What to get right

- Compute every coefficient exactly. Δ, τ(n), Hecke images and j coefficients overflow double precision fast. E₁₂ is the first Eisenstein series without integer coefficients. Over ℤ the ring needs Δ as a third generator.
- Monster data comes from the published ATLAS character table. The largest irreducible dimension is about 2.6 × 10²⁶, and the 194 classes give 171 distinct McKay–Thompson series. Co₁ is only a subquotient of the Monster; the subgroup is 2^{1+24}.Co₁.
- Attributions slip easily: Ribet proved level-lowering and Khare–Wintenberger proved Serre's conjecture; the product formula for j is due to Koike, Norton and Zagier; the root number is minus the Atkin–Lehner eigenvalue.
- Keep coefficients-as-witnesses visible in every act. Give τ, the group element and the form fixed colours across the series.
- A companion series on Lie algebras covers the E₈ and Leech lattices; link to it rather than rebuilding them.
