# Bioinformatics Visualization

I want an eight-article series on the standard plots and methods of modern bioinformatics, for readers with some biology or some data science who have seen these figures in papers and never learned to read them. Biology now measures thousands of cells, millions of bases and whole patient cohorts at once, and the field has built a vocabulary of plots for data that size. What makes this one series is a single biological story running through it: the tumor suppressor TP53 and its target CDKN1A (p21), and how losing TP53 can leave p21 switched off. The first article looks at that story through five kinds of data at once; each later article takes one kind and goes deep.

By the end a reader should be able to open a genome browser, a volcano plot, a clustered heatmap, a t-SNE or UMAP map, a gene-set enrichment plot or a Kaplan-Meier curve and say what it computes, what it hides, and what question it can answer.

## Articles

Four groups: the spine (1), the genome (2-4), expression (5-7), patients (8).

### 1. Five Views of a Broken Pathway
One question, how TP53 loss can leave CDKN1A (p21) off, asked of five kinds of data, with real and simulated data labeled caption by caption. (1) An oncoprint of the shared simulated cohort (BioCohort, 100 patients, 10 genes) with a CDKN1A mRNA row; it sorts itself into the TP53 stripe on first view, shows MDM2 amplification only in TP53-wildtype patients, and leaves 11 low-CDKN1A patients unexplained. (2) A lollipop of real TP53 mutations from the TCGA PanCancer Atlas (cBioPortal) with UniProt domains and zinc ligands, a missense/truncating toggle (missense crowd the DNA-binding domain and hotspots, truncating spread out), contact vs structural hotspot classes, and the cohort's simulated mutations as ticks; prose on why mutant p53 accumulates, with Terzian 2008's caveat. (3) The real hg38 CDKN1A promoter (CpGs, UCSC island, exon 1, both p53 response elements) with the cohort's simulated beta at the real CpGs and a route toggle (intact, TP53 mutant, MDM2 amplified, island methylated): p53 binds in CpG-poor DNA upstream, methylation closes the island at the start, and the unexplained patients are the methylated ones. (4) A pathway diagram with both MDM2 edges, opening at rest, damage and R175H toggles, animation order from rule propagation on the edge list. (5) A simulated single-cell dot plot of two tumors where only the malignant cells carry R175H, so stroma and immune cells are the internal control, with Newcombe intervals and a cells-per-type switch. Closing: what each view cannot say, and one patient where three views agree.

### 2. Genome Browser
Where genes sit on a chromosome, and how stacked tracks layer annotations, signal and reads on one coordinate axis. It stays at CDKN1A, the series' p21 gene, because articles 1 and 4 look at the same promoter. The reader pans and zooms a working browser over the real 40 kb hg38 window: Ensembl gene models, the two p53 response elements, CpG density and UCSC islands, RepeatMasker, per-base phyloP and the bases themselves. The tracks disagree: the island covers untranslated exon 1, conservation sits on the coding exons and on the 3' p53 site, and the most conserved stretch is a pseudogene. A genome-to-mRNA view of CDKN1A's three transcripts shows that an exon is not coding sequence, that "exon 1" depends on the transcript, and that all three make the same protein. A read pileup at TP53 R175H (real reference, simulated reads) makes the 0-based versus 1-based trap and the strand trap something the reader does: read the VCF or BED number in the wrong convention and land on a G with no variant reads, or switch to the transcript strand to see c.524G>A as the VCF's C>T. It ends on the one-window limit and hands off to Circos.

### 3. Circos Plot
How distant parts of the genome relate, drawn as a circle. The hero is a simulated tumor genome on the real GRCh38 scaffold (lengths, G-bands, Ensembl gene positions): the reader filters structural variants by type and switches the five track kinds (tile, histogram, scatter, line, heatmap) on and off. SVs are links between breakpoints, with intrachromosomal events spanning exactly their size; a planted 17p loss under TP53 points back to Five Views, alongside planted BCR::ABL1 and a MYC duplication. The bare ideogram opens a karyogram of any chromosome. The Philadelphia translocation is drawn on chromosomes 9 and 22 alone, its ribbon joining the two swapped segments, with Rowley 1973 and de Klein 1982 told accurately. Real human-mouse synteny, genome-wide (UCSC netMm39, the 160 top-level blocks of at least 3 Mb on human 1-22 and X, 82% of their length, on all 20 mouse chromosomes), is drawn as a circle and a linked dot plot, both read top to bottom so ribbons cross only when the two genomes disagree on order and twist when a block is inverted; the X stays whole, as Ohno predicted. A layout control shows how much of the tangle is layout: 7,529 crossings for the usual clockwise drawing, 3,605 at the exact minimum over every order and orientation of the mouse chromosomes (found by dynamic programming, since flips and order act on disjoint sets of pairs), only 177 of them within one mouse chromosome. It ends on what a circle is and is not good for, measured by that comparison.

### 4. ChIP-seq Peaks
Can mutant TP53 still bind its targets? Simulated wildtype, R175H and input reads on the real CDKN1A locus (Ensembl gene models, both p53 response elements and the intron-1 site), labeled simulated. A toy MACS-style caller (local Poisson rate from the input, exact tails, Benjamini-Hochberg) with a logarithmic threshold slider and a wildtype/R175H toggle; the caption reports what the slider actually does. Differential binding at ten canonical targets and three promoters bound by gain-of-function mutants, called per replicate with a slider for how much binding the mutant keeps, so "lost" can be seen to mean "below detection"; the essay answers "lost or moved" with Zhu et al. 2015. Then the real sequence: a sequence logo of JASPAR MA0106.3 (information content from raw counts, small-sample correction stated and not applied) above the six best motif windows, aligned to the 20-letter consensus and shaded by what each base costs under the matrix, linked to a motif scan of the real locus against dinucleotide shuffles and real ReMap p53 ChIP-seq. Both elements miss two consensus letters, but the 3' element's miss falls on a core G, so it ranks below matches that are almost never bound: a motif alone does not predict binding. Saramaki et al.'s -4500 region is checked against ReMap and the scan.

### 5. Differential Expression
One simulated RNA-seq experiment: TP53 knockout against wildtype cells, 2,000 genes. All 116 direct p53 targets of Fischer's census (2017, Table 1) are planted with falls. CDKN1A and nine others fall clearly, and the other 106 fall by only 0.2 to 0.6 log2 units. 10% of the remaining genes change at random. Each gene is tested with limma-trend's moderated t, ported and checked against limma, and corrected with Benjamini-Hochberg. The MA and volcano plots show the funnel of low-count noise, with CDKN1A ringed on both, picking up the thread from Five Views. The reader moves the fold-change and q thresholds and the replicate count. Because the simulation knows which genes truly changed, a threshold sweep grades every list: power and false discovery proportion against the fold-change cutoff, with and without q, at three and eight replicates. A third figure shows how Benjamini-Hochberg draws its line: the p-value histogram with Storey's pi0 floor, the ranked p-values against qk/m, and a strip of 50 reruns showing that the FDR bound holds on average, not in every run. A last figure runs preranked GSEA on the p53 set, checked against gseapy. It shows the running score against random sets, the leading edge and a member barcode, with a switch that drops the members already called. The rest of the set is still shifted beyond every random set, which is Mootha's case for gene-set analysis.

### 6. Clustered Heatmaps
Hierarchical clustering and the dendrogram, then the series' simulated cohort grouped by consensus clustering. The reader flips a shuffled, simulated gene-by-sample matrix (generic gene IDs, planted up, down and no-pattern genes marked in a side strip) to clustered order and sees the up and down blocks come back while the no-pattern genes scatter, and cuts the gene tree at different heights to see the groups change in both orders. Then the 100 shared cohort patients: CDKN1A alone lumps the three routes (TP53 mutation, MDM2 amplification, promoter methylation in TP53-wildtype tumors), and adding methylation separates them, with the partly methylated tumors left in between. A multi-omics heatmap stacks expression, real-CpG methylation, oncoprint alterations and copy number, with computed and planted subtype strips. The last figure runs consensus clustering at k = 2 to 6 on the cohort and on noise cohorts with the same feature covariance: PAC picks k = 4 on the cohort, but a cluster-free blob can also split cleanly in two, so the evidence for subtypes is the dip below the noise, not a low PAC alone. The partly methylated tumors join the intact cluster, an honest miss. It points forward to the survival essay, which splits these patients by subtype.

### 7. Dimensionality Reduction
PCA, exact t-SNE and UMAP computed in the page on 500 simulated immune cells. Counts are drawn from each cell's library size and normalized the way Seurat and Scanpy do. t-SNE uses scikit-learn's optimizer settings and UMAP follows umap-learn step by step, and the page's own exp and log keep seeded layouts identical in every browser. The reader switches between normalized and raw counts and sees the islands line up by sequencing depth when the normalization is left out. The signature figure hides two interlocking half-moons inside ten noise dimensions and scores each method on whether neighbors stay neighbors. PCA keeps the moons until its second component turns into noise, after which t-SNE on all dimensions wins and t-SNE on the top two PCs cannot. The reader recolors the map by marker gene or library size, enlarges an island, and draws each cell's true high-dimensional neighbors. Neighbor recall against neighborhood size, beside pairwise-distance density plots, shows t-SNE and UMAP winning locally and losing globally. The closing figure sets linked t-SNE and UMAP maps beside a bump chart of the distance ranks between cell types under each method and start. With random starts both lose the arrangement, from PCA or spectral starts both keep about a third of it, and neither comes near PCA: the start, not the algorithm, decides it.

### 8. Clinical Evidence
What the evidence says about patients, on the series' shared simulated cohort so that Part 1's routes and Part 6's clusters carry over. Ranked bars show best response under RECIST rules, sortable by response, CDKN1A or tumor mutational burden and colored by TP53 status or Part 6 cluster; response follows p53 activity, so the methylated tumors respond best and the MDM2 tumors as poorly as the TP53 mutants. One Kaplan-Meier curve carries an at-risk table and a Greenwood log-log band. The reader then splits survival by CDKN1A at a cut fixed in advance, by GAPDH as the null control, or by Part 6's four consensus clusters: the clusters fail a four-group test and fall just short as routes against intact, because clustering puts some routed tumors with the intact ones, and they add nothing to a model that already has CDKN1A. A cutpoint scan plots log-rank p across cut percentiles against 2,000 permuted scans, after Altman et al. 1994: the null gene crosses 0.05 at some cuts but not the permutation threshold, and CDKN1A survives correction. A random-effects meta-analysis of eight separately simulated studies with generic labels follows, as a forest plot with leave-one-out toggles and a funnel plot that follows them; the funnel's asymmetry comes from heterogeneity, not missing studies. It closes back on p21 with what survival splits and meta-analysis cannot show: cause.

## Shared foundations

Three shared modules sit under docs/bioinformatics/shared/, built before the essays and checked by a node test runner (scripts/bio-test.mjs, also tests/bioinformatics.html); each essay may add its own checked library beside them.

- A palette: one diverging pair, brick red for higher, gained or more and blue for lower, lost or less, one teal accent for whatever is in focus, a gray reference, and six categorical slots, with light and dark values, contrast and color-vision checks, and resolved hex for every d3 scale. A mutation is not a direction: mutant groups are drawn in the accent or a subtype color against a gray wildtype. The index explains this in one paragraph rather than a legend.
- One seeded simulated tumor cohort of 100 patients shared by articles 1, 6 and 8, with four routes to a silent CDKN1A (TP53 mutation, MDM2 amplification mutually exclusive with it, CDKN1A promoter methylation in TP53-wildtype tumors, and none), planted subtypes that consensus clustering can recover with one honest ambiguity (partly methylated tumors), survival that depends on CDKN1A with non-informative censoring, and GAPDH as a null gene.
- Real GRCh38 locus data fetched by a reproducible script: the 40 kb CDKN1A window (sequence, Ensembl gene models, UCSC CpG islands, phyloP, RepeatMasker), the two p53 response elements located in the reference sequence, the JASPAR p53 motif, and the TP53 codon-175 region. Articles 1, 2 and 4 all read it.

## What to get right

- Say what is real and what is simulated, figure by figure. Genome structure is real: chromosome lengths and G-bands from the UCSC hg38 cytoband table, Ensembl GRCh38 gene models, UCSC CpG islands and conservation at CDKN1A, real synteny blocks from the UCSC human-mouse alignment net, real TP53 mutations from the TCGA PanCancer Atlas through cBioPortal, ReMap p53 peaks and the JASPAR motif. Reads, expression, cells, cohorts and survival are simulated, and the caption says so.
- Use one genome assembly throughout. Batch-drafted coordinates tend to mix GRCh37 and GRCh38 (chromosome lengths, ABL1, BRCA1, PIK3CA, CDKN2A, the 9;22 breakpoints); check every coordinate against GRCh38. State which transcription start a promoter offset counts from: the literature's +1 for CDKN1A is 15 bp downstream of Ensembl's.
- Never attach invented numbers to real names. A simulated forest plot gets generic study labels, never real consortia with made-up years and effect sizes. Simulated genes use real HGNC symbols or generic IDs, never invented symbols. Never present random synteny as real evolution.
- Figures compute what they show. Statistical models (the moderated t, gene-set enrichment, the peak caller, t-SNE, UMAP, clustering, Cox and log-rank) live in checked libraries tested against R, scipy, scikit-learn or worked examples, and the prose numbers are filled from the page.
- Keep TP53 and p21 at the center of articles 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8: 2 and 4 sit on the real CDKN1A locus, 5 plants CDKN1A among the true changes, 6 and 8 share 1's cohort, and 8 tests 6's clusters. The Circos plot points back with 17p loss under TP53; dimensionality reduction uses generic simulated immune cells. The index walks the thread in a short closing paragraph.
- American spelling throughout.
