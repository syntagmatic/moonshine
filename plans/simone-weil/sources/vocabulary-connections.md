# Vocabulary Connections To Explore

These notes translate the vocabulary files into source questions for the quote packet and eventual interactives.

## 1. Necessity As Structure

Vocabulary note: Noether and Andre Weil treat structure as the invariant form beneath surface phenomena; Simone Weil treats necessity as the impersonal structure of embodied and social reality.

Source path:

- *Gravity and Grace* for `pesanteur`, necessity, and grace.
- *Oppression and Liberty* for social necessity: power, organization, and machinery.
- "The Iliad, or the Poem of Force" for force as necessity moving through war.

Interactive implication:

The gravity field should not look mystical. It should look structural: multiple pressures produce a field, and attention is pulled before the ego announces an intention.

## 2. Attention As Structural Insight

Vocabulary note: The shared move is "stop calculating, start seeing form." Noether's `Denken statt Rechnen`, Andre Weil's analogy, and Simone Weil's attention all suspend manipulation.

Source path:

- School-studies essay for attention as suspended thought.
- Bousquet letter for attention as generosity.
- Factory writings for the destruction of attention by speed and command.

Interactive implication:

Attention figures should distinguish three failures: grabbing, drifting, and imposing a reading. Patient attention should reveal relation.

## 3. The Impersonal And Axiomatic Purity

Vocabulary note: "Axiomatic purity" and "the impersonal" are not the same, but both ask the self to get out of the way of what is required by the object.

Source path:

- *Gravity and Grace* for detachment, void, decreation, and the impersonal.
- *Waiting for God* for attention and obedience.
- Secondary sources such as Sharon Cameron and Robert Chenavier for impersonality.

Interactive implication:

Decreation should be visualized as removing overlays, not erasing the person.

## 4. Metaxu, Analogy, And Hidden Bridges

Vocabulary note: Isomorphism, analogy, and metaxu all name bridge-thinking: recognizing relation across apparent separation.

Source path:

- *Gravity and Grace* notebook clusters on metaxu, beauty, and intermediaries.
- *The Need for Roots* for place, work, past, and community as worldly bridges rather than mere possessions.

Interactive implication:

The final synthesis diagram can use bridges rather than ladders: work, place, beauty, and obligation both separate from and connect to the good.

## 5. Rootedness / Void Polarity

Vocabulary note: the concept graph places rootedness and void as polar opposites. That tension is productive: rootedness is not clinging, and void is not uprootedness.

Source path:

- *The Need for Roots* for rootedness as social and historical participation.
- *Gravity and Grace* for void and detachment.
- Factory writings for the experience of uprooting inside labor.

Interactive implication:

Do not make the finale say "roots good, void bad." The series needs both: social rootedness against oppression, and spiritual void against egoic possession.

## 6. Affliction As Proof Of The Real

Vocabulary note: the structured vocabulary names affliction as a proof-like encounter with reality. That is dangerous if romanticized.

Source path:

- "The Love of God and Affliction" for `malheur`.
- Factory writings for humiliation, fatigue, speed, and obedience.
- "The Iliad, or the Poem of Force" for thingification.

Interactive implication:

The affliction figure should show the lock between body, status, and soul. It should not imply that suffering is automatically spiritually useful.

## 7. Method Versus Technique

Vocabulary note: method belongs with attention and exactness; technique becomes domination when severed from responsibility and feedback.

Source path:

- *Oppression and Liberty* for technocracy, machinery, and thought/action separation.
- Factory writings for mechanical compliance.
- *The Need for Roots* for work, responsibility, and truth.

Interactive implication:

The same tool should appear in multiple relational structures: tool-as-contact, method-as-feedback, system-as-command.

## 8. Honest Reception As Part Of The Vocabulary

Vocabulary note: the series cannot treat "the impersonal" or "the universal" as an escape from history. Weil's anti-Judaism and rejection of Jewish identity are part of the reception problem.

Source path:

- SEP and IEP biographies for balanced framing.
- Recent reception criticism for anti-hagiographic cautions.
- Final explainer source card, not a hidden appendix.

Interactive implication:

The final page should close with intellectual responsibility: posthumous editions, reception criticism, and the danger of sanctifying Weil's extremity.
