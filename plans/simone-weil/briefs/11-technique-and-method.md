# 11 - Technique and Method

## Pitch

This is the series' meta-explainer for Act II. After force (07), factory (08), bureaucracy (09), and war (10) have shown the monsters operating in specific domains, this page names the mechanism that all four share: the relation of the sign to the thing signified is being destroyed, and the game of exchanges between signs multiplies of itself and for itself.

Weil named the three forms explicitly: "Money, mechanization, algebra. The three monsters of contemporary civilization." Each is a mode in which signs lose contact with their referents and begin trading with each other. The factory page showed mechanization and money meeting in one body. The bureaucracy page showed the algebra of the social (reports and commands as sign-exchange without referent). The war page showed all three converging. This page names the abstract structure and offers the positive alternative: method as sign in contact with thing.

Weil's critique is not an attack on tools, science, or intelligence. She admired exactness and method. The geometry/algebra distinction is crucial here: geometry (in Weil's moral sense) keeps sign in contact with form — you can check the figure against the reality it describes. Algebra-as-monster trades symbols without checking their referents. Her target is abstraction uncoupled from reference, not abstraction as such. The distinction is functional, not categorical: the same procedure is method when it stays in contact with its referent and technique-as-domination when it does not.

The page sets up the final account of liberty. A person is freer when action is methodical: joined to perception, necessity, and judgment. A society is more oppressive when technique becomes opaque command.

## Source basis

- *Oppression and Liberty*, especially technocracy, social oppression, machines, and methodical thought.
- *The Need for Roots* for needs of the soul, labor, responsibility, and truth.
- Factory writings for the contrast between intelligent work and mechanical execution.

## Key terms

- technique: procedure or apparatus that can become domination when severed from attention.
- method: intelligent, exact contact with necessity; sign kept in contact with its referent.
- necessity: the real constraints good action must know.
- responsibility: answerability to reality and to persons affected by action.
- the three monsters: money, mechanization, algebra — three forms of sign severed from referent.
- geometry vs. algebra (moral sense): geometry retains contact between sign and form; algebra-as-monster trades signs without checking referents.

## Misreadings to avoid

- Do not make Weil anti-science.
- Do not make hand labor automatically pure or machine labor automatically corrupt.
- Do not treat method and technique as fixed dictionary opposites; the distinction is functional.
- Do not ignore her elitist or severe tendencies around intellectual discipline; explain without smoothing everything out.

## Bridge notes

This page should repair a likely reader simplification after the factory, bureaucracy, and war pages. Weil's target is not complexity as such. The question is whether a person or institution remains in accountable contact with necessity. That lets the final explainer define liberty positively, not just as escape from machines or command.

## Reader takeaway

Technique oppresses when it turns method into opaque command. Method liberates, at least partially, when thought and action remain joined in truthful contact with necessity.
