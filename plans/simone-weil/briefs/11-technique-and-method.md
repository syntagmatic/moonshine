# 11 - Technique and Method

## Pitch

Weil's critique of modern technique is not an attack on tools, science, or intelligence. She admired exactness and method. The problem begins when method loses contact with reality and becomes technique as domination: a procedure that can be expanded, administered, and obeyed without attention. This explainer should distinguish tool, method, and system.

The page sets up the final account of liberty. A person is freer when action is methodical in Weil's sense: joined to perception, necessity, and judgment. A society is more oppressive when technique becomes opaque command. The same machine, formula, or institution can either mediate contact with reality or block it, depending on who understands it, who controls it, and whether it remains answerable to human needs.

## Source basis

- *Oppression and Liberty*, especially technocracy, social oppression, machines, and methodical thought.
- *The Need for Roots* for needs of the soul, labor, responsibility, and truth.
- Factory writings for the contrast between intelligent work and mechanical execution.

## Figures

1. **Tool/method/system split** (interactive): the same lever, procedure, or plan is shown in three arrangements: used by a worker, understood as method, imposed as command.
2. **Opacity slider** (interactive): as understanding of a system decreases, dependence and obedience increase.
3. **Contact with necessity** (interactive): skilled action responds to material feedback; bureaucratic technique ignores feedback until failure.
4. **Science caution card** (static): exact thought is not the enemy; unanswerable technique is.

## Key terms

- technique: procedure or apparatus that can become domination when severed from attention.
- method: intelligent, exact contact with necessity.
- necessity: the real constraints good action must know.
- responsibility: answerability to reality and to persons affected by action.

## Misreadings to avoid

- Do not make Weil anti-science.
- Do not make hand labor automatically pure or machine labor automatically corrupt.
- Do not treat method and technique as fixed dictionary opposites; the distinction is functional.
- Do not ignore her elitist or severe tendencies around intellectual discipline; explain without smoothing everything out.

## Bridge notes

This page should repair a likely reader simplification after the factory, bureaucracy, and war pages. Weil's target is not complexity as such. The question is whether a person or institution remains in accountable contact with necessity. That lets the final explainer define liberty positively, not just as escape from machines or command.

## Reader takeaway

Technique oppresses when it turns method into opaque command. Method liberates, at least partially, when thought and action remain joined in truthful contact with necessity.
