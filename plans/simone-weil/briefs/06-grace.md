# 06 - Grace

## Pitch

Grace completes the first act, but it should not be introduced as a sentimental rescue. In Weil's vocabulary, grace is the non-mechanical counter-movement to gravity. Gravity describes the predictable pull of necessity, ego, prestige, and compensation. Grace is not manufactured by willpower; it is received through attention, waiting, decreation, and consent.

The explainer should be precise about what a diagram can and cannot show. It can visualize the structure: gravity pulls, the ego reacts, attention waits, the field changes. It cannot make grace into a mechanism. This limitation is itself pedagogically useful. The reader should feel the difference between "control panel for an effect" and "conditions under which reality can be received differently."

## Source basis

- *Gravity and Grace*, especially gravity/grace, void, decreation, necessity, and attention clusters.
- *Waiting for God* for attention, waiting, and religious context.
- Use a clear caveat that diagrams model relations among terms, not a supernatural mechanism.

## Figures

1. **Gravity/grace balance** (interactive): gravity forces are adjustable, but grace is not a slider. The figure shows preparatory conditions that alter receptivity rather than producing an effect on command.
2. **Waiting timeline** (interactive): reaction happens quickly; attention introduces duration; grace appears as a discontinuity, not a predictable output.
3. **Consent distinction** (interactive): consent to necessity is contrasted with submission to domination. The same word is placed in different relational structures.
4. **Act I synthesis map** (static): gravity, attention, affliction, decreation, contradiction, and grace connected with arrows and cautions.

## Key terms

- grace: counter-movement to gravity, not self-generated force.
- consent: truthful relation to necessity, not acceptance of injustice.
- waiting: attention extended through time.
- void: the space in which compensating fantasy is not immediately installed.

## Misreadings to avoid

- Do not make grace a controllable psychological technique.
- Do not make it vague consolation after affliction.
- Do not make consent sound politically submissive.
- Do not erase the Christian and mystical context, but keep the page intelligible for general readers.

## Bridge notes

This is the Act I capstone, so the synthesis figure should be reusable as an index thumbnail or act opener later. It should also prepare for Act II by clarifying that grace does not cancel social analysis. The next pages return to factories, war, and bureaucracy with a sharper sense of how force narrows perception.

## Reader takeaway

Grace is Weil's name for a transformation that cannot be forced. The human work is not to manufacture it, but to stop filling every wound and limit with egoic reaction.
