# 01 - Gravity

## Pitch

Open the series with Weil's most portable metaphor: gravity. In the notebooks later arranged as *Gravity and Grace*, gravity is not merely a theological image or a synonym for sin. It names the downward pull of necessity: bodies get tired, hunger narrows perception, prestige bends judgment, institutions reward obedience, and the self tries to restore its importance whenever reality wounds it. The explainer should make this pressure visible before naming it mystical.

The reader should leave with a concrete distinction: gravity is the order of compulsion, reaction, and weight; grace is not yet explained, but it will have to be something other than a stronger act of will. The page should also establish the series bridge: the same structural pull Weil diagnoses in factories, war, and bureaucracy will later reappear as `pesanteur` in the notebooks.

## Source basis

- *Gravity and Grace*, especially notebook clusters on gravity, necessity, void, imagination, and the social imprint.
- *Oppression and Liberty* as background for social necessity: power, prestige, and organization move through people.
- Use edition caveat: *Gravity and Grace* is a posthumous topical selection, not a systematic book Weil arranged.

## Figures

1. **Gravity field** (interactive): points labeled hunger, fatigue, prestige, fear, resentment, and social approval bend an "attention" particle toward themselves. Sliders vary each mass.
2. **Reaction loop** (interactive): insult -> wound -> compensating fantasy -> action -> new wound. The reader can interrupt the loop only by changing the relation to attention, not by adding more force.
3. **Social gravity map** (static/interactive hybrid): factory, army, bureaucracy, and public opinion shown as different fields with the same structure: a center of pressure and a narrowing of possible perception.
4. **Term card** (static): `pesanteur`, necessity, force, and gravity placed side by side with short distinctions.

## Key terms

- `pesanteur`: gravity or weight.
- `necessite`: necessity, the order of constraint.
- `force`: social and bodily pressure that acts through people.
- `imagination`: the ego's compensating picture of reality.

## Misreadings to avoid

- Do not make gravity a vague mood of sadness or a decorative spiritual darkness.
- Do not oppose gravity to "positive thinking" or moral effort.
- Do not imply that necessity is simply evil. For Weil, necessity is also part of reality's order.
- Do not make the political writings look secondary. This page should already hint that social force is one form of gravity.

## Bridge notes

This opener should establish the visual grammar for the whole series. The gravity field can recur later in social forms: factory rhythm, bureaucracy, war, and prestige. Keep the tone exact rather than atmospheric, because the reader needs to trust that gravity is a working concept, not decoration.

## Reader takeaway

Gravity is Weil's name for the way necessity pulls perception, desire, and action downward into reaction. It is physical, social, psychological, and spiritual at once.
