# 11 — Ideals as the New Numbers

## Pitch

In the ring `ℤ[√−5]`, the number `6` has two different factorizations into irreducibles: `2 · 3` and `(1 + √−5)(1 − √−5)`. Unique factorization fails, and with it a century of number theory begins to wobble. Kummer's idea, sharpened by Dedekind and then made abstract by Noether, was to replace numbers by *ideals* and recover unique factorization at the level of ideals. This explainer makes the failure tactile, introduces the ideal as the object that fixes it, and opens Act III with the same move Noether made: *don't try harder with numbers — change what counts as a number*.

## Key formulas / constructions

- Ring: `ℤ[√−5] = {a + b √−5 : a, b ∈ ℤ}`, norm `N(a + b √−5) = a² + 5 b²`.
- Non-unique factorization: `6 = 2 · 3 = (1 + √−5)(1 − √−5)`, with every factor irreducible (check by norms: 4, 9, 6, 6).
- Ideal generated by elements: `(a₁, …, a_n) = {r₁ a₁ + … + r_n a_n : r_i ∈ R}`.
- Product of ideals: `I · J = {Σ_k i_k j_k : i_k ∈ I, j_k ∈ J}`, a finite sum.
- Key fact: in a Dedekind domain, every nonzero ideal factors uniquely as a product of prime ideals.
- Class group: `Cl(R) = (nonzero fractional ideals) / (principal fractional ideals)`. For `ℤ[√−5]`, `Cl = ℤ/2ℤ`.
- Specific factorization: `(6) = (2, 1 + √−5)² (3, 1 + √−5)(3, 1 − √−5)`.

## Reader takeaway

The reader has seen unique factorization fail in `ℤ[√−5]` and get rescued by ideals. They understand that an ideal is a subset closed under multiplication by the whole ring, that ideals can be multiplied, and that the "right" primes in a Dedekind domain are prime *ideals*. They are primed to accept that Noether's 1921 generalization will take these ideal operations to any ring with the right finiteness condition — the Noetherian chain condition of the next explainer.
