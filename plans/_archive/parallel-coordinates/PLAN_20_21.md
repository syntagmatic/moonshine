## 20-fourfold-h4.html — "The Fourfold Fold"

### Thesis

Where `/16` projects E₈ into the Coxeter plane and recovers **two** concentric 600-cells (inner H₄ and outer φH₄), a different 8×8 rotation projects into **four** chiral 600-cells: `H₄L + φH₄L + H₄R + φH₄R`. The new matrix is volume-preserving (det = 1), traceless, symmetric, and palindromic — and its eigenstructure splits ℝ⁸ into two quaternion-like 4-planes with intrinsic left/right handedness.

The punchline is the emergence of a distinguished **edge group**: exactly 192 edges of norm 2φ connect snub 24-cells across the L↔R divide, and 40 tesseracts emerge as the internal scaffolding.

### Mathematical data

Core quantities to precompute (offline, dump as constants):

- The 8×8 rotation matrix `U` with palindromic coefficients. Wilson (or Dechant + co.) gives explicit entries; otherwise derive numerically from two orthogonal H₄ planes whose Coxeter elements give eigenvalues `e^{±2πi/30}` and `e^{±14πi/30}`.
- Four 120-vertex sets: `H₄L` (at radius 1), `φH₄L` (radius φ), `H₄R`, `φH₄R` — with `{L, R}` the chirality label.
- 6720 Gosset edges, classified by (norm, shell-pair). Expected strata:
  - 1920 edges at norm 1 within each scale-pair (inner-inner or outer-outer, same chirality)
  - 1920 edges at norm φ between chirality-matched shells
  - 2880 edges at norm 2φ — the **bridge group** — connecting L↔R across the handedness divide
- 40 tesseracts: vertices where `L = R` or `L = −R` parity classes match the permutations of (±1, ±1, ±1, ±1).

### Scene list

1. **Hero**: Coxeter mandala colored by (L/R × inner/outer) in four colors, with the 192 bridge edges highlighted. Carries the page.
2. **Projection demo**: 4-panel small multiples of `H₄L`, `φH₄L`, `H₄R`, `φH₄R` as individual 600-cells (stereographic from 4D), with a master rotation slider.
3. **Edge histogram**: bar chart of edge norms, with the 2φ bar drawn distinctly.
4. **Bridge hunter**: click any root; see its 56 edge-neighbors split by which of the four shells the other end lives in. Highlights the L↔R bridge pairs.
5. **Parallel-coords panel**: straight polylines for L = R parity (40 tesseracts) vs. crossing polylines for the mixed classes. Classic `/16`-style PC but with chirality color coding.
6. **Recovery**: slider from fourfold (spread) → bipartite (H₄ ⊕ φH₄ from `/16`) → Coxeter mandala. Shows how `/20` specializes back to `/16` when you degrade U.

### Interaction patterns to borrow from /16

- Scrubber for scene transitions
- Canvas for dense edge rendering, SVG for PC axes
- ResizeObserver on the container
- Coset colors: use a 4-color palette (L-inner, L-outer, R-inner, R-outer); avoid reusing /16's 10-color palette so the figures are visually distinct

### What's hard

- Finding the matrix `U` explicitly. The atlas/10b Clifford-induction machinery plus the E8 library gets us most of the way, but the specific volume-preserving rotation matrix with palindromic polynomial may need a literature dig (try Dechant's "A new construction of E₈" or Wilson 2009).
- Edge classification at norm 2φ. Precompute in Node and dump as a const array (like `/16`'s coset partition).

### Budget

2000-2500 LOC, comparable to `/16`. Mostly reuses math-engine patterns from `/16`.

## 21-cut-and-project.html — "Cut and Project"

### Thesis

E₈'s root lattice, cut along a specific 4-dimensional subspace and projected orthogonally into the complementary 4-plane, produces the **Elser–Sloane quasicrystal** (ESQC). Project the ESQC into 3D via the icosian-compatible angle `cos θ_A = 1/(φ+2)` and you get the **Fibonacci icosagrid** — a physical 3D quasicrystal with icosahedral point-group symmetry. The atomic nucleus of this 3D structure is the **20-Group** or **Compound of Five Cuboctahedra (C5C)**.

The explainer walks the chain E₈ → ESQC → Fibonacci icosagrid step by step, with a movable cut window so the user watches periodic-in-8D become aperiodic-in-3D.

### Mathematical data

- E₈ root lattice shells, truncated to some reasonable point count (say 2000-5000 roots)
- 4-dimensional cut window: an axis-aligned "slab" in the 4D complement. Width controls the density of the resulting quasicrystal.
- Angle `θ_A = arccos(1/(φ+2)) ≈ 57.9°` for the 4D → 3D projection
- Icosahedral rotation group action: use binary icosahedral 2I to place 5-fold rotated copies of a cuboctahedron core

### Scene list

1. **Hero**: a tilting cut-window over the 8D Coxeter mandala, with an adjacent 3D panel updating live to show the Fibonacci icosagrid grow/shrink as the cut shifts.
2. **Cut-and-project primer**: start with a 2D analog — project ℤ² to a line at an irrational slope, get a Fibonacci sequence. Build intuition before going to 8D.
3. **E₈ → ESQC step**: E₈'s 240 roots, projected by the 4×8 matrix, with a radius histogram showing the aperiodic shell structure emerging.
4. **ESQC → icosagrid step**: the 4D quasicrystal, sliced at the magic angle, collapsing to 3D points.
5. **Compound of Five Cuboctahedra**: a rotatable 3D polytope built from 5 cuboctahedra interlocking by icosahedral symmetry — shown as the "atomic nucleus" of the 3D quasicrystal.
6. **Quasiperiodic verification**: diffraction-style figure (Fourier transform of the point cloud) showing the 10-fold / icosahedral symmetry peaks characteristic of quasicrystal order.
7. **Physical hook**: short section linking to real quasicrystal materials (e.g., Al-Mn alloys Shechtman 1984, Penrose tilings).

### What's hard

- Stable 3D projection with a movable cut that doesn't flicker. Use smooth interpolation of the window position; precompute a dense sample set and reveal/hide based on cut position.
- C5C geometry. 5 cuboctahedra, each at a specific 2I rotation, sharing a common center. 8 × 5 = 40 vertices, but some coincide — net count ≈ 30 points. Build with quaternion rotations of a base cuboctahedron.

### Budget

2500-3000 LOC. Slightly larger than `/20` because of the 3D rendering (may want light three.js or hand-rolled Canvas + perspective).

## Sequencing recommendation

Build **`/20` first**. Reasons:
- Math is more self-contained (no quasicrystal diffraction machinery)
- Narrative continuity with `/16` is cleaner
- Forces us to derive the volume-preserving 8×8 rotation matrix, which is a reusable artifact for `/21` too

Then **`/21`**, which uses `/20`'s fourfold H₄ as the "before" state that gets cut-and-projected down.

Both explainers slot directly after `/16-e8-folds-to-600-cells.html` in the parallel-coordinates series. Current PC series ends at `23-connections.html`, so these land at `/20` and `/21` positions — or as `16b` and `16c` letter-label style, matching the atlas convention.
