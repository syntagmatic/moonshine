# 07 — Subsystems as Peeling

## Pitch

Delete a single node from the E8 Dynkin diagram, and the remaining 7 nodes generate a subsystem — a smaller root system sitting inside E8. The possibilities depend on which node you delete: removing α₈ gives E_7, removing α₁ gives D_7, removing the branch node gives A_7 × A_1 (or similar, depending on labeling), and so on. The explainer makes this concrete by letting the reader click nodes in the diagram and watch the subsystem light up inside the full 240. The payoff: E8 contains every exceptional root system as a nested subsystem — E_8 ⊃ E_7 ⊃ E_6 ⊃ D_5 ⊃ A_4 — and this nested "peeling" is the honest geometric version of the branching rules readers may have heard about.

## Key formulas / constructions

- Subsystem construction: removing αᵢ gives the root system generated by {αⱼ : j ≠ i} under reflections.
- Root counts for rank-7 subsystems of E_8: E_7 has 126, D_7 has 84, A_7 has 56, E_6 × A_1 has 72 + 2 = 74, A_6 × A_1 has 42 + 2 = 44, D_6 × A_1 has 60 + 2 = 62, etc.
- E_n family inclusions: E_8 ⊃ E_7 ⊃ E_6 ⊃ E_5 = D_5 ⊃ E_4 = A_4.
- "Exceptional ends at E_8": E_9 is not finite, E_10 is hyperbolic, E_11 is Lorentzian.

## Reader takeaway

The reader can name every rank-7 subsystem of E8 and point to which Dynkin deletion yields it. They understand the exceptional chain E_8 ⊃ E_7 ⊃ E_6 ⊃ D_5 as a concrete peeling process, not an abstract inclusion. They are prepared to hear the "branching rule" language in physics or rep-theory contexts and know what those rules are actually tracking geometrically.
