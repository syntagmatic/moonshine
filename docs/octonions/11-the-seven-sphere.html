The Seven-Sphere

Part 11 of 15. Most spheres are just shapes. But four spheres are special: they are the only ones where you can multiply points to stay on the surface.

In topology, a sphere ($S^n$) is just a set of points at a fixed distance from zero. But the unit elements of our four division algebras inherit a special algebraic power. Because they are normed algebras ($|ab|=|a||b|$), the product of two unit vectors is always another unit vector.

This means that $S^0, S^1, S^3,$ and $S^7$ aren't just shapes—they are **manifolds with multiplication**. They are the only spheres that are "parallelizable," meaning you can pick a consistent direction to "fly" in at every point without ever hitting a pole.

Mission: The S⁷ Navigator

You are a pilot on the 7-Sphere. Your current "heading" is an 8D vector of length 1. Use the multiplication buttons to shift your heading. Each button multiplies your current state by an imaginary unit. Your goal is to reach the Target Coordinate.

TARGET: [0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0] (e4)
CURRENT: [1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]

Reach the target in exactly 3 steps!

Figure 1. Navigating the 7-Sphere. Every multiplication moves you along a great circle of the sphere. On $S^7$, you can never get stuck—there is always a clear direction to move.

The Final Cliff: This is the end of the line for multiplicative spheres. Because Hurwitz's Theorem stops at 8D, there is no $S^{15}$ or $S^{31}$ that allows for this kind of smooth navigation. $S^7$ is the largest sphere of its kind in the multiverse.
