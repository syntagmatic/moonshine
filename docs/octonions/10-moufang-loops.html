Moufang Loops

Part 10 of 15. Without associativity, $(ab)c \neq a(bc)$. But the Octonions aren't lawless. They follow a deeper kind of order called a Moufang Loop.

When you lose associativity, you lose the ability to move parenthesis. But Octonions aren't "random." They obey specific survivor laws that keep the geometry coherent. The set of Octonions is part of a structure called a **Moufang Loop**.

The Moufang Identity

The most famous of these laws is the Moufang Identity. It says that if you "wrap" a product with the same element on both sides, the parenthesis reconcile. Your goal is to verify this law using random Octonions.

The Identity: $(a(bc))a = (ab)(ca)$

Notice how the element $a$ appears on both ends. This symmetry forces the non-associativity to cancel out.

The Rule of Two: Diassociativity

There is one more law that makes Octonions usable: **Diassociativity**. This law states that any two Octonions generate a standard, associative system (like the Quaternions). You only run into trouble when you add a third vector that isn't in their plane.

In other words: as long as you only talk about two things at a time, you can move parenthesis however you like. The non-associative "cliff" only appears in the interaction of three or more independent directions.
