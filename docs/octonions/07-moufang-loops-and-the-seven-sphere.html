Moufang Loops and the Seven-Sphere

Part 7 of 8. Without associativity, the octonions still obey Moufang identities — a deeper algebraic law. And the unit octonions form S⁷, one of only four spheres where you can multiply points.

When you lose associativity, you lose the ability to move parentheses freely. But the octonions are not lawless. They obey specific survivor laws that keep their geometry coherent. The most important of these are the Moufang identities, and the algebraic structure they define is called a Moufang loop.

The Moufang Identities

There are four classic forms of the Moufang identity. Each says that if you "wrap" a product with the same element in a particular pattern, the non-associativity cancels out. These identities are weaker than full associativity but strong enough to do real algebra.

The four Moufang laws:

1.  $a(b(ac)) = ((ab)a)c$  —  left Moufang

2.  $((ca)b)a = c(a(ba))$  —  right Moufang

3.  $(ab)(ca) = a(bc)a$  —  middle Moufang

4.  $(ab)(ca) = (a(bc))a$  —  flexible variant

The element $a$ always appears on both sides, forcing the non-associativity to cancel.

Diassociativity: The Rule of Two

There is one more law that makes octonions usable: diassociativity. Any two octonions generate a standard, associative sub-algebra (isomorphic to the reals, complexes, or quaternions). The non-associative "cliff" only appears when a third independent direction enters the picture.

As long as you only work with two octonions at a time, you can move parentheses however you like. Non-associativity is a three-body problem.

The Parallelizable Spheres

In topology, a sphere $S^n$ is the set of points at unit distance from the origin. But the unit elements of our four division algebras inherit something extra: because each is a normed algebra ($|ab|=|a||b|$), the product of two unit vectors is always another unit vector. This means $S^0, S^1, S^3,$ and $S^7$ are not just shapes — they are manifolds with multiplication.

These four spheres are also the only ones that are parallelizable: at every point, you can define a full frame of independent tangent vectors that varies smoothly over the entire sphere — no singularities, no "bald spots." This is a direct consequence of the division-algebra structure.

What We Take With Us

  • The Moufang identities (e.g. $a(b(ac)) = ((ab)a)c$) hold for all octonions. They are weaker than associativity but strong enough to do algebra.
  • A Moufang loop is a "non-associative group." The unit octonions form one under multiplication.
  • The Moufang identities fail in 16 dimensions (sedenions), just as the norm-multiplicativity property does.
  • $S^0, S^1, S^3, S^7$ are the only parallelizable spheres — each is the unit sphere of a normed division algebra.
  • $S^7$ carries a smooth multiplication, making it the largest sphere with a group-like structure.
