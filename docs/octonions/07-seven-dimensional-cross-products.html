7-Dimensional Cross Products

Part 7 of 15. The cross product is a geometric miracle. It works in 3D, fails in almost every other dimension, but makes a final, grand appearance in 7D.

In your first physics class, you learned that $x \times y$ gives you a vector perpendicular to both $x$ and $y$. This property is so useful that we assume it's a universal law. But if you try to define a cross product in 2D or 4D, the math breaks. It turns out that a true, bilinear cross product can only exist in dimensions **3** and **7**.

Why these two? Because those are the dimensions of the "purely imaginary" parts of the Quaternions ($4-1=3$) and the Octonions ($8-1=7$).

The Geometric Rule: $x \times y = \text{Im}(xy)$. In the Octonions, if you multiply two imaginary vectors, the result's imaginary part is exactly the 7D cross product. It obeys the Fano Plane rules you learned in Part 4.

This 7D cross product is essential for modern physics. It is the language of the **G2 symmetry group**, which we explore in Part 8. Without this 7D geometry, the exceptional structures of string theory could not exist.
