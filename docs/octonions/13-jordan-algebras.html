Jordan Algebras

Part 13 of 15. Standard matrix math is usually associative. But quantum mechanics needed a rule that allowed for non-associativity. Pascual Jordan found it.

In quantum physics, physical quantities are represented by matrices. For a matrix to represent a real quantity, it must be **Hermitian** (it equals its own conjugate transpose). But if you multiply two Hermitian matrices $A$ and $B$, the result $AB$ is usually not Hermitian.

Pascual Jordan invented a new multiplication rule—the **Jordan Product**—that fixes this problem by averaging the two possible orders. This makes multiplication commutative but non-associative.

The Jordan Product: $x \circ y = \frac{1}{2}(xy + yx)$

This rule works for any system, but it creates a unique "Exceptional" algebra when applied to $3 \times 3$ Octonionic matrices: the **Albert Algebra** ($M_3(O)$).
