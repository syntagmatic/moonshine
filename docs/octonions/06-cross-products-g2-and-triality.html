Cross Products, G₂, and Triality

Part 6 of 8. The octonions give 7D the only cross product outside 3D. Their symmetry group is the exceptional G₂. And in 8 dimensions, vectors and spinors become interchangeable through triality.

In your first physics class, you learned that $x \times y$ gives you a vector perpendicular to both $x$ and $y$. This property is so useful that we assume it is a universal law. But if you try to define a cross product in 2D or 4D, the math breaks. It turns out that a true, bilinear cross product can only exist in dimensions 3 and 7.

Why these two? Because those are the dimensions of the "purely imaginary" parts of the quaternions ($4-1=3$) and the octonions ($8-1=7$). The formula is simple: $x \times y = \text{Im}(xy)$. The Fano plane you met in Part 4 is nothing other than the multiplication table for this 7D cross product.

The G₂ Symmetry Group

A symmetry of an algebra is a rotation that respects its "logic." If you rotate the space so that $x$ becomes $x'$ and $y$ becomes $y'$, the symmetry requires that their product also rotates correctly: $(xy)' = x'y'$.

For the complex numbers, the symmetry is simple. For the quaternions, it is the group of all 3D rotations ($SO(3)$). But for the octonions, the symmetry is something special: G₂, the smallest of the exceptional Lie groups.

The Exceptional Group: G₂ is called "exceptional" because it does not fit into the standard families of rotations. It exists only because of the unique non-associative geometry of 8D space. It is 14-dimensional, meaning there are 14 independent ways to "twist" octonion space without breaking the math.

Triality and Spinors

In 3D physics, vectors and spinors are fundamentally different. If you rotate a vector 360 degrees, it returns to normal. But a spinor (like an electron) must be rotated 720 degrees to return to its original state. They inhabit different worlds.

But 8 dimensions is a geometric miracle. Here, there is a perfect three-way symmetry between vectors ($V$), left-handed spinors ($S_L$), and right-handed spinors ($S_R$). This is Triality, and it is the reason octonions are the bridge to superstring theory.

What we take with us

  • The cross product exists only in 3D and 7D, because Im(H) and Im(O) are the only imaginary spaces of normed division algebras.
  • $x \times y = \text{Im}(xy)$. The Fano plane IS the multiplication table for the 7D cross product.
  • G₂ is the automorphism group of the octonions: the 14-dimensional group of rotations preserving the multiplication table (= the Fano plane lines).
  • Triality: in 8D, vectors ($V_8$), left-spinors ($S_+$), right-spinors ($S_-$) are interchangeable. This three-way symmetry exists only in dimension 8 and is why octonions connect to string theory.
