The G2 Symmetry Group

Part 8 of 15. Every algebra has a group of rotations that keep its multiplication table intact. For the Octonions, this group is the 14-dimensional G2.

A symmetry of an algebra is a rotation that respects its "logic." If you rotate the space so that $x$ becomes $x'$ and $y$ becomes $y'$, the symmetry requires that their product also rotates correctly: $(xy)' = x'y'$.

For the Complex numbers, the symmetry is simple. For the Quaternions, it is the group of all 3D rotations ($SO(3)$). But for the Octonions, the symmetry is something special: **G2**, the smallest of the exceptional Lie groups.

Mission: The Fano Lock

The Fano Plane encodes the Octonion multiplication. A symmetry of the Octonions is any permutation of the units $\{e_1, \dots, e_7\}$ that preserves the "lines" of the Fano Plane. Most permutations break the lines. Your goal is to find one that doesn't.

Broken Lines: 0 / 7

If all 7 lines are preserved, you've found a G2 symmetry!

Figure 1. The G2 Symmetry Lock. Most movements of the nodes will break the triangle/circle structure of the Fano Plane. The group of all "legal" movements is G2.

The Exceptional Group: G2 is called "exceptional" because it doesn't fit into the standard families of rotations. It exists only because of the unique non-associative geometry of 8D space. It is 14-dimensional, meaning there are 14 independent ways to "twist" Octonion space without breaking the math.

G2 is the first of five exceptional groups ($G_2, F_4, E_6, E_7, E_8$). These are the "mountaintops" of mathematical symmetry. In Part 9, we see how G2 allows for a miraculous three-way symmetry called **Triality**.
