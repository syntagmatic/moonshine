Hamilton’s Bridge

Part 2 of 8. In 1843, William Rowan Hamilton realized that to multiply in 3D, he needed a 4th dimension—and he had to lose commutativity.

For years, Hamilton was obsessed with a specific problem: finding a 3D version of the complex numbers. He wanted a way to multiply triplets $(a, b, c)$ that would represent rotations in 3D space. But the math refused to cooperate. Every rule he tried broke the law $|ab| = |a||b|$.

The solution came to him in a flash while walking across Brougham Bridge. He needed three imaginary units ($i, j, k$) and one real unit. He needed the Quaternions.

The Bridge Formula: $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$

This single line implies that $ij = k$, but $ji = -k$. For the first time in history, mathematicians accepted a number system where order matters. Multiplication was no longer commutative.
