The Fano Plane

Part 4 of 15. The Octonions have 7 imaginary units. To multiply them, we don't use a table; we use a geometric circuit called the Fano Plane.

In Part 3, we reached the 8th dimension using the Cayley-Dickson recipe. We now have a real part ($e_0$) and seven imaginary parts ($e_1$ through $e_7$). Multiplying these units is the key to 8D geometry, but with 64 possible combinations, we need a better tool than a textbook table.

Enter the Fano Plane. It is the smallest possible projective plane, consisting of 7 points and 7 lines. In the world of Octonions, it is the master circuit board for all algebraic interactions.

How to read the Plane: Every line in the diagram has three points and an arrow.
1. If you multiply two units in the direction of the arrow, the result is the third unit: $e_1 e_2 = e_3$.
2. If you go against the arrow, the result is negative: $e_2 e_1 = -e_3$.
3. Every unit squares to -1: $e_i e_i = -e_0$.

Mission: Fano Pathfinding

The Fano Plane isn't just a mnemonic; it's a profound geometric statement. It shows that the 7 imaginary directions of the Octonions are perfectly symmetric. No direction is "better" than the others.
