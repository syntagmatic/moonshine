Vertex Operators and the Moonshine Module

Part 13 of 14. The Conway–Norton conjecture asks for a graded Monster module whose graded dimension is J = j − 744 and whose graded traces are Hauptmoduls. This part states that specification, introduces the vertex operator algebra, and builds V♮ from the Leech lattice by a ℤ/2 orbifold, checking its graded dimension against J.

In 1988, mathematicians Frenkel, Lepowsky, and Meurman finally built the "Moonshine Module" (V♮) that Conway and Norton had predicted. To do this, they used a relatively new framework from string theory called a vertex operator algebra (VOA). This allowed them to create a mathematical object where the "states" (or dimensions) interact with each other in extremely rigid ways.

Before the construction, the target. Conway and Norton turned McKay's observation into a precise list of properties the module would need, and that list is what FLM had to meet.

Notation. In Act IV, j(τ) = q−1 + 744 + … is the classical modular invariant, while J(τ) = j(τ) − 744 = q−1 + 0 + 196884q + … is the moonshine-normalised graded dimension. The Moonshine Module is built so that its graded dimension is J, not the unshifted j.

The specification

An infinite-dimensional ℤ-graded vector space is a vector space V that decomposes as a direct sum of finite-dimensional pieces indexed by integers:

Each Vn is a homogeneous component, or grade. The space is infinite-dimensional overall, but every grade is finite-dimensional, so linear algebra can be done one grade at a time. The graded dimension of V packages all of the dimensions into one power series:

The Conway–Norton conjecture (1979) says there is a graded Monster module V♮ = ⊕n ≥ −1 Vn♮ with graded dimension J, and that for every g ∈ 𝕄 the graded trace Tg is the Hauptmodul of a genus-zero group. Three grades carry most of the structure:

Grade −1: dim 1. A copy of ℂ on which the Monster acts trivially. It produces the q−1 term.

Grade 0: dim 0. In the vertex-algebra construction below, this grade would form a Lie algebra, and exponentiating it would give V♮ a continuous group of symmetries. An empty grade 0 leaves room for a finite automorphism group. This is why the graded dimension is J with constant term 0, and the construction will get it from the fact that the Leech lattice has no vectors of norm 2.

Grade 1: dim 196 884. This grade carries a commutative non-associative product, the one Griess used in 1982, and splits as 1 ⊕ 196 883 under the Monster. That splitting is McKay's observation.

The dimensions alone leave the Monster action underdetermined. The traces pin it down. A Hauptmodul for a group Γ commensurable with SL2(ℤ) is a single modular function that generates the whole field of Γ-invariant functions, and one exists exactly when the compactified quotient of ℍ by Γ has genus zero. j is the Hauptmodul for SL2(ℤ). Conway and Norton proposed a specific Hauptmodul for each of the Monster's 194 conjugacy classes. Algebraically conjugate classes, such as 23A and 23B, share a series, so these amount to 171 distinct functions.

States that talk to each other

A graded vector space is inert. A VOA gives each state v ∈ V an action on every other via a formal power series of linear maps indexed by a complex variable z, the vertex operator:

Each v(n) is a linear endomorphism of V; Y(v, z) is a field in the physicist's sense, an operator-valued distribution on the punctured complex line. z is the worldsheet coordinate of a string.

The four axioms

A vertex operator algebra is a graded vector space V = ⊕n≥0 Vn, graded by weight, equipped with a vertex operator map Y, a vacuum vector |0⟩ ∈ V0, and a conformal vector ω ∈ V2, satisfying four axioms.

1. Vacuum. Y(|0⟩, z) = idV, and Y(v, z)|0⟩ → v as z → 0. Creating v at the origin and collapsing the insertion is just v.

2. Translation. An operator T with [T, Y(v, z)] = ∂z Y(v, z) and T|0⟩ = 0 — translation covariance on the worldsheet.

The weight grading and the J-grading used above differ by one: the graded dimension is Σ dim Vk qk−1, so the vacuum (weight 0) gives q−1, the empty grade 0 is the weight-1 space, and the Griess algebra is the weight-2 space.

Why VOAs matter for moonshine. Virasoro fixes the grading, locality controls singularities, the vacuum pins the identity. Together they make V♮ so rigid that an automorphism is fixed by what it does on the weight-2 space, which is how FLM pin Aut(V♮) down to the Monster.

The free boson: a toy example

The simplest VOA is the free boson (Heisenberg VOA) of central charge c = 1: a polynomial algebra in α−1, α−2, … with α−n of degree n. The graded dimensions are partition numbers:

From free bosons to lattice VOAs

d independent free bosons give central charge d. Compactifying on a lattice Λ ⊂ ℝd gives the lattice VOA VΛ, with central charge d and graded dimension the theta series divided by ηd. Physically this is a bosonic string on ℝd/Λ.

V1 has dimension 24, one boson per lattice direction, generating an abelian Lie algebra of continuous symmetries. Conway–Norton demands no continuous symmetries, so VΛ₂₄ ≠ V♮. The fix is an orbifold.

VOAs as mathematical string theory. A VOA axiomatises the chiral half of 2D CFT: vertex operators are OPE, Virasoro is conformal symmetry, central charge counts target dimensions. c = 24 is the number of transverse directions of the bosonic string in its critical dimension 26. Borcherds imported the no-ghost theorem as a pure-algebra lemma.

Constructing V♮: the FLM orbifold

FLM's 1988 construction of V♮ proceeds in three steps:

Step 1: The Leech lattice VOA

Start with VΛ24 as above. The problem is immediate: dim V1 = 24, generating continuous symmetries that Conway–Norton forbids.

Step 2: The ℤ2 orbifold

The Leech lattice has a natural involution θ: x ↦ −x. This lifts to an automorphism of VΛ24 that acts as −1 on each oscillator αi−n (so as +1 on states with an even number of oscillators and −1 on odd) and sends each momentum state eα to ±e−α. Taking the invariant subspace

kills every weight-1 state (since all 24 oscillators are odd under θ), but it also kills too many states at higher grades. The graded dimension of VΛ24+ alone does not match j(τ) − 744. Something is missing.

Step 3: The twisted sector

In orbifold conformal field theory, quotienting by a symmetry creates a twisted sector — a new Hilbert space of strings that close only up to the action of the orbifold group element. For the θ-orbifold, the twisted sector VΛ24T consists of strings on the 24-torus whose boundary conditions are anti-periodic. The orbifold recipe. Start with VΛ24. Quotient by θ: x ↦ −x. Keep the invariants (kills weight-1), add the θ-invariant part of the twisted sector (restores the missing states). The result V♮ = VΛ24+ ⊕ VΛ24T,+ is a VOA of central charge 24 with the correct graded dimension.

The graded dimension is j(τ) − 744

Each piece of the recipe has a graded dimension that can be written down and expanded. The Leech lattice VOA gives ΘΛ/η24, and since ΘΛ = E43 − 720Δ this is j − 720. Averaging with the trace of θ keeps the invariant half. The twisted sector starts at weight 3/2 with 212 ground states, and its θ-invariant part is the integer-weight part. The Monster as automorphism group

The deepest result of FLM's construction is that Aut(V♮) = 𝕄. The weight-2 space (grade 1 in the J-indexing) inherits a commutative non-associative product from the vertex operator (the v(1)w coefficient), making it the 196884-dimensional Griess algebra whose automorphism group Griess proved to be the Monster in 1982. Because V♮ is a VOA, the vertex operators propagate any automorphism of V1♮ to all higher grades, so Aut(V♮) embeds into the automorphism group of the Griess algebra, which is 𝕄. FLM then show every Monster element extends to a VOA automorphism: the embedding is surjective.

Why the twisted sector is essential. On its own, VΛ24+ has the wrong graded dimension, and its automorphism group is 224·Co1, where Co1 = Co0/{±1} and Co0 is the automorphism group of the Leech lattice. The symmetries of V♮ that preserve the split into untwisted and twisted sectors form 21+24·Co1, which is the centraliser of an involution of class 2B in 𝕄. Co1 itself is only a quotient of that subgroup; it does not sit inside 𝕄 as a subgroup. FLM add one more automorphism that mixes the two sectors, and together with 21+24·Co1 it generates the whole Monster.

What FLM proved, and what remained

FLM established that V♮ is a VOA with the correct graded dimension and automorphism group, but they did not prove the full Conway–Norton conjecture. They could compute graded traces for elements of the involution centraliser 21+24·Co1, but had no method that reached every one of the 194 conjugacy classes. Proving that every Tg(τ) is the predicted Hauptmodul required a Lie algebra large enough to encode the j-function's product expansion and a denominator identity rigid enough to constrain the graded traces. This is Borcherds' contribution, the subject of Part 14.

Takeaways

  • Vertex Operator Algebras (VOAs): A VOA is an algebraic structure inspired by string theory. It allows us to define an infinite-dimensional space where elements interact in highly constrained, symmetric ways.
  • The FLM Construction: Frenkel, Lepowsky, and Meurman constructed the Moonshine Module (V♮) by taking the VOA of the 24-dimensional Leech lattice and performing an "orbifold" operation (folding it over its own symmetries) to remove the weight-1 states.
  • The Griess Algebra: Because there are no weight-1 states, the lowest non-trivial grade of V♮ contains exactly 196884 dimensions. This forms the Griess algebra, and the group of symmetries that preserve this algebra is precisely the Monster group.
  • The Final Piece: FLM proved that this module exists and that its symmetries are the Monster. But they didn't prove that its trace functions are the Hauptmoduls predicted by Conway and Norton. That required Richard Borcherds.
