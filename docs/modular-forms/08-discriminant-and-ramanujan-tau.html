The Discriminant, Ramanujan's τ, and Cusp Forms

Part 8 of 20. Δ(τ) = η(τ)²&sup4; = q∏(1−qⁿ)²&sup4; = Σ τ(n)qⁿ, the unique weight-12 cusp form. Ramanujan's τ is multiplicative, and τ(n) ≡ σ₁₁(n) mod 691.

Unlike the Eisenstein series, which approach 1 as they go higher up the imaginary axis, a cusp form is a modular form that strictly vanishes (goes to zero) as it approaches the cusp (infinity). The first and most important of these functions is the modular discriminant, denoted by Δ(τ).

Why "discriminant". A lattice Λ = ℤτ + ℤ defines an elliptic curve y² = 4x³ − g₂x − g₃; the cubic's discriminant g₂³ − 27g₃² equals (2π)¹² Δ(τ). Δ ≠ 0 iff the cubic has distinct roots and the curve is non-singular. Since Δ vanishes only at the cusp, every lattice in ℍ gives a non-singular curve.

Ramanujan's τ function

The q-expansion of Δ defines an arithmetic function:

n ↦ τ(n) is Ramanujan's tau function. Values: τ(1) = 1, τ(2) = −24, τ(3) = 252, τ(4) = −1472, τ(5) = 4830. Signs oscillate irregularly; magnitudes grow as n11/2.

In 1916, Ramanujan computed the first 30 values and conjectured three remarkable properties:

  1. Multiplicativity: τ(mn) = τ(m)τ(n) whenever gcd(m, n) = 1.
  2. Prime-power recursion: τ(pr+1) = τ(p)τ(pr) − p11τ(pr−1) for each prime p.
  3. The Ramanujan conjecture: |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 for each prime p.

Mordell proved the first two properties in 1917 using Hecke operators (explainer 10): Δ is a Hecke eigenform, and eigenforms have multiplicative coefficients. The third waited until Deligne's 1974 proof of the Weil conjectures.

65520/691 has 691 in the denominator; reducing mod 691 kills the Eisenstein contribution, leaving τ(n) ≡ σ11(n). This is the first instance of a recurring phenomenon: primes dividing Bernoulli-number numerators control congruences between modular forms.

Why 691 matters more broadly. 691 is an irregular prime: it divides the numerator of B12/12, equivalently the class group of ℚ(ζp) has p-torsion. The Ramanujan congruence is a shadow of the Herbrand–Ribet theorem.

Lehmer's conjecture and open questions

Lehmer (1947) asked whether τ(n) = 0 for any n ≥ 1. Verified up to n ~ 1022, but still open.

Deligne's bound |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 implies |τ(n)| ≤ d(n) · n11/2 where d(n) is the divisor count. So τ(n) grows roughly as n11/2 = n(k−1)/2.

The Sato–Tate conjecture (theorem, Barnet-Lamb–Geraghty–Harris–Taylor 2011) says τ(p)/p11/2 is equidistributed with respect to (2/π)√(1 − t²) dt on [−1, 1] — like traces of random SU(2) elements.

Takeaways

  • The Discriminant: Δ is a cusp form of weight 12. Unlike Eisenstein series, it vanishes at the cusp.
  • Ramanujan's τ: The coefficients of Δ's q-expansion form a sequence of numbers with deep, mysterious arithmetic properties.
  • Multiplicativity: If you pick any two numbers without common factors, multiplying their τ values together gives the τ value of their product.
  • The 691 Congruence: The τ numbers share a hidden link with the divisor sums from the last explainer, tied together by the prime number 691.
