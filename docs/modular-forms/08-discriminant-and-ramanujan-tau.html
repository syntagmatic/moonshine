In the previous explainer we built Eisenstein series Ek and saw that their q-expansions have constant term 1 and arithmetically meaningful coefficients. Why "discriminant"? The name comes from the theory of elliptic curves. A lattice Λ = ℤτ + ℤ determines an elliptic curve y² = 4x³ − g2x − g3, and the polynomial discriminant of the cubic is g2³ − 27g3², which (after standard normalisation) equals (2π)12 Δ(τ). When Δ ≠ 0, the cubic has distinct roots and the curve is nonsingular. Since Δ vanishes only at the cusp and never on ℍ, every lattice in the upper half-plane gives a nonsingular curve.

Ramanujan's τ function

The q-expansion of Δ defines an arithmetic function:

The function n ↦ τ(n) is Ramanujan's tau function. Its first few values are: τ(1) = 1, τ(2) = −24, τ(3) = 252, τ(4) = −1472, τ(5) = 4830. The signs oscillate irregularly, the absolute values grow roughly as n11/2, and the sequence is dense with number-theoretic surprises.

In 1916, Ramanujan computed the first 30 values and conjectured three remarkable properties:

  1. Multiplicativity: τ(mn) = τ(m)τ(n) whenever gcd(m, n) = 1.
  2. Prime-power recursion: τ(pr+1) = τ(p)τ(pr) − p11τ(pr−1) for each prime p.
  3. The Ramanujan conjecture: |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 for each prime p.

The first two were proved by Mordell in 1917 using what we now call Hecke operators (explainer 10): Δ is a Hecke eigenform, and eigenforms have multiplicative coefficients. The third waited until Deligne's proof of the Weil conjectures in 1974, a Fields Medal result.

Multiplicativity checker

Multiplicativity says that the value of τ at a product mn, when m and n are coprime, is determined by its values at m and n separately. This is a deep structural property: it says Δ is a simultaneous eigenfunction for all Hecke operators. We can verify it directly.

The coefficient 65520/691 has 691 in the denominator, and when we reduce mod 691, the Eisenstein contribution vanishes, leaving τ(n) ≡ σ11(n). This congruence is the first example of a phenomenon that runs throughout the modern theory: primes dividing numerators of Bernoulli numbers control congruences between modular forms of different types.

Why 691 matters beyond this congruence. The prime 691 divides the numerator of B12/12, which makes it an irregular prime in the sense of Kummer. Irregular primes are exactly those for which the class group of ℚ(ζp) has p-torsion. The Ramanujan congruence is thus a shadow of deep algebraic number theory, connecting modular forms to class groups and the Herbrand–Ribet theorem.

Lehmer's conjecture and open questions

In 1947, D. H. Lehmer asked whether τ(n) = 0 for any n ≥ 1. Despite extensive computation (the conjecture has been verified for n up to at least 1022), the question remains open. There is no known n with τ(n) = 0, but no proof that such an n cannot exist.

What we do know about the size of τ(n) is controlled by two results. Deligne's bound gives |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 for primes p, which implies |τ(n)| ≤ d(n) · n11/2 where d(n) is the number of divisors. Since d(n) grows slower than any power of n, the values of τ(n) grow roughly as n11/2, with the exponent 11/2 = (k−1)/2 for weight k = 12.

In the other direction, the Sato–Tate conjecture (now a theorem, proved by Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, and Taylor in 2011) tells us that the normalised values τ(p)/p11/2 are equidistributed with respect to the Sato–Tate measure (2/π)√(1 − t²) dt on [−1, 1]. The values of τ at primes are distributed like the traces of random elements of SU(2).

What we take with us

  • Δ = (E4³ − E6²)/1728 is the unique normalised weight-12 cusp form. It equals η(τ)24 = q∏(1 − qn)24.
  • The q-expansion Δ = Σ τ(n)qn defines Ramanujan's τ. It is multiplicative (because Δ is a Hecke eigenform) and satisfies |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 (Deligne).
  • The congruence τ(n) ≡ σ11(n) mod 691 comes from the Bernoulli number B12 and foreshadows the theory of Galois representations attached to modular forms.
  • Whether τ(n) = 0 for any n remains Lehmer's open conjecture.
  • Δ vanishes at the cusp and nowhere else on ℍ, ensuring that every lattice gives a nonsingular elliptic curve.
