Modular Forms

The functions that respect the lattice. Fifteen interactive explainers from the upper half-plane and its hyperbolic geometry through Eisenstein series, Hecke operators, and L-functions, culminating in the modularity theorem and the j-function's role as the universal parameter for elliptic curves.

In the 1870s, Dedekind and Klein realised that certain functions on the upper half-plane ℍ = {τ ∈ ℂ : Im τ > 0} carry extraordinary arithmetic information, but only if they transform in a very particular way under the modular group SL₂(ℤ). The constraint is severe: a modular form of weight k must satisfy f((aτ+b)/(cτ+d)) = (cτ+d)k f(τ) for every integer matrix with determinant 1. This single transformation law, combined with holomorphicity, is so restrictive that the space of weight-k modular forms is finite-dimensional, and its q-series coefficients encode deep information about number theory, geometry, and physics.

The series tells this story in three acts. Act I builds the stage: the upper half-plane as a hyperbolic space, SL₂(ℤ) as its isometry group, the fundamental domain, cusps, and the group's generators and relations. Act II develops the classical theory: Eisenstein series and their lattice-sum origins, the discriminant Δ and Ramanujan's τ function, the graded ring of modular forms generated by E4 and E6, and Hecke operators that decompose forms into arithmetic atoms. Act III builds the bridges to modern arithmetic: modular curves, L-functions, the modularity theorem connecting elliptic curves to weight-2 forms, the j-function as universal parameter, Maass forms, and the wider automorphic landscape leading toward the Langlands program.

The companion monstrous moonshine series picks up where this one ends: the j-function's q-expansion coefficients 1, 744, 196 884, … encode the dimensions of representations of the Monster group, a connection that required the full machinery of vertex operator algebras to explain. The Lie algebras series provides the algebraic context via affine Kac–Moody algebras, and the E8 lattice series connects through theta functions and lattice sums.
