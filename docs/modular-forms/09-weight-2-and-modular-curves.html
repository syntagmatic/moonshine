Weight 2, Differential Forms, and Modular Curves

Part 9 of 14. Weight-2 modular forms on Γ₀(N) are holomorphic 1-forms on the modular curve X₀(N). The Mellin transform gives L-functions with functional equations.

Weight-2 forms don't exist for the full SL₂(ℤ). To find them we weaken the group to a congruence subgroup Γ₀(N), which enforces the modular law only for matrices whose lower-left entry is divisible by the level N. In exchange for that lost invariance, the quotient stops being a sphere and becomes a curve X₀(N) with genuine holes.

The number of holes, the genus, equals the dimension of the space of weight-2 cusp forms on Γ₀(N), because each such cusp form is a holomorphic differential on X₀(N). This is the setting where elliptic curves will live in the next part. In the explorer and chart below, the genus stays at 0 until N = 11, the first level with a weight-2 cusp form.

For prime N = p the index is p + 1, there are 2 cusps, ν₂ = 1 + (−1|p) and ν₃ = 1 + (−3|p), so the genus depends on p mod 12. N = 11 gives genus 1, and N = 23 and N = 37 both give genus 2.

For N = 1 through 10 the genus is 0. At N = 11 it jumps to 1, and X₀(11) is itself an elliptic curve. The unique cusp form in S₂(Γ₀(11)) corresponds to y² + y = x³ − x² − 10x − 20, the first curve in Cremona's tables.

At N = 11 the unique normalised cusp form is f(τ) = q − 2q² − q³ + 2q⁴ + q⁵ + 2q⁶ − 2q⁷ + ⋯, and f(τ)dτ is the unique holomorphic 1-form on the genus-1 curve X₀(11).

The root number ε = ±1 is fixed by the Atkin–Lehner involution wN: τ ↦ −1/(Nτ), which normalises Γ₀(N) and acts on S₂(Γ₀(N)). A newform f is an eigenvector of wN with eigenvalue η ∈ {±1}, and in weight 2, ε = −η. When ε = −1 the functional equation forces L(1, f) = 0, and BSD then predicts that the elliptic curve has positive rank.

For prime N, η = −a(N). At N = 11, a(11) = 1, so η = −1 and ε = +1. There L(1, f) ≠ 0 and the curve y² + y = x³ − x² − 10x − 20 has rank 0. At N = 37, ε = −1 forces L(1, f) = 0, and the curve has rank 1.

The functional equation of Λ is equivalent to the modularity of f: under the Mellin transform, τ ↦ −1/(Nτ) becomes s ↦ 2 − s. The next part turns this around. The modularity theorem says every elliptic curve over ℚ arises from such a form.
