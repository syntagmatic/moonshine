Cusps and the Cusp-Form Condition

Part 4 of 14. Cusps are the points ℚ ∪ {i∞} on the boundary of ℍ, all equivalent under SL₂(ℤ). A modular form must be holomorphic at cusps; a cusp form must vanish there.

The fundamental domain of Part 3 is not compact: it has a spike running up to i∞. Every tile has a spike too: the translates of F share the one at i∞, and every other tile ends at a rational point of the real axis. These boundary points are the cusps, and the definition of a modular form includes a condition on how it behaves there.

What is a cusp?

The cusps of SL₂(ℤ) are the points of ℚ ∪ {i∞}. They lie on the boundary of ℍ, not in it: i∞ is the top of the spike of F, and each rational p/q is the end of a spike in some other tile.

SL₂(ℤ) acts transitively on ℚ ∪ {i∞}: given p/q in lowest terms, find a matrix

with ad − bc = 1 sending i∞ to a/c = p/q. Given p/q in lowest terms, the extended Euclidean algorithm finds integers x, y with px + qy = 1, and then [[p, −y],[q, x]] has determinant 1 and sends i∞ to p/q. Composing through i∞ connects any two rationals, so all cusps are equivalent and there is a single cusp orbit, represented by i∞.

The compactified quotient ℍ*/SL₂(ℤ) is therefore a compact Riemann surface of genus 0 with one cusp. The congruence subgroups Γ₀(N) of Part 9 have more than one: two when N is prime, and more when N has repeated or many prime factors (six for N = 12).

The q-expansion

The map τ ↦ q = e2πiτ sends ℍ to the punctured disk {0 < |q| < 1}. Since |q| = e−2πy, the cusp Im τ → ∞ goes to q = 0. A function with f(τ + 1) = f(τ) is a function of q:

This Laurent series is the q-expansion, and behaviour at i∞ becomes behaviour at q = 0. A function is holomorphic at the cusp when a(n) = 0 for all n < 0, so there is no pole at q = 0; this is part of the definition of a modular form. It vanishes at the cusp when also a(0) = 0, and a modular form with that property is a cusp form.

Eisenstein series have constant term 1 after normalisation. The discriminant Δ(τ) = q − 24q² + 252q³ − ⋯ has a(0) = 0 and is the first cusp form.
