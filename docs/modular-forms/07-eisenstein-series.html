Eisenstein Series and q-Expansions

Part 7 of 20. Ek(τ) = ½ Σ 1/(mτ+n)k. Converges for k ≥ 4. The q-expansion has integer coefficients involving divisor sums σk−1(n).

We have ℍ, SL₂(ℤ), and the definition of a modular form. The Eisenstein series are the simplest non-trivial examples: sum a weight-k expression over the lattice.

The q-expansion of an Eisenstein series has integer coefficients built from divisor sums. Geometry encodes arithmetic.

The lattice-sum definition

Fix an even integer k ≥ 4. The Eisenstein series of weight k is defined by summing over all nonzero points of the lattice ℤ + τℤ:

The sum runs over (m, n) ∈ ℤ² \ {(0, 0)}. The factor ½ accounts for (m, n) ↔ (−m, −n) producing the same value for even k.

The lattice Λτ = ℤ + τℤ is an infinite grid in ℂ; Ek(τ) is a "potential" with each lattice point contributing 1/(distance)k. Close points dominate; distant ones contribute negligibly.

Absolute convergence

The sum converges absolutely for k ≥ 4 but not for k = 2. The lattice has ~R points at radius R, each contributing 1/Rk, so the annulus contribution is R1−k, summable iff k > 2. k = 2 is conditionally convergent and gives a quasi-modular form.

Restricting to m = 0, n ≠ 0 gives 1/nk summing to 2ζ(k), which converges for k ≥ 2. The full 2D sum needs k ≥ 4 because the lattice has ~R points per radius R.

SL₂(ℤ) permutes the lattice points, so the sum is automatically modular. Modularity is built in. Ek is holomorphic on ℍ and bounded at the cusp.

For odd k the series vanishes: terms (mτ + n)−k and (−mτ − n)−k = −(mτ + n)−k cancel.

E4: the first Eisenstein series

E4 is the lowest-weight modular form: M2 = 0 and dim M4 = 1, so every weight-4 form is a scalar multiple of E4.

E6: weight-6 Eisenstein series

M6 is one-dimensional, spanned by E6. Together E4 and E6 generate the entire graded ring of modular forms: every modular form is a polynomial in E4 and E6.

Now the coefficient of qn is −504 σ5(n). These grow quickly: σ5(2) = 1 + 32 = 33, so the second coefficient is −504 · 33 = −16 632.

E4's coefficients are all positive; E6's alternate. Both have integer coefficients — not obvious from the lattice-sum definition.

Divisor sums

The divisor sum function σk(n) is the sum of the k-th powers of all positive divisors of n:

For instance, the divisors of 12 are {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}, so σ3(12) = 1 + 8 + 27 + 64 + 216 + 1728 = 2044. When k = 0, σ0(n) simply counts the number of divisors. When k = 1, σ1(n) is the sum of divisors. For the Eisenstein series Ek, the relevant divisor sum is σk−1(n).

A crucial property: σk is multiplicative. If gcd(m, n) = 1, then σk(mn) = σk(m) · σk(n). This follows from the bijection between divisors of mn and pairs (d1, d2) where d1 | m and d2 | n. Multiplicativity means that to compute σk(n) for any n, it suffices to know it at prime powers and then multiply. For prime powers, the formula simplifies to a geometric sum:

When n = p is prime, the only divisors are 1 and p, so σk(p) = 1 + pk. This means the coefficient of qp in Ek is ck(1 + pk−1), which grows like pk−1. The prime coefficients carry the most "raw" arithmetic information; composite coefficients are determined by multiplicativity. Why integer coefficients. ck = −2k/Bk is an integer (von Staudt–Clausen), and σk−1(n) is an integer by definition. Integrality is required for the arithmetic theory: the ring of modular forms over ℤ is generated by E4 and E6.

Lattice visualisation

The lattice has basis 1 and τ. Each point is m + nτ. Varying τ deforms the lattice: τ = i gives a square grid, τ = e2πi/3 gives a hexagonal grid, general τ gives an oblique parallelogram tiling.

Convergence vs weight. The tail is O(R1−k), so each additional unit of weight buys another power of decay.

From lattice sum to q-expansion: two views of the same function

The lattice sum makes modularity obvious but hides arithmetic. The q-expansion makes arithmetic explicit (divisor sums, integrality, multiplicativity) but hides modularity. The Lipschitz summation formula bridges them. The same duality appears for the discriminant, the j-function, and L-functions.

Takeaways

  • Ek(τ) is defined as a sum over the lattice ℤ + τℤ, converging absolutely for k ≥ 4.
  • The q-expansion is Ek = 1 + ck ∑ σk−1(n) qn, with c4 = 240, c6 = −504.
  • Divisor sums σk(n) = ∑d|n dk are the building blocks of Eisenstein coefficients.
  • All coefficients are integers. The ring of modular forms is generated by E4 and E6 over ℤ.
  • E8 = E42 because M8 is one-dimensional, giving non-trivial identities among divisor sums.
  • In the next explainer, we use E4 and E6 to build the discriminant Δ = (E43 − E62)/1728, the first cusp form, whose coefficients are the Ramanujan τ-function.
