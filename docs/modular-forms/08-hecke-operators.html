Hecke Operators and the Hecke Algebra

Part 8 of 14. Tn is a linear operator on Mk. On q-series: Tp acts by am ↦ apm + pk−1 am/p. Hecke eigenforms have multiplicative coefficients and Euler-product L-functions.

So far the q-coefficients have been Fourier data, facts about a function of τ. The Hecke operators Tn are linear maps on Mk that mix those coefficients by an explicit arithmetic rule. A form that every Tn merely rescales is a Hecke eigenform. Its coefficients are then multiplicative, its L-function is an Euler product, and its coefficient a(p) is the eigenvalue of Tp.

In the calculator below, Tp sends Δ, E₄ and E₆ to scalar multiples of themselves. E₄³ is different. It lives in the two-dimensional space M₁₂ spanned by Δ and E₁₂, which have different eigenvalues, so Tp(E₄³) is not a multiple of E₄³.

What Hecke operators do

For a modular form f = Σ a(m) qm of weight k and prime p, the Hecke operator Tp gives a new modular form with coefficients

(a(m/p) = 0 if p ∤ m.) Each new coefficient combines a "further out" a(pm) and a "closer in" a(m/p) weighted by pk−1. Tp is linear, preserving both Mk and Sk.

For composite n, the Hecke operators satisfy the recursion Tmn = Tm Tn when gcd(m, n) = 1, and Tpr = Tp Tpr−1 − pk−1 Tpr−2 for prime powers. So the prime operators Tp determine all the others.

Commutativity and simultaneous diagonalisation

The Hecke operators commute:

for all m and n. They generate a commutative algebra on Mk that can be simultaneously diagonalised, and its eigenvectors are the Hecke eigenforms.

When dim Sk = 1 the unique cusp form is automatically a Hecke eigenform. This applies to S12, S16, S18, S20, S22.

The first weight with dim Sk > 1 is k = 24 (dim 2). There the Hecke operators have to be diagonalised, and the eigenforms that come out are not simple products of Eisenstein series and Δ.
