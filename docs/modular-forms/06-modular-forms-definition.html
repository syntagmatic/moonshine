Modular Forms: Definition and Weight

Part 6 of 20. A holomorphic function f: ℍ → ℂ is a modular form of weight k if f(γ·τ) = (cτ+d)k f(τ) for all γ ∈ SL₂(ℤ), and f is holomorphic at cusps.

Why weight matters

The weight determines dim Mk(SL₂(ℤ)):

  • Weight 0: dim M₀ = 1. The only modular form of weight 0 is a constant. (The j-function is not a modular form because it has a pole at i∞.)
  • Weight 2: dim M₂ = 0. There are no nonzero modular forms of weight 2 for the full modular group. The Eisenstein series E₂ fails the transformation law by a correction term.
  • Weight 4: dim M₄ = 1. The space is one-dimensional, spanned by the Eisenstein series E₄.
  • Weight 6: dim M₆ = 1. One-dimensional, spanned by E₆.
  • Weight 8: dim M₈ = 1. Spanned by E₈, and since E₄² is also a weight-8 form, we get the identity E₈ = E₄².
  • Weight 10: dim M₁₀ = 1. Spanned by E₁₀ = E₄E₆.
  • Weight 12: dim M₁₂ = 2. This is the first weight with room for a cusp form, and the cusp form is Δ = (E₄³ − E₆²)/1728, the discriminant function.

Odd weights vanish. −I ∈ SL₂(ℤ), so f(τ) = (−1)k f(τ) forces f = 0 for odd k.

The weight-k slash action. (f|kγ)(τ) = (cτ+d)−kf(γτ) is a right action of SL₂(ℤ) on holomorphic functions. Modular forms are the action-fixed functions holomorphic at cusps. The cocycle condition makes this a genuine group action.

Takeaways

  • A modular form of weight k satisfies f((aτ+b)/(cτ+d)) = (cτ+d)kf(τ) for all [[a,b],[c,d]] ∈ SL₂(ℤ), and is holomorphic at cusps.
  • For T: f(τ+1) = f(τ) (periodicity, giving the q-expansion). For S: f(−1/τ) = τkf(τ) (the deep constraint).
  • The automorphy factor (cτ+d)k satisfies a cocycle condition ensuring consistency under composition.
  • Odd weights give no forms (−I forces f = 0). Weight 2 gives none for the full group. Weights 4, 6, 8, 10 each give a one-dimensional space. Weight 12 is the first with a cusp form (Δ).
  • Every modular form is a polynomial in E₄ and E₆, so the graded ring of modular forms is ℂ[E₄, E₆].
