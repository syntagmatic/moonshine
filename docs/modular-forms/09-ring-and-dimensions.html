The Ring of Modular Forms and Dimension Formulas

Part 9 of 20. M* = ℂ[E₄, E₆]. Every modular form is a polynomial in E₄ and E₆. dim Mk depends on k via ⌊k/12⌋, and Sk = Δ · Mk−12.

Modular forms form a graded ring: products are modular forms with weights adding. The graded ring for SL₂(ℤ) is a polynomial ring in two generators, with exact dimension formulas at each weight.

To find a modular form of weight k with prescribed properties, search polynomials in E₄ and E₆ of the right total weight. The search space is finite-dimensional with known dimension.

The graded ring M*

fg ∈ Mj+k; multiplication respects the grading. E₄ and E₆ are explicit generators, and the structure theorem says they are all we need:

Every modular form is a polynomial in E₄ and E₆. The monomial E₄aE₆b has weight 4a + 6b, so Mk is spanned by non-negative integer solutions to 4a + 6b = k.

Why E₄ and E₆ suffice. gcd(4, 6) = 2, so every even k ≥ 4 (except k = 2 where M₂ = 0) is 4a + 6b. Induction on weight: subtract a multiple of E₄aE₆b to kill the constant term, producing a cusp form. For k < 12 cusp forms vanish; for k ≥ 12, divide by Δ to drop to weight k − 12.

Dimension formulas

Riemann–Roch on X(1) = ℍ*/SL₂(ℤ) (genus 0, orbifold points of orders 2 and 3, one cusp) gives:

For negative or odd k, dim Mk = 0 (−I forces f = (−1)kf, no holomorphic forms of negative weight). dim M₀ = 1 (constants). For k = 2 the orbifold contribution reduces to 0. For even k ≥ 4 the dimension grows as k/12, stepping up by 1 each multiple of 12.

A cusp form has zero constant term, costing one degree of freedom: dim Sk = dim Mk − 1 for k ≥ 4, and 0 for k < 12. dim S₁₂ = 1 picks out Δ as the unique normalised weight-12 cusp form.

Weight 4: E₄ alone, dim M₄ = 1. Weight 6: E₆, dim M₆ = 1. Weight 8: E₄², dim M₈ = 1 (forcing E₈ = E₄²). Weight 10: E₄E₆, dim M₁₀ = 1. Weight 12: {E₄³, E₆²}, dim M₁₂ = 2, with Δ = (E₄³ − E₆²)/1728 spanning S₁₂.

The dimension increases by 1 each multiple of 12, with a correction at weights ≡ 2 mod 12.

The identity E₈ = E₄². dim M₈ = 1 and both forms are normalised, so they must be equal. Comparing q-coefficients gives σ₇(n) = σ₃(n) + 120 Σm σ₃(m)σ₃(n−m). An arithmetic identity proved by finite-dimensionality of an analytic function space.

The ideal of cusp forms

Cusp forms Sk ⊂ Mk have a₀ = 0. S* = ⊕ Sk is an ideal in M*, generated by Δ:

Every cusp form of weight k is Δ times a weight-(k − 12) form. f ↦ Δf is an isomorphism Mk−12 ≅ Sk; the inverse f ↦ f/Δ is holomorphic because Δ vanishes only at the cusp where f vanishes too.

So dim Sk = dim Mk−12, and S* is the principal ideal (Δ) inside ℂ[E₄, E₆].

S₁₂ = ℂ · Δ is 1-dimensional. S₁₆ = Δ · M₄ = ℂ · ΔE₄. The first 2-dimensional cusp space is k = 24: S₂₄ = Δ · M₁₂ with basis {Δ · E₄³, Δ · E₆²} or {Δ², Δ · E₄³}.

Takeaways

  • M* = ℂ[E₄, E₆]: the graded ring of modular forms for SL₂(ℤ) is a polynomial ring in E₄ (weight 4) and E₆ (weight 6).
  • Dimension formula: dim Mk = ⌊k/12⌋ + 1 for even k ≥ 4 with k not congruent to 2 mod 12; ⌊k/12⌋ if k ≡ 2 mod 12.
  • Cusp forms: Sk = Δ · Mk−12, so dim Sk = dim Mk − 1 for k ≥ 4.
  • Free identities: when dim Mk = 1, normalised forms of weight k are forced to be equal, yielding arithmetic identities like E₈ = E₄².
