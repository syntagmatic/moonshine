The four steps

  1. Affine E₈ and conformal vectors. Start with the affine (extended) E₈ Dynkin diagram, written Ẽ8, which has 9 nodes instead of 8. A specific node of Ẽ8, the extending node (conventionally labelled α₀), corresponds to a sublattice of finite index inside E8. This sublattice, when transported into the vertex-operator-algebra framework (Act III), gives a conformal vector of central charge ½. A conformal vector is an element of the VOA whose associated vertex operator generates a copy of the Virasoro algebra; "central charge ½" means it generates the simplest possible conformal field theory, the Ising model.
  2. D₄ triality and the E₈³ configuration. The E8 root system contains a D4 sublattice, and D4 has a unique 3-fold symmetry called triality, the only Dynkin diagram with an order-3 automorphism. Using triality, the single conformal vector from step 1 can be conjugated into a triple of conformal vectors, one per E8 factor. This produces three orthogonal copies of E8 inside ℝ24: the E₈³ configuration we met in the previous explainer as the deep hole of Λ24.
  3. Embedding in the Leech lattice. The sum of the three E8 sublattices from step 2 is isometrically embedded in Λ24. More precisely, the E₈³ deep-hole neighbourhood provides an explicit identification of the three-fold direct sum E8 ⊕ E8 ⊕ E8 with a sublattice of Λ24. The conformal vectors from steps 1–2 thereby live inside the lattice VOA VΛ₂₄ that Act III will construct.
  4. Miyamoto's involution bijection. In the 1990s, Masahiko Miyamoto proved that conformal vectors of central charge ½ inside a vertex operator algebra correspond bijectively to involutions (elements of order 2) of the automorphism group of that VOA. Applied to V♮ (the moonshine module, which is an orbifold of VΛ₂₄), this bijection maps the conformal vectors we built in steps 1–3 to specific involutions of the Monster. These involutions land in the Monster's 2A conjugacy class, the class of involutions that generates the entire group.

Why the bridge matters

Remove any step and the chain breaks. Without E8 there are no conformal vectors; without triality the triple doesn't form; without Λ24 the triples have no 24-dim home; without Miyamoto, vectors don't become group elements.

The 2A involutions

The Monster has involution classes 2A and 2B. The 2A involutions arise from central-charge-½ conformal vectors via Miyamoto and generate the Monster. Each 2A class corresponds to an E8 sublattice configuration inside Λ24: the Monster's algebra is, at bottom, the algebra of E8-shaped structures inside the Leech lattice.

Graded vector spaces and the moonshine specification

An infinite-dimensional ℤ-graded vector space is a vector space V that decomposes as a direct sum of finite-dimensional pieces indexed by integers:

Each Vn is called a homogeneous component or grade. A graded space is infinite-dimensional overall, but every individual grade is finite-dimensional, so we can do linear algebra one grade at a time.

The graded dimension of V is the formal power series

which encodes all the finite-dimensional data at once. If every dim Vn is a non-negative integer (as it always is), the graded dimension is a formal power series with non-negative integer coefficients, just like a q-expansion of a modular form.

Group actions on graded spaces

If a group G acts on V by linear maps that preserve the grading (meaning each g ∈ G sends Vn into itself), then g acts as a finite matrix on each Vn, and we can take the trace Tr(g | Vn). The graded trace

is a power series in q that encodes how g acts across all grades. For the identity element g = 1, the trace of the identity matrix on Vn is just dim Vn, so the graded trace of the identity is the graded dimension. For other elements of G, the graded trace is a different power series; in representation theory the trace of a group element on a representation is called a character value, so the graded trace is the "graded character" of V evaluated at g.

The Conway–Norton wish list

Armed with this language, the Conway–Norton conjecture says: there exists a graded Monster module V♮ with the following specific properties.

Three grades are structurally special:

Grade −1: dim 1. The pole of j. The one-dimensional grade V−1♮ is a copy of ℂ on which the Monster acts trivially (by the trivial representation). This produces the q−1 term in the q-expansion.

Grade 0: dim 0. Nonzero V0♮ would generate a Lie algebra acting on V♮, making the automorphism group a Lie group times a finite group. dim V0♮ = 0 forces the automorphism group to be purely discrete. This is the consequence of the Leech lattice having no roots.

Grade 1: dim 196884. This is the Griess algebra. The 196884-dimensional space V1♮ carries a commutative non-associative product (the one Griess defined in 1982), and the group preserving that product is the Monster. The decomposition 1 ⊕ 196883 into Monster irreps reproduces McKay's observation.

Why j − 744 and not j. The graded dimension of V♮ is j(τ) − 744, not j(τ) itself. The constant 744 is missing because dim V0♮ = 0, while the constant term of j is 744. The "missing 744" is the algebraic content of the rootless condition: the Leech lattice's absence of roots removes exactly the 744 dimensions that would have appeared at grade 0.

Hauptmoduls as a constraint

Graded dimension fixes dim Vn♮ = c(n), but not the Monster representations. The genus-zero constraint — each graded trace Tg(τ) is a Hauptmodul — pins down the representations. A Hauptmodul for Γ ⊂ SL₂(ℝ) generates the field of Γ-invariant functions on ℍ, existing iff ℍ/Γ has genus zero. j is the Hauptmodul for SL₂(ℤ); the 194 McKay–Thompson series are Hauptmoduls for 194 other genus-zero groups, tabulated by Conway and Norton and proved by Borcherds.

Takeaways

  • The Bridge: There are four precise mathematical steps that take us from the E₈ lattice, through the Leech lattice, and finally to the Monster group.
  • The Missing 744: The constant term 744 in the j-function is missing from the dimensions. This is the direct result of the Leech lattice having no "roots" (vectors of squared length 2).
  • The Wish List: Conway and Norton laid out exactly what properties a hypothetical "Moonshine Module" would need to have to perfectly explain the coincidence.
  • The Challenge: The challenge was set: to prove Moonshine, mathematicians needed to actually construct this module and prove that it behaves exactly as Conway and Norton predicted.
