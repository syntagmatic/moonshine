The Wider Landscape: Automorphic Forms and the Langlands Program

Part 15 of 20. Modular forms are GL₂ automorphic forms. Langlands (1967) conjectured that every arithmetic L-function arises from an automorphic form. Connections to Lie algebras, moonshine, and information geometry.

The series so far has been the theory of modular forms on GL₂: Eisenstein series, Δ and Ramanujan's τ, Hecke eigenforms, modularity.

GL₂ is one reductive group. Replacing it with GLn or an arbitrary reductive G gives the theory of automorphic forms, organised by the Langlands program.

An automorphic form on a reductive group G is a function on G(ℚ)\G(𝔸ℚ) with growth conditions, K-finiteness, and annihilation by a centre ideal. For G = GL₂ this recovers classical modular forms; the definition works for any G — GLn, symplectic, orthogonal, exceptional groups.

The Langlands program generalises modularity from GL₂ to all reductive groups, unifying number theory, representation theory, and algebraic geometry.

The Langlands program in one paragraph

Every arithmetic L-function should equal the L-function of an automorphic form on some GLn. Langlands reciprocity predicts a correspondence between n-dimensional Galois representations and automorphic representations of GLn(𝔸ℚ). Modularity is the n = 2 case. Langlands proposed the general conjecture in 1967.

Why "reciprocity". It connects to quadratic reciprocity. Artin reciprocity (class field theory) handles abelian extensions = GL₁ automorphic forms = Dirichlet characters. Langlands extends this to non-abelian extensions via GLn.

p-adic modular forms

Modular forms also have a p-adic life. A p-adic modular form is a p-adic limit of q-expansions (Serre 1973, Katz). Ek and Ek' are p-adically close when k ≡ k' modulo (p−1)pm.

Overconvergent modular forms (Coleman, 1996) and the eigencurve (Coleman–Mazur, 1998) — a rigid analytic curve parametrising p-adic eigenform families — are central to modular lifting (Wiles, Kisin, Emerton, Calegari–Geraghty).

Cross-series connections

The connections to companion series are structural:

Lie algebras and affine Kac–Moody algebras

The Exceptional Atlas develops finite-dimensional Lie algebras and affine Kac–Moody algebras. The characters of integrable highest-weight representations of an affine algebra are modular forms; the Weyl–Kac formula gives their q-expansions. The denominator identity of the Monster Lie algebra (Borcherds, Act IV) is the product expansion of the j-function.

Information geometry and the Weil–Petersson metric

The information geometry series shows ℍ as the space of Gaussian distributions parametrised by mean and variance, with the Fisher metric coinciding with the Poincaré metric. Teichmüller spaces carry the Weil–Petersson metric, a Kähler metric of negative curvature.

One object, four perspectives. ℍ is the natural domain of modular forms, the parameter space of elliptic curves, the Gaussian statistical manifold with Fisher metric, and SL₂(ℝ)/SO(2).

Takeaways

This arc has fifteen explainers of classical theory behind it, with five more to come.

  • Act I (explainers 1–5) built the stage: ℍ as a hyperbolic space, SL₂(ℤ) as its isometry group, the fundamental domain, cusps, and the group's generators and relations.
  • Act II (explainers 6–10) populated the stage with functions: Eisenstein series from lattice sums, the discriminant Δ and Ramanujan's τ, the graded ring M* = ℂ[E₄, E₆], and Hecke operators that decompose forms into arithmetic eigenforms.
  • Act III (explainers 11–15) built bridges to arithmetic: weight-2 forms as differentials on modular curves, the modularity theorem linking elliptic curves to newforms, the j-function as universal parameter, Maass forms breaking the holomorphy assumption, and the automorphic landscape beyond GL₂.
  • Act IV (explainers 16–20, coming up) follows the j-function into monstrous moonshine: the Monster group and McKay's 196 884 = 1 + 196 883 coincidence, the E8 and Leech lattices, the bridge specification, vertex operator algebras and the moonshine module V♮, and finally Borcherds' 1992 proof via the denominator identity of the Monster Lie algebra.

The transformation law f(γτ) = (cτ+d)kf(τ) is severe enough to force finite-dimensionality, to make q-coefficients carry arithmetic, and to link L-functions to primes. Modularity says it is universal: every rational elliptic curve arises from a function with this one law.

The Langlands program extends this beyond GL₂. The companion series continue the story: the Exceptional Atlas for the algebraic backbone, and information geometry for the same Poincaré metric as the Fisher metric on Gaussians.
