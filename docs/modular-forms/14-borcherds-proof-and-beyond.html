Borcherds’ Proof and Beyond

Part 14 of 14. Finite and affine Lie algebras cannot accommodate the j-function’s exponential coefficient growth. Borcherds introduced imaginary simple roots, extracted the Monster Lie algebra from V♮ via string theory’s no-ghost theorem, and obtained a denominator identity that is the product formula for j. Twisted versions yield replication formulas that reduce the full Conway–Norton conjecture to a finite check.

To prove monstrous moonshine, Richard Borcherds had to show that the graded traces of the moonshine module are the Hauptmoduls Conway and Norton predicted. He introduced generalised Kac–Moody algebras and used the no-ghost theorem from string theory to extract a Monster Lie algebra from V♮.

A note on notation: in Act IV, j(τ) = q−1 + 744 + … is the classical modular invariant, while J(τ) = j(τ) − 744 = q−1 + 0 + 196884q + … is the moonshine-normalised graded dimension. Borcherds' product formula has j(p) − j(q) on the right, which equals J(p) − J(q), so the constant term drops out. Root multiplicities are the J coefficients, so c(0) = 0 throughout this page.

The machinery yields replication formulas, rigid enough that the coefficients at n = 1, 2, 3 and 5 of a McKay–Thompson series, together with the series for the powers of g, fix every other coefficient. The figure below lays out the steps.

The limitation of finite and affine Kac–Moody algebras

Finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras have all-positive root norms; affine Kac–Moody adds null directions but with polynomial multiplicity growth. Neither matches the j-coefficients' e4π√n growth.

The constraint is in the Cartan matrix: a Kac–Moody algebra has aii = 2, so every simple root has positive norm and multiplicity 1.

Borcherds’ relaxation

Borcherds allowed aii ≤ 2. Simple roots with aii = 2 behave classically, with an sl₂ and a Weyl reflection. Simple roots with aii ≤ 0 are imaginary: they have non-positive norm, no reflection, and possibly multiplicity greater than 1.

These are generalised Kac–Moody (GKM) algebras, or Borcherds algebras. Root spaces, the triangular decomposition and the Weyl group all survive, and imaginary simple roots with large multiplicities give the growth the j-function needs.

Real roots versus imaginary roots

A real root lies in the Weyl orbit of a positive-norm simple root and has multiplicity 1. Every other root is imaginary, with zero or negative norm and possibly multiplicity greater than 1.

Finite Lie algebras have only real roots. Affine algebras have imaginary roots only as multiples of δ. GKM algebras can have many imaginary roots with large multiplicities.

The root system of A₂ (= sl3), whose six roots are all real, and a schematic GKM root system. Real roots are filled circles; imaginary simple roots are open amber circles.

The no-ghost theorem

Borcherds built a GKM algebra from V♮ whose denominator formula is a j-function identity, using a result from 1970s bosonic string theory. Goddard and Thorn (1972) proved that in 26 dimensions the physical states of the bosonic string have positive norm and are isomorphic to the states built from the 24 transverse directions. Above 26 dimensions negative-norm states appear.

V♮ at central charge 24 embeds into the Fock space of a 26-dimensional bosonic string, 24 transverse directions plus 2 lightcone ones. Applying the no-ghost theorem to the BRST cohomology gives a ℤ²-graded Lie algebra.

The construction of 𝔪

The Monster Lie algebra 𝔪 is ℤ²-graded:

For (m, n) ≠ (0, 0), whatever the signs, 𝔪(m,n) is isomorphic as a Monster representation to Vmn♮. The (0, 0) piece is a two-dimensional Cartan subalgebra coming from the two lightcone directions. So the (m, n) piece has dimension c(mn), the coefficient of qmn in J. Since c(0) = 0 and c(k) = 0 for k < −1, the pieces with mn = 0 or mn ≤ −2 are empty. The no-ghost theorem guarantees a non-degenerate invariant bilinear form.

The construction tensors V♮ with the vertex algebra of the two-dimensional Lorentzian lattice II1,1, giving central charge 24 + 2 = 26, the value the no-ghost theorem needs. V♮ has c = 24 because Λ24 is 24-dimensional.

Simple roots and multiplicities

The root lattice of 𝔪 is II1,1 = ℤ2 with the form (m, n)·(m′, n′) = −(mn′ + m′n), so (m, n) has norm −2mn. The simple roots are (1, n) for n = −1 and n ≥ 1, with multiplicity c(n); (1, n) has norm −2n. The root (1, −1) has norm 2 and multiplicity c(−1) = 1, and it is the only real simple root. The simple roots (1, n) for n ≥ 1 have norm −2n and are imaginary, with multiplicities c(1) = 196 884, c(2) = 21 493 760, c(3) = 864 299 970, growing exponentially as the GKM framework allows.

From the identity element to all of 𝕄: twisted denominators

The Koike–Norton–Zagier identity is the case of the identity element: it matches the graded dimension of V♮ with the j-function. The Conway–Norton conjecture asks that the graded trace of every Monster element be a specific Hauptmodul Tg(τ).

Replication formulas

From the twisted identities Borcherds extracted replication formulas: recurrences that express a Fourier coefficient of Tg through coefficients at smaller indices. Write Tg(τ) = ∑ ag(n) qn. The formulas give ag(n) for every n other than 1, 2, 3 and 5 as a polynomial in coefficients at smaller indices, of Tg itself and of the series Tgd for powers of g.

The simplest case is g = 1, where every Tgd is J. Comparing coefficients in J(τ)2 − 2c(1) = J(2τ) + J(τ/2) + J((τ+1)/2) gives every even coefficient c(2n), n ≥ 2, from coefficients with smaller index. Similar identities built from J(3τ), J(4τ) and so on reach the remaining indices, apart from 1, 2, 3 and 5. (At n = 1 the formula below reads c(2) = c(2) and says nothing.)

So once the coefficients at n = 1, 2, 3 and 5 are known, together with the series for the powers of g, every other coefficient is determined. Two series that satisfy the twisted identities and agree at those four indices agree everywhere.

The finite check

Norton and Koike had already verified by direct calculation that Conway and Norton's Hauptmodul candidates (171 distinct functions for the 194 classes) satisfy the same replication formulas. Borcherds showed that the graded traces of V♮ satisfy them too, since they come from the twisted denominator identities. What remains is to check the initial coefficients.

For each of the 194 classes, ag(1), ag(2), ag(3) and ag(5) must equal the corresponding coefficients of the conjectured Hauptmodul. These are traces of g on V1♮, V2♮, V3♮ and V5♮, computable from the known decompositions of those spaces into Monster irreducibles. The check is finite and it succeeds. Borcherds received the Fields Medal in 1998 for this work.

What came after

Borcherds’ proof settled the Conway–Norton conjecture. What remained open was why moonshine happens, and whether other groups have their own.

Mathieu Moonshine (2010)

In 2010, Eguchi, Ooguri, and Tachikawa noticed that the elliptic genus of K3 surfaces decomposes into representations of the Mathieu group M24, a sporadic simple group of order 244 823 040 that acts on the Steiner system S(5, 8, 24). The coefficients of a weak Jacobi form encode M24 representations, just as the coefficients of J encode Monster representations. Gannon proved the conjecture in 2012.

Umbral Moonshine (2012)

Cheng, Duncan, and Harvey generalised Mathieu Moonshine to a family of 23 “umbral moonshine” instances, one for each of the 23 Niemeier lattices with roots (the even unimodular lattices in 24 dimensions). Each instance pairs a finite group with a family of mock modular forms. Duncan, Griffin and Ono proved the conjecture in 2015. The 24th Niemeier lattice, Leech, has no roots and no umbral instance; its moonshine is the monstrous one.

O’Nan Moonshine (2017) and Thompson Moonshine

Duncan, Mertens, and Ono established moonshine for the O’Nan group, via weight-3/2 modular forms related to arithmetic invariants of elliptic curves. Harvey and Rayhaun proposed moonshine for the Thompson group, via weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight 1/2, and Griffin and Mertens proved it. The modular objects in both cases are not Hauptmoduls, but the pattern is the same: a sporadic group acts on a graded space whose graded traces are distinguished modular objects.

The lattices that feed this story, E8 and Leech, are the subject of the companion Exceptional Atlas series.
