Maass Forms and Non-Holomorphic Generalisations

Part 14 of 20. Real-analytic eigenfunctions of the hyperbolic Laplacian Δf = λf on ℍ/SL₂(ℤ). Fourier expansions involve Bessel functions. The Selberg trace formula relates eigenvalues to closed geodesics.

Beyond holomorphicity

So far we have studied holomorphic modular forms. Holomorphicity forces non-negative q-powers, makes Mk finite-dimensional, and ties coefficients to arithmetic.

Hans Maass (1949) found another class. A Maass form is a real-analytic SL₂(ℤ)-invariant function on ℍ, an eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian, decaying in the cusp. Where holomorphic forms live in algebra, Maass forms live in spectral geometry and quantum chaos.

Δ is the Laplace–Beltrami operator of the Poincaré metric, invariant under every isometry of ℍ, in particular under SL₂(ℤ). Holomorphic weight-0 forms are constant by Liouville. Maass forms fill the gap as non-trivial weight-0 automorphic eigenfunctions.

The Laplacian and its spectrum

On compact manifolds the Laplacian has a discrete spectrum. SL₂(ℤ)\ℍ is non-compact but has finite area π/3, so the spectrum has a discrete part (Maass cusp forms) plus a continuous part from Eisenstein series.

The eigenvalues have no closed form. Unlike the holomorphic case (divisor sums, Bernoulli numbers), the Maass spectrum is transcendental and only approximable numerically.

Weyl's law: N(R) ~ R²/12 (Weyl asymptotic on a surface of area π/3). Eigenvalue gaps follow GOE random matrix statistics — "quantum chaos on the modular surface."

The Selberg trace formula

The deepest structural result is the Selberg trace formula (1956), an identity relating two descriptions of SL₂(ℤ)\ℍ:

  • Spectral side: a sum over the Laplacian eigenvalues λj = 1/4 + rj².
  • Geometric side: a sum over conjugacy classes of hyperbolic elements in SL₂(ℤ), which correspond to closed geodesics on the surface.

This is a non-abelian generalisation of Poisson summation. Consequences:

  • Weyl's law: the eigenvalue counting function N(R) ~ R²/12 follows from the identity term on the geometric side.
  • Prime geodesic theorem: closed geodesics of length ≤ L grow as eL/L, analogous to the prime number theorem.
  • Selberg zeta function: an Euler product over closed geodesics whose zeros encode the Maass eigenvalues, paralleling the Riemann zeta function.

The Selberg trace formula prototypes the Arthur–Selberg trace formula on higher-rank groups, the engine of the Langlands programme. The next explainer generalises from SL₂ to arbitrary reductive groups.

Takeaways

  • Maass forms are real-analytic, SL₂(ℤ)-invariant eigenfunctions of the hyperbolic Laplacian Δ = −y²(∂²/∂x² + ∂²/∂y²).
  • The eigenvalue is λ = 1/4 + r² with r ≥ 0 real. The first eigenvalue has r₁ ≈ 9.534.
  • Fourier expansions replace the exponential q-series with Bessel-function expansions: u(x, y) = √y Σ a(n) Kir(2π|n|y) e2πinx.
  • The Selberg trace formula equates a spectral sum (over eigenvalues) with a geometric sum (over closed geodesics), a deep duality between analysis and geometry.
  • Maass forms complete the spectral decomposition of L²(SL₂(ℤ)\ℍ) alongside holomorphic forms and Eisenstein series.
