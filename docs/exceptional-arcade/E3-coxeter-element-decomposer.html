Coxeter Element Decomposer

A starting root, a target root, eight buttons. Pick a sequence of simple reflections that walks one to the other.

Prerequisite. Drills Exceptional Atlas Part 3 sl₂ and Weyl Reflections. If you haven't met the formula and seen that the simple reflections generate the full Weyl group, start there. Go to explainer.

The Weyl group of E8 acts transitively on the 240 roots: starting from any root, some sequence of simple reflections reaches every other. Finding such a sequence is the word problem for the group; finding the shortest one is harder. This game asks you to find any sequence (and rewards you for finding short ones).

The mechanic. A round shows you a starting root α (blue) and a target root β (amber ring), both projected to the Coxeter plane. Click a simple-reflection button s1 … s8 to apply that reflection to your current red position. Keep clicking. When red lands on amber, the round advances. The shorter your word, the better.

What this drills

The Weyl group is the symmetry group of the root system, and every element is a word in the eight simple reflections. After a few rounds in Fluency you start to see what each does to a root: which coordinate it touches, which way it shifts the Coxeter-plane projection, which roots are fixed and which move. By Challenge tier the eight buttons stop feeling abstract — they feel like eight directions of motion on a 240-vertex graph.

Two facts to keep in mind:

  • Each simple reflection is an involution: clicking the same button twice in a row brings you back where you started. The Undo button uses this: it just appends the most recently used reflection again.
  • Some pairs of roots are very far apart in the Cayley graph — the diameter from a simple root to its antipode is 29. Challenge tier never picks distances that high; we cap at 6 so a round stays winnable in 60 seconds.
