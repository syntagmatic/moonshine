Exceptional Arcade

Seven headline exhibits and fifteen fluency drills across the exceptional geometric objects of 8 and 24 dimensions. The exhibits show you what E8 looks like from different angles; the drills give you muscle memory.

The Exceptional Atlas teaches you what E8 and the octonions are. This arcade is the companion workshop. It's split into two wings. The exhibition wing — Track H, the headliners — is seven showcase interactives, one per architectural principle of high-dimensional visualization: ascending the chain A₁ → A₂ → A₃ → D₄ → D₅ → E₆ → E₇ → E₈ step by step, slicing the Gosset polytope, morphing between projections, folding E8 into two golden-ratio 600-cells, tuning Viazovska's auxiliary function, playing Lisi's particle-fusion game, and climbing the sphere-packing density curve from 1D to 24D. The drill wing — Tracks A through E — is fifteen short fluency games that build muscle memory for the operations those exhibits rely on: inner products, reflections, Fano-plane multiplication, shell sorting, code decoding.

You can walk in either door. Headliners are exhibits: no clock, no score, no win condition. Drills are games: a timer, a streak counter, a local high-score table. Every cabinet — exhibit or drill — lists its prerequisite explainer from the narrative series at the bottom. If a mechanic or an exhibit doesn't make sense, start there.

Read these first: the arcade assumes you've worked through the Exceptional Atlas — Parts 8–10 for the octonion and Fano plane games, and Parts 13–15 for the root system, polytope, lattice, and Coxeter games. Each individual game repeats its specific prerequisite at the top of the page.
Current state: 7 exhibits + 15 drills, all playable. Libraries live in lib/arcade.js (Timer, Scoreboard, Streak, drag-and-drop primitives), lib/e8-math.js (E8 roots, Weyl reflections, Coxeter plane, Gosset edges, alternate projections, H4 fold, fusion table, packing densities), and lib/oct-math.js (octonion basis, Fano-plane products, Cayley–Dickson expansion, the 240 octavian integer units).

Companion project to the Exceptional Atlas. Part of the Moonshine explainer project. High scores are stored locally in your browser via localStorage; nothing is transmitted anywhere.
