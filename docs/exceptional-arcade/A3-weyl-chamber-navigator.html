Weyl Chamber Navigator

Drive a starting E8 root to a target root by applying simple reflections. The Weyl group is transitive, so the trip always exists — the only question is how short you can make it.

Prerequisite. Drills Exceptional Atlas Part 3: sl₂ and Weyl Reflections. Go to explainer if you haven't read it — this game assumes you already know that sα(x) = x − ⟨x, α⟩α on E8, and that the eight simple reflections generate the entire Weyl group.
How to play.
  1. The red dot is your current root. The green dot is the target. Both are E8 roots projected to the Coxeter plane.
  2. Click one of the eight buttons to apply that simple reflection to the current root.
  3. Keep applying reflections until the red dot lands on the green dot. There is always a path; the only metric is its length.
  4. Ten challenges per round. The optimal-path counter tells you the theoretical minimum length so you can chase a perfect round.

Per-challenge breakdown

    Arena. All 240 E8 roots are projected to the Coxeter plane as the familiar 8-ring mandala. The red dot is your current root; the green dot is the target. Each reflection button moves the red dot to a new root — sometimes closer, sometimes farther. The optimal-path counter is computed by breadth-first search over the Cayley graph of E8 with the eight simple reflections as generators.

    What this drills

    The Weyl group of E8 has order 696,729,600 and is generated by just eight simple reflections. Every pair of roots can be linked by a sequence of these reflections — that is the meaning of "the Weyl group acts transitively on the root system." This game gives you that fact in your hands: pick any two roots, find a path. After enough rounds you stop searching and start reaching: certain reflections become reflexes, and you start matching the optimal path on harder targets.
