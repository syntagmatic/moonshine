Petrie Polygon Trace

Click the next root in the Coxeter orbit. One step is a 12° rotation in the Coxeter plane; thirty steps close the polygon.

Prerequisite. Drills Exceptional Atlas Part 13 240 Roots, Gosset, Coxeter. Go to explainer if you haven't read it — this arcade assumes you already know that c has order 30 and rotates the plane by 12° per step.

How to play

All 240 roots of E8 are shown in their Coxeter-plane projection: 8 concentric rings of 30 dots each. One root is highlighted in red — that is your current position. Your job is to click the next position in its Petrie orbit: where the red dot will land after applying the Coxeter element once. If you're right, the red dot moves there and a thin red line connects the two positions, slowly building the 30-gon. Get it wrong and the correct target flashes amber, your streak resets, and the current position stays put so you can try again.

The one trick. Applying c is a rotation by 2π/30 = 12° counter-clockwise. Your next target is always on the same ring, exactly 12° further around.

What this drills

After a few rounds you stop computing anything: your eye tracks the 12° step on autopilot, and you feel why the orbit closes after exactly 30 clicks. That reflex — “same ring, 12° counter-clockwise” — is the whole point. You also notice that all 8 rings rotate in lockstep, which is how the Coxeter element manages to act on all 240 roots as a single rigid rotation of the plane.
