Kissing Arrangement

Place unit spheres around a central one without overlap. Six is the ceiling in 2D; twelve is the ceiling in 3D.

Prerequisite. Drills E8 series explainer #01 Lattices and Kissing Numbers. The arcade assumes you already know that two unit spheres just touch when their centers are at distance 2, and that kissing is a local question: how crowded can the immediate neighborhood of one point get?

How to play

A central unit sphere sits at the origin. Every sphere you place must have its center at distance from the origin (so it just touches the central one) and must not overlap any sphere you have already placed. What this drills

After a few rounds you stop computing distances: your eye learns the 60° envelope, and you start placing spheres in patterns — the hexagon in 2D, two staggered triangles plus six equator spheres in 3D. That is exactly the cuboctahedron arrangement of the FCC lattice. The rule the game enforces — "any two placed centers separated by at least 60°" — is the same constraint that produces the kissing-number bounds the explainer derives. In 8D the answer is 240 and you cannot draw it; here the rule is the same, the dimension is just small enough to play with directly.
