Quaternion Rotation Puzzle

Six unit quaternions on the palette; one cube to rotate; one target orientation to reach. The goal is the right answer in the fewest clicks.

Prerequisite. This game drills quaternion composition. Exceptional Atlas Part 8 covers the normed division algebras ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆, and Part 13 shows the same rotation machinery in action on the Gosset polytope. Refresher: quaternions and rotations →

The colored cube on the left starts in some orientation. The ghost cube on the right shows the target. You apply 90° rotations around the three axes by clicking one of the six quaternion buttons; each click composes one unit quaternion onto your current orientation. Reach the target. The fewer clicks, the better the score.

What this drills

After enough rounds you stop reasoning move by move. The starting orientation, the target, and the sequence of buttons start to live in your hands — you reach for X+ before you have finished thinking through it. That reflex is the whole purpose: composing unit quaternions becomes a motor action, the same way a chess player reaches for a knight without computing each square. The only math you have used is , applied at most six times per puzzle.
