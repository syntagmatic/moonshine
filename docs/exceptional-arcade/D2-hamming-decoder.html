D2 · Hamming Decoder

Track D, Game 2. A corrupted 8-bit word arrives. Find the original codeword from the 16 valid words of the extended Hamming [8, 4, 4] code.

Prerequisite. Drills Exceptional Atlas Part 14 Packing, Codes, and the Leech Lattice. Go to explainer.

The extended Hamming code [8, 4, 4] has 16 codewords, each an 8-bit string. Pick any two distinct codewords and at least four bit positions disagree — that’s the minimum distance. So if a transmitter sends a codeword and at most one bit flips along the way, the receiver can always recover it: every other codeword sits at distance 3 or more from the corrupted word, and the original sits at distance 1. Decoding is just “pick the nearest valid codeword.”

Push the corruption to two flips and the answer becomes ambiguous (two codewords may tie for nearest). Push it to three and you can be certain the message is corrupted but you can’t always reconstruct it. This game drills the reflex of doing nearest-neighbor decoding by eye.

Hamming Decoder. The receiver sees an 8-bit word that may have one, two, or three flipped bits. Click the codeword you believe was sent. A correct guess scores a point and your time-to-decide is logged. A wrong guess shows the true codeword, the flipped positions, and the Hamming distance from your guess.

How to read the codewords

Each codeword is built from the four generator rows by XOR. Pick any subset of the four generators, XOR them together, and you get a codeword. Sixteen subsets, sixteen codewords. The all-zero word and the all-one word both belong (the empty subset and the full subset). Every other codeword has weight 4, which is exactly the minimum distance.

Two-step decoder. First scan the corrupted word against the 16 codewords. The one that disagrees in the fewest positions is the most likely original. Second, count the disagreements — the Hamming distance. Distance 1 means “one flip, certain decode.” Distance 2 means “the answer might tie with another codeword.” Distance 3+ means the message is severely corrupted; pick the nearest and accept the risk.

The fluency reflex this game builds: scan, count, click. Don’t do XOR algebra in your head — just count where the bits differ.

The three difficulty tiers

  • Tutorial. Exactly one bit is flipped each round. The reference panel of 16 codewords stays visible. Use this to learn the visual scan: which codeword differs in just one place?
  • Fluency. One or two bits flipped. The reference panel is hidden by default but you can toggle it on. With two flips, occasionally two codewords tie at distance 2; either tied answer counts as correct.
  • Challenge. Up to three flips. Reference panel forced off. Ten-second timer per word. The minimum distance is 4, so one flip is decodable, two might be ambiguous, and three is on the edge of the code’s correcting power. Pick the nearest codeword you can find before the clock runs out.
