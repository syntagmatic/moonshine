Simple Roots and Dynkin Diagrams

Part 3 of 9. Integrality allows only four angles between roots. Record the integers between simple roots in a Cartan matrix and draw the matrix as a small graph, and every simple Lie algebra compresses to one of these graphs.

Part 2 showed that nβ,α = 2(β, α)/(α, α) is an integer for any two roots α, β. This article turns that integrality into a finite combinatorial description of every root system.

Only four angles

Take two linearly independent roots α and β with angle θ between them. Both Cartan integers

are integers, and their product is

Since 0 ≤ cos2θ < 1 for independent roots, the product is an integer in {0, 1, 2, 3}. Each value pins down the geometry:

  • 0: θ = 90°. Both integers are 0 and the lengths are unconstrained.
  • 1: θ = 60° or 120°. Both integers are ±1 and the roots have equal length.
  • 2: θ = 45° or 135°. The integers are ±1 and ±2, and the squared lengths are in ratio 2 : 1.
  • 3: θ = 30° or 150°. The integers are ±1 and ±3, and the squared lengths are in ratio 3 : 1.

When the lengths differ, the larger integer is the one that divides by the shorter root: if |β| > |α| then |nβ,α| = 2|β| |cos θ| / |α| is the bigger of the two. For B2, with a long root β = ε1 − ε2 and a short root α = ε2, nβ,α = 2(−1)/1 = −2 and nα,β = 2(−1)/2 = −1.

Simple roots

Pick a linear functional h on 𝔼 that is nonzero on every root, and call a root positive when h(α) > 0:

A positive root is simple if it is not the sum of two positive roots:

The simple roots form a basis of 𝔼, and every positive root is a combination of them with nonnegative integer coefficients. Different choices of h give different Π, but any two are related by an element of the Weyl group, so nothing derived from Π depends on the choice.

Distinct simple roots satisfy (α, β) ≤ 0. If the inner product were positive, α − β would be a root, and one of α = β + (α − β) or β = α + (β − α) would decompose a simple root. So between simple roots only 90°, 120°, 135° and 150° survive.

The Cartan matrix and the diagram

For simple roots α1, …, αn, the Cartan matrix collects the pairwise integers:

The diagonal is 2 and each off-diagonal entry is 0, −1, −2 or −3. The matrix is symmetric up to root lengths:

so Aij = −2 or −3 exactly when αi is the long root and αj the short one.

Dynkin's diagram draws the same data as a graph: one node per simple root, and AijAji ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} lines between nodes i and j. A double or triple bond carries an arrow from the long root to the short one, recording which entry is larger. With the arrow the diagram determines the Cartan matrix, which determines the Lie algebra. A simple algebra has a connected diagram; a disconnected diagram is a direct sum.

In rank 2 the four possible angles give four root systems. A2 has a single bond, B2 and C2 a double bond, G2 a triple bond. B2 and C2 are the same root system, rotated by 45° and rescaled, which is why the two families are listed separately only from rank 3 on.

The classical families

The four infinite families come from the matrix algebras of Part 1, and their diagrams differ only at one end.

TypeAlgebraDimensionDiagram
An, n ≥ 1sln+1n(n + 2)a chain of n nodes, single bonds
Bn, n ≥ 2so2n+1n(2n + 1)a chain whose last bond is double, arrow pointing to the short end node εn
Cn, n ≥ 3sp2nn(2n + 1)the same chain with the arrow reversed: the end node 2εn is long
Dn, n ≥ 4so2nn(2n − 1)a chain of n − 2 nodes that forks into two at one end

Figure 7. The classical families. The lower bounds on n avoid repeats: at small rank the diagrams coincide, giving B1 = C1 = A1 (so3 ≅ sp2 ≅ sl2), C2 = B2 (sp4 ≅ so5), D3 = A3 (so6 ≅ sl4), and D2 = A1 × A1 (so4 ≅ sl2 ⊕ sl2).

Bn and Cn have the same dimension and the same Weyl group for every n, yet from rank 3 on they are different algebras: the arrow decides which end of the chain carries the short roots. Beyond these four families there are five more diagrams: G2, F4, E6, E7 and E8. Part 4 shows that these nine are the only connected graphs that can be Dynkin diagrams.
