E₇, E₈, and the End of the E-Series

Part 12 of 15. The last two simply-laced branched solutions. (4, 3, 2) gives E₇ with 126 roots; (5, 3, 2) gives E₈ with 240. The fourth hypothetical solution (6, 3, 2) lands at 1/p + 1/q + 1/r = exactly 1, not strictly greater, and the classification stops there. E₈ is the ceiling.

The two remaining Diophantine solutions (4, 3, 2) and (5, 3, 2) give E7 and E8. E7 ⊂ E8 as a sub-root-system, and both share a Jordan-theoretic construction parallel to E6's. We spend most of this explainer on E7; the next three handle E8 in geometric detail: 240 roots and the Coxeter plane, sphere packing and the Leech lattice, and E8 in physics.

The E-series stops where it does because of arithmetic. A hypothetical "E9" with arms (6, 3, 2) gives 1/6 + 1/3 + 1/2 = 1 exactly, the boundary between finite-dimensional positive-definite Cartan matrices and infinite-dimensional positive-semi-definite ones. Lose strict inequality and you fall into affine Kac-Moody algebras.

E₇: the (4, 3, 2) solution

The (4, 3, 2) arm triple gives 1 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 7 nodes, so E7 is rank 7. In Bourbaki labelling the main chain is α1, α3, α4, α5, α6, α7, with α2 branching off α4.

The structural numbers are:

  • Dimension: 133 (= rank 7 + |Δ| 126).
  • Roots: 126, all of the same squared length (E7 is simply-laced).
  • Coxeter number: 18.
  • Weyl group order: 2,903,040.
  • Highest root height: 17.

E7 contains E6 by deleting α7, and E8 contains E7 by deleting α8. The chain E6 ⊂ E7 ⊂ E8 shrinks from 240 → 126 → 72 roots as we remove simple roots.

E₇ and the Freudenthal–Tits construction

Like E6, E7 goes through h3(𝕆). E7 is built from the Freudenthal triple system: pairs (A, B, α, β) with A, B ∈ h3(𝕆) and α, β ∈ ℝ, total 56 dimensions, with a quartic form analogous to h3(𝕆)'s cubic determinant.

E7 preserves the quartic form, with 56-dim fundamental rep. dim E7 = 133, and 133 − 56 = 77 = dim (E6 ⊕ ℝ).

The geometric analogue of the Cayley plane is the Freudenthal variety, a 27-dim projective subvariety of ℙ55 with automorphism group E7. It is not strictly a projective plane (those stop at 𝕆P2) but carries the structural traces.

E₈: the (5, 3, 2) solution and the top of the classification

The (5, 3, 2) triple gives 1 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 8 nodes, so E8 is rank 8.

  • Dimension: 248 (= rank 8 + |Δ| 240).
  • Roots: 240, all of the same squared length.
  • Coxeter number: 30.
  • Weyl group order: 696,729,600 (the largest of any exceptional Weyl group; see Part 3 for the orbit construction).
  • Highest root height: 29, with specific marks (2, 3, 4, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2), the classical "marks of E8."

E8 is the largest exceptional Lie algebra by dimension and Weyl group order. Its root lattice is the densest sphere packing in 8 dimensions (Viazovska 2016).

Figure 1. The E-series terminator. Slide the longest arm length p from 4 to 6, with (q, r) fixed at (3, 2). At p = 4 we get E7 (sum = 13/12 ≈ 1.083). At p = 5 we get E8 (sum = 31/30 ≈ 1.033). At p = 6 the sum is exactly 1: the Cartan matrix becomes positive semi-definite, one of its eigenvalues drops to zero, and the associated "Lie algebra" is no longer finite-dimensional. Instead, (6, 3, 2) corresponds to the affine Kac-Moody algebra Ẽ8, the simplest infinite-dimensional extension of E8.

What lies beyond

E8 is the top of the E-series and the largest exceptional simple Lie algebra. (The classical families An, Bn, Cn, Dn are unbounded in dimension.) Relaxing the Diophantine inequality opens three increasingly exotic regimes:

  • Sum > 1 (strict). Finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras. This is the classical Cartan–Killing classification that we have just completed: An, Bn, Cn, Dn, E6, E7, E8, F4, G2.
  • Sum = 1 (boundary). Affine Kac-Moody algebras. These are infinite-dimensional Lie algebras built from a finite simple Lie algebra by adjoining one extra node to its Dynkin diagram. The extra node makes the resulting Cartan matrix positive semi-definite rather than positive definite, with a 1-dimensional kernel. The resulting diagrams are called the extended Dynkin diagrams Ãn, B̃n, …, Ẽ8, and affine Kac-Moody algebras are the mathematical backbone of 2D conformal field theory, integrable systems, and the sugawara construction in string theory.
  • Sum < 1 (hyperbolic / Lorentzian). Even further out, you can pick arm configurations where the Cartan matrix has one negative eigenvalue (Lorentzian) or more. These are called hyperbolic Kac-Moody algebras, and they are still less well understood than the affine case. Some of them turn up in the moduli spaces of 2D string theory, in the conjectural symmetries of 11-dimensional supergravity, and in monstrous moonshine (via the fake monster Lie algebra, which is a hyperbolic Kac-Moody algebra).

Explainer 10 places the five exceptionals on the Freudenthal magic square.

The E-series terminates because arithmetic terminates. 1/p + 1/q + 1/r > 1 with p ≥ q ≥ r ≥ 2 is a finite problem; with q = 3, r = 2 the largest p is 5. Beyond that the inequality fails. "E8 is the largest exceptional Lie algebra" is arithmetic masquerading as algebra.

Takeaways

  • E7: rank 7, dim 133, 126 roots. Arm triple (4, 3, 2), Coxeter number 18, Weyl group order 2,903,040. Built from the Freudenthal triple system on h3(𝕆), with a 56-dimensional fundamental representation. Sits between E6 and E8 in the sub-root-system chain E6 ⊂ E7 ⊂ E8.
  • E8: rank 8, dim 248, 240 roots. Arm triple (5, 3, 2), Coxeter number 30, Weyl group order 696,729,600. The largest exceptional simple Lie algebra. Its root lattice is the densest 8-dimensional sphere packing (Viazovska 2016). See Part 13–15 for the full geometric treatment.
  • Why no E9. The (6, 3, 2) arm triple gives 1/6 + 1/3 + 1/2 = 1 exactly. The Cartan matrix becomes positive semi-definite, one eigenvalue drops to zero, and the resulting structure is the affine Kac-Moody algebra Ẽ8, infinite-dimensional, outside the Cartan–Killing classification.
  • The E-series ends at E8. E6, E7, E8 are the only exceptional simply-laced Lie algebras, and E8 is the topmost. The classification is now complete: nine families total, with no further additions.
