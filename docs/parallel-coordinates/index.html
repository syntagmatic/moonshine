Parallel Coordinates

Twelve interactive articles on Inselberg's geometry of parallel coordinates: the point-line duality, what crossings, envelopes and axis order show, containment and brushing, four applications, and the regular polytopes of four dimensions.

A 5D point can't be pictured directly. Draw five parallel axes and connect its values with a polyline, and you can see it. Correlations show up as crossing patterns, clusters as bundles, and hyperplanes as patterns of indexed points.

Alfred Inselberg worked out the geometry over several decades. These articles let you drag points, brush axes and rotate polytopes to see it.

The vocabulary

Six colors recur across the series, each tied to a geometric role: blue is always a polyline, red is always a convergence or crossing.

polyline
The image of a point, a broken line that zigzags across every axis.
data lines · individual observations · cluster bundles · outlier signatures
point
A Cartesian dot, vertices, reference positions, indexed-point pivots.
polytope vertices · indexed points · test points in containment figures
crossing
Where polylines intersect, the place correlations and convergences become visible.
dual-image points · convergence patterns · highlighted anomalies · Pareto fronts
brush
The viewer's selection, what gets highlighted when you drag on an axis.
axis brushes · strum brushes · selected subsets · interactive filters
surface
An envelope bounding a family of polylines, hypersurfaces, trade-off curves, workspaces.
tangent envelopes · Pareto fronts · collision envelopes · reachable workspaces · hstar regions
axis
The structural backbone, the parallel rails against which everything else is plotted.
parallel axes · coordinate axes · reordering handles · permutation controls
