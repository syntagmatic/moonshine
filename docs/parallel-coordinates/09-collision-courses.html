Collision Courses

Conflict detection for air traffic was an early use of parallel coordinates. Two aircraft are one point in an 8-dimensional state space, and the states on a collision course fill a region of it.

Part 8 compared fixed profiles against bundles of known cases. Here the points move. An aircraft in 2D airspace has four numbers, two for position and two for velocity. Two aircraft have eight, so the pair's state is one point in 8D, and this part asks where the states that lead to a collision lie. Air traffic conflict detection was one of the early applications Inselberg worked on with parallel coordinates.

The Dangerous Region

Most random trajectory pairs are safe, and the dangerous ones cluster. The condition defines a region in 8D space. In the reduced CPA-time vs. CPA-distance view, dangerous trajectories sit in a band near the bottom.

Brush vertically in the scatter plot (or Tab to it and use the arrow keys) to select dangerous approaches. The corresponding polylines highlight in the parallel coordinates view.

Figure 2. CPA time against CPA distance for 100 random trajectory pairs, with dangerous pairs below the dashed threshold, and the 8D states of all pairs in parallel coordinates.

A controller can't evaluate the CPA formula for every pair, but because the dangerous polylines cluster, a trained eye can learn to pick them out.

Time Evolution

As time advances, one pair of aircraft traces a path through the 4D position space , and each moment is a polyline. As the aircraft close in, the later polylines converge, and after closest approach they spread out again.

Where the Dangerous Pairs Sit

The boundary between safe and dangerous states is a hypersurface in 8D, and like the surfaces in Surfaces You Can't See it has a representation in parallel coordinates. Figure 4 does not draw that representation. It shades the range the dangerous pairs cover on each axis, which is only a bounding box.

Safe polylines can pass through that box, because the CPA distance depends on the differences between A's and B's positions and velocities, not on any single axis. Deciding membership from the picture needs the kind of test in Inside or Outside, applied to the collision surface itself.
