When E₈ Folds into Two 600-Cells

Scrub from E₈'s 240-root Petrie mandala, through a 3-D lift, into two concentric 600-cells inside a single 4-plane — then split one of them into five disjoint 24-cells.

The previous explainer, Exceptional Structures in Parallel, met E₈'s 240 roots as 240 polylines across 8 axes and as a 2-D Coxeter-plane mandala. That view is beautiful but deceptive: it flattens an 8-D object onto a plane and loses the structure that makes E₈ special. What E₈ actually is, down in its bones, is two copies of H₄ glued together by the golden ratio. And H₄ is the symmetry group of the 600-cell.

This explainer makes that claim visible. The scrubber below runs through four stages. Stage 0 is the Petrie mandala you already know. Stage 1 lifts it to a 3-D orthographic view by activating a third eigenvector of the Coxeter element. Stage 2 completes the fold: the 240 roots become two concentric 600-cells at radii in ratio φ, living in the same 4-plane. Stage 3 picks the inner 600-cell and colors it into five disjoint 24-cells — the cosets of the binary tetrahedral group 2T inside the binary icosahedral group 2I.

The icosian fold in one sentence. E₈'s Coxeter element has eigenvalues e2πik/30 for k in the exponent set {1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29}. Group the eight exponents into {1, 11, 19, 29} and {7, 13, 17, 23} — the two sets are images of each other under multiplication by 7 in (ℤ/30)₊. Each set is H₄'s exponents, so each spans a 4-plane on which the Coxeter element acts as H₄'s Coxeter element. Two 4-planes, two 600-cells, one Galois twist.

Stage 0: The Petrie Mandala

The Coxeter element c of E₈ is a product of the 8 simple reflections. It is a 30-fold rotation of R8 with no real eigenvalues; the closest thing to an axis is a 2-plane on which c acts as rotation by 12°. That 2-plane is the Petrie plane. Projecting the 240 roots onto it gives the famous mandala: 8 concentric rings, 30 points each, each ring an orbit of c.

In parallel-coordinate space, the same rotation is less photogenic but more honest. The 240 polylines never overlap; they cycle through their 30-step orbit along 8 axes, permuting the integer and half-integer families separately. Colors in both panels mark these two families: integer roots (blue) are supported on 2 of 8 coordinates; half-integer roots (indigo) touch all 8.

Stage 1: Adding a Third Direction

The Petrie plane is c's slowest-rotating eigenplane — the one where c acts as e2πi/30. There is a second 2-plane where c acts as e22πi/30 — faster rotation, but still orthogonal to the first. Together the two 2-planes span a 4-dimensional subspace of R8. This 4-plane is an H₄-invariant subspace.

Stage 1 activates this structure by adding one coordinate from the second 2-plane as a depth axis. The mandala gains z-extent; the 8 rings pull apart in 3-D. Every ring, seen from the original Petrie vantage, still looks concentric — but now the rings occupy different elevations, tracing out the first hint of the 600-cell's vertex structure.

Stage 2: The Icosian Fold

Stage 2 projects onto the full 4-plane: all four coordinates a1, b1, a2, b2 of H₄'s eigenbasis. The 240 roots now live in R4. In this 4-plane they distribute over two concentric 600-cells:

inner shell: 120 roots at radius √(1 − 1/√5) ≈ 0.7435
outer shell: 120 roots at radius √(1 + 1/√5) ≈ 1.2030
ratio of radii: φ = (1 + √5)/2

The two shells are both 600-cells. In the complementary 4-plane (spanned by the c-eigenplanes with exponents 7 and 13), the same 240 roots split the same way — except the inner/outer labels swap. A root in the inner shell of one 4-plane sits in the outer shell of the other, with norms in Galois-conjugate proportion. This is the icosian fold: E₈ viewed through the pair of embeddings of ℚ(√5) into R.

Visually, the two concentric shells can be hard to tell apart — a stereographic or orthographic projection overlays them. The scrubber separates them by pushing the outer shell radially outward, opening a gap you can see through.

Stage 3: Ten 24-Cells — Five per Shell

The 120 vertices of a 600-cell carry a group structure. Identified with unit quaternions via the icosian ring, they form the binary icosahedral group 2I. Inside 2I sits the binary tetrahedral group 2T of order 24 — whose 24 unit quaternions are precisely the vertices of a 24-cell. Since |2I| / |2T| = 5, every 600-cell is a union of five disjoint 24-cells: the five left cosets of 2T in 2I.

Both shells do this independently. The inner 600-cell splits into five 24-cells (shown in the left pentagon, saturated colors); the outer 600-cell splits into its own five (right pentagon, pastel colors). Ten polytopes in total, 24 vertices each: 10 × 24 = 240 = |E₈|. Every E₈ root lives inside exactly one of these ten 24-cells.

Hover any polytope in the scene to highlight its 24 polylines in the parallel-coordinates panel below — one colored bundle of 24 lifts out of the mass, the other 216 dim away. The scrubber is paused; the toggles at the bottom control which planes the polytopes spin in and whether PC shows vertices or edge midpoints.

Why five, not six or seven. 2T has 24 elements and acts freely on 2I by left multiplication. The quotient 2I / 2T has 5 elements, and those 5 elements are in natural bijection with the 5-element alternating group A₅, whose double cover is 2I itself. The 5-fold factorization is the same 5-fold symmetry you see in a regular dodecahedron or in φ = 2·cos(π/5). A second way to see the same group: 2I is the set of 120 unit rotors in Cl(3), the Clifford algebra of R3, produced by doubling the 12 vertices of the icosahedron as reflection generators (see the PC Clifford figure and the Exceptional Atlas walkthrough).

Merging back to E₈

The Merge to E₈ button runs the decomposition in reverse. The ten 24-cells re-converge into the two 600-cells, the two shells slide back together, and the 4-plane collapses to the Coxeter plane — but every edge keeps its 24-cell color. The end state is the Petrie mandala you saw at stage 0, now traced by 960 colored edges instead of 240 bare points. The same 240 roots; the same 8 rings of 30; but now you can see which 24-cell each edge belongs to, and the reassembly proof is visible as line art.

What Parallel Coordinates Reveals That Petrie Doesn't

In the mandala, the icosian fold is invisible. All 240 roots sit on 8 concentric rings with 30 points each; every ring mixes inner and outer, every ring mixes cosets. The Petrie projection is a rotation-equivariant view: it compresses everything that commutes with c, which happens to compress exactly the structure we want to see.

In parallel coordinates, the structure survives. Each root is an 8-tuple of coordinates, preserved exactly. The scrubber in this explainer is a sequence of recolorings and reorderings of those polylines, not a redrawing: the set of 240 polylines is the same at every stage, only the labels change. That's why the PC panel updates smoothly: it shows the algebra without the loss.

The Other 4-Plane

The Coxeter element has eight complex eigenvalues on R8, grouped by conjugation into four invariant 2-planes. Stage 2's H₄ fold used two of them — the pair at exponents (1, 11). The other pair (7, 13) spans a second, orthogonal, H₄-invariant 4-plane. Same 240 roots, different eigenbasis. What happens?

The answer is the Galois conjugate of stage 2. The 240 roots again split 120 / 120 into an inner and an outer 600-cell, at radii √0.553 and √1.447. But the labels swap: every root that was inner in the (1, 11) 4-plane is outer in the (7, 13) 4-plane, and vice versa. The two shell radii obey |L|² + |R|² = 2 exactly — the two 4-planes are orthogonal complements in R8.

Both folds, one lattice. E8 embeds into the icosian ring I, a subring of the quaternions over Q(φ). The two embeddings Q(φ) ↪ R — one sending φ to its decimal value φ ≈ 1.618, the other to its Galois conjugate 1−φ ≈ −0.618 — give two inequivalent ways to turn the icosian lattice into a lattice in R8. The left disc is one; the right disc is the other. Neither is canonical; together they encode all of E8's arithmetic.
