The Game Is the Math

A ten-part interactive series on combinatorial game theory. Each article is a game you play before you read the theorem that explains it.

These are games with perfect information and no luck, where the player who cannot move loses. Each article starts with a game against the computer. You play, you lose a few times, you notice a pattern, and then the article gives you the mathematics behind the pattern.

The first four parts treat impartial games, where both players have the same moves, and end with the Sprague-Grundy theorem and its failure under misère play. The next four treat partizan games, where Left and Right have different moves: Hackenbush values become fractions, the fractions become Conway's surreal numbers, and hot games like Domineering need temperature. The last two apply the theory to Hex and to Dots and Boxes.

Based on Berlekamp, Conway & Guy, Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays (A K Peters 2001–2004), Conway, On Numbers and Games (A K Peters 1976), and Siegel, Combinatorial Game Theory (AMS 2013). Part of the Moonshine explainer project.
