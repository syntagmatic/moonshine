Force and Attention

Simone Weil on Gravity, Grace, Oppression, and Liberty. Twelve interactive explainers in two acts.

Weil's thought spans two registers — the inner discipline of attention and the political diagnosis of force — that turn out to be the same project at different scales. Act I follows the inner path from gravity through decreation to grace. Act II follows the social path from force through the three monsters (money, mechanization, algebra) to liberty. Both paths converge on the same move: keeping the sign in contact with what it represents.

A note on reading Weil

Posthumous editions. Gravity and Grace and Oppression and Liberty are posthumous collections arranged by editors, not books Weil designed. The series treats them as organized entry points into her larger notebooks and essays, not as finished systematic works.

Anti-Judaism. Weil's relationship to Jewish identity and tradition is a serious reception problem. Her anti-Judaism — expressed in letters to a Catholic priest and elsewhere — is documented, debated, and cannot be set aside. The honest-reading card in explainer 12 addresses this directly.

Against sanctification. Weil's austerity, her simone-as-saint reception, and her influence on both left and right politics have produced readings that flatten her into an icon. These explainers aim for the concepts, not the legend.
