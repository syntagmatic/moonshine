E8 and the Shape of 8 Dimensions

A ten-part interactive tour of E8: 240 vectors in eight dimensions, the Coxeter plane, the lattice, the packing, and what it all touches.

E8 has a reputation for being hard. It isn't. It is a specific finite set of 240 vectors in ℝ8, and you can write them all down on a single page. The vectors come in two families — 112 integer vectors and 128 half-integer vectors — and together they are closed under a rich set of reflections, produce the densest possible sphere packing in 8 dimensions (proven in 2016 by Maryna Viazovska), and appear in places as varied as heterotic string theory, quasicrystals, and the construction of the Leech lattice in 24 dimensions.

This series walks through E8 as a concrete geometric and computational object. By the end, you should be able to generate the 240 roots from memory, apply a Weyl reflection by hand, project onto the Coxeter plane, peel subsystems from the Dynkin diagram, and verify the five-step derivation of the packing density π4/384. We avoid Lie algebra representation theory in order to keep every claim visible and checkable; the final explainer acknowledges that gap and points at where to learn the rep-theoretic side.

The ten explainers are organized into three acts. Act I builds vocabulary and introduces E8. Act II explores its structure — polytope, projections, lattice, and packing. Act III places E8 in the wider landscape of codes, the Leech lattice, and physics.
