From Roots to Optimal Packing

Part 8 of 10. The 240 roots are the shortest vectors of an infinite lattice. The lattice achieves the maximum kissing number in 8D (240, proved 1979) and the maximum packing density (π&sup4;/384, proved by Viazovska in 2016).

A lattice is an infinite set

The E8 root system is the minimum shell of a much larger object — an infinite, periodic, 8-dimensional discrete subgroup of ℝ8 called the E8 lattice. The root system is the set of its shortest nonzero vectors. The rest of the lattice extends outward in shells of increasing squared length.

D₈¹ construction

In words: a vector is in the E8 lattice iff either all 8 of its coordinates are integers or all 8 are half-integers (of the form n + ½ with n ∈ ℤ), and in either case the coordinate sum is an even integer. The integer part recovers the 112 roots of D8 — the (±1, ±1, 0, …, 0) vectors — while the half-integer part recovers the 128 vectors (±½, …, ±½) with an even number of minuses. Together: all 240 roots.

E8 is unimodular. There are only 2 even unimodular lattices in 8 dimensions (E8 and its mirror), only 24 in dimension 24 (the Niemeier lattices, which include the Leech lattice), and none in dimensions 1–7. Unimodularity is what makes the theta series a modular form rather than "almost a modular form," and it forces E8’s fundamental domain to have volume exactly 1.

The lower bound: E8 gives 240

Normalize the 240 roots to unit length by dividing by √2. The resulting unit vectors have pairwise inner products in , all ≤ 1/2, so they form a valid kissing configuration: 240 unit spheres touching a central sphere in ℝ8. The 8D kissing number is therefore at least 240.

Why 8 and 24 and no one else. For most dimensions the Delsarte bound is strictly greater than the best-known lower bound. For dimensions 8 and 24, by a mathematical miracle, the LP bound is exactly tight. This is related to the existence of specific self-dual lattices (E8 and Leech) with extreme combinatorial structure.

Packing density is not kissing number

The kissing question was "how many neighbors can one sphere have?" — a local question about one sphere’s immediate neighborhood. The packing question is "how densely can equal spheres fill all of ℝn?" — a global question about the entire infinite arrangement. For a lattice packing, density = (volume of one ball) / (volume of the lattice’s fundamental domain). E8 settled the local question in 1979; the global question took 37 more years.

Two weeks. The 8D and 24D packing proofs arrived within seven days of each other, 37 years after the kissing proofs in those dimensions. Both used the same modular-form infrastructure. Both closed the Cohn–Elkies gap exactly. For every other dimension the gap is still open.

What we take with us

  • E8 lattice. All integer-coordinate vectors with even sum, plus all half-integer vectors with even sum (the D₈¹ form).
  • Shell structure. 1, 240, 2160, 6720, 17520, … vectors at squared lengths 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, …
  • Theta series. ΘE₈(q) = E4(τ), a weight-4 modular form. Unimodularity (self-dual) is why.
  • Kissing number in 8D = 240 exactly. Lower bound from E8, upper bound from the 1979 Delsarte LP.
  • Packing density of E8 = π&sup4;/384 ≈ 0.2537. Proved optimal by Viazovska (2016) using modular forms.
  • Only dimensions 8 and 24 have tight LP bounds for both kissing and packing.
