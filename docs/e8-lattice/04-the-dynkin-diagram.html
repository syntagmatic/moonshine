The Dynkin Diagram

Part 4 of 10. The 240 roots compress down to 8 "simple" roots, which compress further to a diagram of 8 nodes and 7 edges. Everything about E8 is encoded in that diagram.

E8 has 240 roots. That's a lot to track. The good news is that you don't have to. Every root of E8 is an integer linear combination of just 8 special "simple" roots, and the combinatorial relationships among those 8 simple roots are captured by a picture of 8 dots and 7 lines — the Dynkin diagram. If we tell you the diagram, we've told you E8: the full root system, the Weyl group, every subsystem, and even the way E8 embeds into larger structures can be reconstructed from the diagram.

This explainer builds the diagram from scratch, verifies that every one of the 240 roots decomposes into integer simple-root coefficients, and introduces the height of a root — the sum of those coefficients. We also meet the idea of subsystems: deleting a single node from the diagram and seeing what smaller root system is left. Subsystems get a full treatment in explainer 7, but the diagram is where the deletion move first makes sense.

Simple roots: eight vectors that generate everything

A simple root system is a choice of 8 roots in E8 with two properties: (a) every root is a non-negative integer combination or a non-positive integer combination of the chosen 8, and (b) the 8 are linearly independent. There are many valid choices; we fix one — the standard Bourbaki choice — and use it consistently across the whole series.

α1 is special: it is the only simple root with half-integer coordinates. The other seven are D8-style integer roots. The specific α1 choice is dictated by a constraint we'll see in a moment — the Cartan matrix must have certain integer entries.

Count the −1 entries above the diagonal: there are exactly 7. Each corresponds to a pair of simple roots that are adjacent in the diagram. The diagram is just this adjacency relation drawn as a graph.

The diagram

Figure 3. The E8 Dynkin diagram. Eight nodes for the eight simple roots; seven edges for the seven pairs at 120°. Node α4 is the branch node: it has three neighbors (α2, α3, α5), while every other node has at most two. The three "legs" emanating from α4 have lengths 1, 2, 4 (measured in edges), which is the signature pattern that makes E8 what it is.

The diagram has three legs meeting at α4. Their edge-lengths are 1 (the α2 branch), 2 (the α3–α1 branch), and 4 (the α5–α6–α7–α8 branch). The pattern (1, 2, 4) is what makes this E8 and not some other exceptional root system: (1, 2, 2) would give E7, (1, 2, 1) gives E6, and (1, 2, 0) gives D5.

Why legs of length 1, 2, 4? Because that's the maximum leg length that produces a finite Weyl group. If you extended the long leg to length 5 or more, the resulting group would be infinite — an affine root system or worse. E8 sits on the boundary: it is the largest finite exceptional root system, and extending it in any direction breaks finiteness. This is the sense in which E8 is "the last one."

Every root as a combination

Here is the payoff of having a basis: every one of the 240 roots is a specific integer combination of α1, …, α8. For each root α we can write α = c1α1 + c2α2 + ⋯ + c8α8 with all ci integers, and crucially, the ci are either all non-negative (a positive root) or all non-positive (a negative root). No root mixes positive and negative coefficients.

Height: a single number per root

The height of a root α is the sum of its simple-root coefficients: if α = Σ ciαi, then ht(α) = Σ ci. For simple roots themselves, the height is 1 (exactly one ci is 1, the rest are 0). For the negative simple roots, the height is −1. The positive roots fill the integer heights from 1 through 29, and the negative roots fill −1 through −29 symmetrically.

Height distribution across all 240 roots. Symmetric around 0. Positive roots at heights 1 through 29; negative roots mirror them.

Figure 5. Height distribution of the 240 roots. Each bar counts the number of roots at that height. There are 120 positive roots and 120 negative roots, and no root has height 0. Subsystems by deletion

Because the Dynkin diagram compresses the full root system to 8 nodes, we can ask: what happens if we delete a node? The remaining 7 nodes still define a Dynkin diagram — a smaller one, corresponding to a smaller (rank-7) root system that sits inside E8 as a sub-root-system. Which sub-root-system depends on which node you delete.

The ADE family and E8 as the last member

The Dynkin diagrams that correspond to finite root systems with all single-length roots are classified into three infinite families and two exceptional cases:

  • An (n ≥ 1): a straight chain of n nodes. The root system has n(n+1) roots and its symmetry group is the symmetric group Sn+1.
  • Dn (n ≥ 4): a chain of n−2 nodes with a "fork" at one end (two leaves attached to the penultimate node). These are the Dn lattices we met in explainer 2.
  • E6, E7, E8: the three exceptional diagrams, each with three legs meeting at a single branch node, with leg lengths (1, 2, 2), (1, 2, 3), and (1, 2, 4) respectively.

There is no E9 — extending E8's long leg to length 5 produces an affine root system with infinitely many roots. E8 is the largest finite exceptional of ADE type, and the classification is complete.

What we take with us

  • Simple roots. Eight specific vectors from which every root of E8 can be reconstructed by integer combination.
  • Cartan matrix. The 8×8 record of pairwise inner products of simple roots. Diagonal 2, off-diagonal 0 or −1.
  • Dynkin diagram. The adjacency graph of the Cartan matrix, with 8 nodes and 7 edges, branch node α4, leg lengths (1, 2, 4).
  • Height. The sum of simple-root coefficients of a root. Positive roots have heights 1 through 29, with one root at each extreme.
  • Marks. The coefficients (2, 3, 4, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2) of the highest root. Their sum plus 1 equals the Coxeter number h = 30.
  • Subsystem by deletion. Removing a node from the diagram yields a rank-7 sub-root-system of E8. We will use this operation as a peeling move in explainer 7.

With the Dynkin diagram in hand, we have compressed E8 from "240 vectors in 8 dimensions" to "8 nodes and 7 edges." Everything in Act II will unfold from that picture.
