The Gosset Polytope and Subsystem Peeling

Part 6 of 10. The 240 roots are the vertices of a single 8-dimensional uniform polytope. Two roots share an edge iff their inner product is 1. Deleting a node from the Dynkin diagram peels away a subsystem, revealing E₇, E₆, D₅ nested inside.

So far we have treated E8's 240 roots as a set: 240 vectors you can list. But the set has combinatorial structure. Certain pairs of roots are "neighbors" in a geometrically meaningful sense, those neighbor relations form edges, the edges bound faces, the faces bound cells, and the whole tower of incidences defines a specific uniform polytope in 8 dimensions — the Gosset polytope 421. Discovered by Thorold Gosset in 1900, it is the largest member of a small family of exceptional uniform polytopes and it is the "natural habitat" of the E8 root system. This explainer gives the polytope its edges, projects a 3D shadow you can rotate, and then peels away subsystems one Dynkin node at a time.

Which pairs are neighbors?

Two roots α and β are connected by an edge of the Gosset polytope iff their inner product is exactly 1. Since every root has squared length 2, the angle between neighboring roots is:

Sixty degrees. From any fixed root, the neighbors are exactly those roots at a 60° angle from it. How many such neighbors does a given root have? From the inner-product histogram in explainer 3, we know each root has the same distribution of angles with the others:

With 240 vertices each of degree 56, the total number of edges is:

Six thousand seven hundred and twenty edges. That is a lot of edges for an 8-dimensional object whose vertex set we can write down by hand, and it is one of the reasons the Gosset polytope is visually striking in any projection — even a poor one.

Vertex figure: the neighborhood of any root

The vertex figure of a polytope is the shape you get when you take the edge neighbors of a single vertex and ask what polytope they form. For the Gosset polytope 421, the 56 neighbors of any vertex are themselves the vertex set of a smaller uniform polytope called 321 — which happens to be the root polytope of E7.

The vertex figure gives us a clue: the 56 neighbors of any E8 root form an E7 root system. We can make this concrete by working directly with the Dynkin diagram.

How deletion produces a subsystem

Pick any simple root αi and delete it. The remaining seven simple roots span a 7-dimensional subspace of ℝ8. The roots of E8 that live in that subspace — equivalently, the roots whose αi-coefficient is zero in the simple-root basis — form a new root system, a rank-7 subsystem of E8. Its type depends on which node you deleted.

For example: deleting α8 (the far end of the long leg) leaves a diagram with legs of length (1, 2, 3) meeting at α4. That's the Dynkin diagram of E7, which has 126 roots — which are exactly the 126 roots of E8 whose α8-coefficient is zero.

Iterated peeling: E₈ → E₇ → E₆ → D₅ → A₄

The deletion move can be repeated. Start with E8, delete α8 to get E7. Now E7's Dynkin diagram has a "long leg" of its own; delete its end to get E6. E6 has a shorter long leg; deleting its end gives D5. D5 has a fork; keep peeling to reach A4, and from there the rest of the A-family. The chain of root counts as you peel:

Figure 7. Iterated peeling from E8 down the exceptional chain. Each bar shows the number of roots in that step's root system. The exceptional chain ends at D5; from there the peeling becomes classical (D-family, then A-family).

Why the chain stops. E9 is not a finite root system (it's affine, and has infinitely many roots), so there is no "E9 → E8" peeling step above. On the other end, the chain goes below E6 into D-types because E5 is formally just D5 — the "exceptional" character of the E-family requires a three-legged branch with a long leg, and below dimension 6 the long leg shrinks to zero.

What we take with us

  • Gosset polytope 421. A uniform 8-dimensional polytope whose vertex set is exactly the 240 roots of E8, with 6,720 edges (inner product = 1) and vertex degree 56.
  • Vertex figure. The 56 neighbors of any vertex form an E7 root polytope 321. The local structure of E8 at any root is an E7.
  • Deletion produces a subsystem. Removing any simple root from E8's Dynkin diagram yields a rank-7 sub-root-system. The subsystem's roots are exactly the E8 roots whose coefficient for the deleted simple root is zero.
  • Eight deletions, eight subsystems. Depending on which node you delete, you get E7, D7, A7, A1+A6, A1+A2+A4, A4+A3, D5+A2, or E6+A1.
  • The exceptional chain. Iterated peeling gives E8 (240) → E7 (126) → E6 (72) → D5 (40) → A4 (20), and below that the classical ADE families.
