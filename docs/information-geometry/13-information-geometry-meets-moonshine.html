Information Geometry Meets Moonshine

Part 13 of 15. The pivot. Everything in the series so far has been classical applications of information geometry to classical statistics. The last three parts point at an unclaimed territory, the Moonshine ecosystem of modular forms, Monster-group symmetries, and conformal field theories, where the machinery of Acts I and II is conspicuously missing from the standard toolkit and where it has the potential to say something new.

Everything in the series so far has been about fitting and comparing probability distributions — the core of statistics. We are about to argue that habitat is smaller than it should be.

The objects of Monstrous Moonshine — the connections between the Monster sporadic group, modular forms, and conformal field theories that McKay stumbled onto in 1978 and Borcherds resolved in the 1990s — are fundamentally statistical, but nobody has written down their Fisher metric. The sibling Modular Forms series tells the story end-to-end: Part 16 on McKay, Part 19 on Frenkel-Lepowsky-Meurman, Part 20 on Borcherds and Mathieu/Umbral moonshine. The ecosystem is studied with representation theory, algebraic geometry, and string theory; the statistical toolkit almost never. Part 13 is about that gap.

Not a claim that Moonshine is solved. The conjectural content is that the statistical-manifold reading has not been systematically done. Some correspondences are established, some are reasonable guesses, some are speculation. The distinctions stay visible.

Why the frontier matters

Two possible payoffs.

First: unification. Moonshine's proofs (Borcherds' generalised Kac-Moody algebras, the Frenkel-Lepowsky-Meurman VOA, string-theoretic derivations, lattice constructions) agree on the facts but share no single language. Information geometry could hold the correspondences in one mental model.

Second: new theorems. Why 24 dimensions for the Monster, why the Niemeier lattices sort the way they do for Umbral Moonshine, what the partition functions physically mean. If the Fisher metric on a K3 moduli space is a real invariant of the K3, it should obey relations the algebraic approach misses.

Neither payoff is guaranteed. The series is pointing at a door.
