What would a proof look like?

Three things would need to be done.

  1. Construct the J-invariant inner product explicitly. The natural candidate is the VOA's invariant bilinear form restricted to the Lie-algebra substrate.
  2. Compute the Gibbs distribution and its partition function. Check that agrees with up to normalisation. This is the verification step nobody has done.
  3. Interpret the Monster action thermodynamically. Twisted characters in Monstrous Moonshine should correspond to free energies with twisted boundary conditions.

All three need both VOA and Koszul-Souriau expertise that almost nobody has. The programme is open and worth attempting.

Natural gradient as renormalisation-group flow

If the Koszul-Souriau manifold of the Monster supports the Act I–II machinery, natural gradient descent on it has a physical reading: descending the free energy is RG flow from one CFT to another, with the inverse Fisher matrix as the couplings-and-anomalous-dimensions combination. Zamolodchikov's c-theorem (1986) becomes the monotonicity of KL along natural-gradient trajectories — the Part 4 asymmetry reinterpreted as RG irreversibility.

If correct, this unifies the c-theorem (physics), KL asymmetry (information theory), and RG-as-natural-gradient (differential geometry). Not yet a theorem, but the pieces are in the existing literature.

A closing reflection

Information geometry began in 1945 with Rao's observation that the Fisher information matrix is a Riemannian metric tensor on the space of distributions. It sat as a textbook footnote for forty years until Amari turned it into a discipline, and it has been the quiet framework behind much of machine learning since.

Monstrous Moonshine has been fashionable since McKay noticed and . It has been studied by some of the best pure mathematicians of the twentieth century. The statistical-manifold reading sketched in Parts 13–15 is one corner that does not seem to have been mapped.

This reading does not solve Moonshine; it is suggestive. The partition functions of conformal field theories are probability distributions; their moduli spaces are statistical manifolds; their Weil-Petersson metrics are Fisher metrics; their KL divergences are thermodynamic lengths; their RG flows are natural gradients; their modular invariants may be entropic potentials. If that turns out to be right, a piece of Moonshine is a corollary of Rao's 1945 observation.

You now have the toolkit — Fisher, KL, exponential families, Pythagorean, Bregman, natural gradient, EM, Voronoi — and a sketched dictionary for applying it to the Moonshine ecosystem. The dictionary is incomplete; the problem is open.
