Lie-group thermodynamics and the moment map

The story starts in symplectic mechanics. Given a Lie group acting on a symplectic manifold , the moment map is a smooth function that records how the group action deforms the symplectic form — it is the symplectic-geometric generalisation of the conserved charge associated with each infinitesimal generator of . For a momentum-preserving action, the moment map is conserved along trajectories, and Noether's theorem follows. This is the setting in which Souriau wrote the 1969 book Structure des systèmes dynamiques, and it is the foundation on which Lie-group thermodynamics is built.

The thermodynamic extension, due to Souriau and later developed by Koszul, makes the moment map into the natural parameter of a Gibbs-like distribution. A beautiful reinterpretation that falls out: the Fisher metric, in this language, is the derivative of the mean moment map with respect to geometric temperature — a geometric specific heat. The second derivative of the free energy in ordinary statistical mechanics is the specific heat; the second derivative of the Koszul-Souriau free energy, which is the Fisher information matrix, plays exactly the same role in Lie-group thermodynamics. If you have ever wondered why the Fisher matrix feels thermodynamic, this is the reason: in the Souriau formulation, it literally is a specific heat.

Everything is Legendre-dual, dually flat, and Pythagorean. The Lie algebra of the Monster

Now the speculative step. The Monster group has an associated Lie algebra — infinite-dimensional, technically a generalised Kac-Moody or Borcherds algebra — through its action on the Moonshine module . The construction of that module and the proof that the algebra underlies Moonshine live in the sibling Modular Forms series; for our purposes all that matters is that the algebra is well-defined and is the substrate over which we are about to do Koszul-Souriau thermodynamics.

If you imagine doing Koszul-Souriau thermodynamics with as the substrate, the natural parameter of the exponential family is an element of and the free energy is a function on that algebra. The central claim of Part 15 — and we are about to label it explicitly as speculative — is that this free energy is the -function of classical number theory, restricted to appropriate slices of , and that the Legendre dual plays the role of the entropy of the Monster's quantum states.

A cleaner way to state the claim: there is a J-invariant positive-definite inner product on — an inner product respected by the Monster's action — and with respect to that inner product, the Koszul-Souriau free energy of the associated Gibbs distribution is exactly . The Legendre transform of this free energy gives the dual potential, which can be interpreted as the differential entropy of the Moonshine module's thermal state. Monstrous Moonshine's numerical coincidences are then statements about the moments of that thermal state, and the -function's modular symmetries become statements about the Legendre invariances of the free energy.

Where the rigor runs out. The identification of the Koszul-Souriau free energy on with the -function is, as far as we know, a conjecture rather than a theorem. Pieces of the picture exist in the Souriau-Marle 2014 treatise on Lie-group thermodynamics and in Barbaresco's work on the Koszul geometry of exponential families, but there is no paper we can cite that does the calculation end-to-end for the Monster-Lie-algebra case. The correspondence is plausible — everything around the claim type-checks — but it has not been verified. Parts 13 and 14 made concrete claims you could in principle redo with your own hands; Part 15 is sketching a research programme rather than reporting a result.

What would a proof look like?

If the conjecture is right — if really is the Koszul-Souriau free energy on the Monster Lie algebra — what would a proof look like? Three things would need to be done.

  1. Construct the J-invariant inner product explicitly. For an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra, "inner product" needs to be specified carefully. The natural candidate is the one induced by the VOA's invariant bilinear form on its graded components, restricted to the Lie-algebra substrate. Whether this specific inner product gives the Koszul-Souriau free energy identified with is a calculation that can in principle be done — but has not been, as far as we know.
  2. Compute the Gibbs distribution and its partition function. With the inner product in hand, write down , compute the partition function , and check that agrees with up to a suitable normalisation. This is the step that would actually verify the central claim, and it is the step that the existing literature does not, to our knowledge, perform.
  3. Interpret the Monster action thermodynamically. The Monster acts on as an automorphism group; in the thermodynamic reading, it should act on the Gibbs distribution as a symmetry of the free energy — and the twisted characters of Monstrous Moonshine should correspond to free energies computed with twisted boundary conditions. This is where the full Moonshine dictionary would be derived from the Koszul-Souriau framework.

None of these is easy, and all three require expertise in both VOAs and Koszul-Souriau geometry that almost nobody currently has. The research programme is open, and (we believe) genuinely worth attempting.

Natural gradient as renormalisation-group flow

One more piece of the conjectural picture that deserves a paragraph, because it ties back to Part 10. If the Koszul-Souriau statistical manifold of the Monster supports all the machinery of Acts I and II, then natural gradient descent on it has a physical interpretation. Natural gradient descent from one free-energy value to a lower one is renormalisation-group flow from one CFT to another; the inverse Fisher matrix is exactly the combination of couplings and anomalous dimensions that governs how the CFT evolves as you change the energy scale. Zamolodchikov's c-theorem (1986), which says that a certain function of the CFT is monotone under RG flow, becomes a statement about the monotonicity of KL divergence along natural-gradient trajectories — the same asymmetry of KL that we met in Part 4, now reinterpreted as the irreversibility of information loss along the RG.

If this reinterpretation is correct, it unifies three apparently-distinct objects: the c-theorem (physics), the KL asymmetry (information theory), and the RG flow as natural gradient (differential geometry). The unification is not yet a theorem, but the pieces are all present in the existing literature and the gluing work is mostly about keeping the bookkeeping straight.

A closing reflection

Information geometry exists because C. R. Rao noticed, in 1945, that the Fisher information matrix is a Riemannian metric tensor on the space of distributions. For forty years it sat as a footnote in statistics textbooks, and then Amari turned it into a fully-fledged discipline. For another forty years it has been the quiet framework behind half of machine learning, from maximum likelihood through natural gradient to variational autoencoders. It has never been in fashion, and it has always been the right answer to the questions it addresses.

Monstrous Moonshine, by contrast, has been fashionable since the moment McKay noticed and . It has been studied by some of the best pure mathematicians of the twentieth century and it has produced one Fields Medal and countless theorems. Everything about it has been mapped and catalogued, except — apparently — the statistical-manifold reading that Part 13 through Part 15 of this series point at.

We do not claim that this reading solves Moonshine, or proves Monstrous Moonshine, or does anything a pure mathematician would recognise as a theorem. We claim that it is suggestive. The partition functions of conformal field theories are probability distributions; their moduli spaces are statistical manifolds; their Weil-Petersson metrics are Fisher metrics; their KL divergences are thermodynamic lengths; their RG flows are natural gradients; their modular invariants may be, in a precise sense that has not yet been pinned down, entropic potentials. If this turns out to be right, a large piece of Moonshine is a corollary of Rao's 1945 observation that the Fisher information matrix is a Riemannian metric tensor.

That is where the series ends. You now have the full information-geometric toolkit — Fisher, KL, exponential families, Pythagorean, Bregman, natural gradient, EM, Voronoi — and a sketched dictionary for applying it to the Moonshine ecosystem. The dictionary is incomplete. The frontier is real. The problem is open.
