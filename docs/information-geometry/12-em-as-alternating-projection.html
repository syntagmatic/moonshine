EM as Alternating Projection

Part 12 of 15. The Expectation-Maximization algorithm is a geometric thing in disguise. Its E-step is an m-projection, its M-step is an e-projection, and its monotone convergence is a one-line consequence of the Pythagorean theorem we proved in Part 8.

EM (Dempster-Laird-Rubin, 1977) fits latent-variable models — Gaussian mixtures, HMMs, topic models, factor analysis, probabilistic PCA — by alternating an E-step (soft assignments) with an M-step (weighted likelihood update). It is guaranteed to increase the log-likelihood at every iteration. The textbook proof uses Jensen's inequality.

Amari (1990s) showed the E-step is an m-projection and the M-step is an e-projection. Alternating projections trace a zig-zag between two dually flat submanifolds, and the Pythagorean theorem makes monotone convergence a one-line corollary.

  2. The data submanifold : the set of joint distributions whose marginal over matches the empirical data distribution . This submanifold is m-flat, it is the set of joints whose marginal is pinned to a given point, and it is closed under m-geodesics (mixtures).

Monotone convergence, geometrically

Each E-step reduces the KL from model to data submanifold; each M-step reduces the KL from data-submanifold point to model family. Total KL between and can only decrease, and by the Pythagorean theorem the log-likelihood can only increase.

Watching it work

The first step moves components decisively; subsequent steps shrink as the log-likelihood curve flattens. After ten or so iterations you are at the MLE.

The zigzag path in parameter space is the alternating-projection trajectory. EM is a local optimiser — the Pythagorean theorem gives monotone convergence, not global.

What EM is not

The picture covers exponential-family mixtures with complete-data likelihoods in exponential form: Gaussian, Bernoulli, Poisson, multinomial mixtures (LDA's core step), HMMs with exponential-family emissions. Stochastic EM, Variational EM, and Generalised EM relax different parts and get weaker guarantees.

The model family must be dually flat — automatic for exponential families. Part 9's Bregman framework generalises this to "flat with respect to the chosen Bregman generator," giving Bregman EM variants used in signal processing.

Variational inference, belief propagation, mean-field, Gibbs sampling, and convex clustering are also (single or iterated) projection schemes.
