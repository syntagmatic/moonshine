Fisher as Weil-Petersson on Moduli Spaces

Part 14 of 15. The first concrete Moonshine-frontier connection. On the moduli space of instantons in the CP¹ nonlinear sigma model, the Fisher information metric is literally three-dimensional hyperbolic space — which is also the Weil-Petersson metric on the moduli space, which is also Euclidean anti-de Sitter space AdS₃. Three derivations, one geometry.

Part 13 promised to make one row of the correspondence table rigorous. This is that row. The claim: if you compute the Fisher information metric on the moduli space of instanton configurations in the CP¹ nonlinear sigma model, treating the normalised field profile as a parametric family of probability distributions, the answer is a three-dimensional hyperbolic metric. That same metric, derived through algebraic geometry rather than information theory, is the Weil-Petersson metric on the moduli space. And the same metric, derived through string theory, is the Euclidean anti-de Sitter space that shows up in the AdS/CFT correspondence. Three completely different routes, one geometric answer.

This three-way coincidence is the cleanest piece of evidence that information geometry has a natural home inside the Moonshine ecosystem. Whatever the "right" way to compute the moduli-space metric is, the answers agree, and the information-geometric derivation is arguably the most elementary of the three. If you accept that partition functions are probability distributions (Part 13), the rest of the calculation is a Fisher-matrix exercise of the kind we did in Part 2. You can do it without string theory and without algebraic geometry.

Pedagogical moral: hyperbolic geometry keeps appearing for one reason. Whenever you have a probability distribution parameterised by a scale factor, the Fisher metric includes a term, because shifting the scale by a fractional amount looks like a "unit" statistical move. That is the defining feature of the hyperbolic half-space metric, and any family with a scale parameter is secretly hyperbolic. The Gaussian family, the CP¹ instanton family, the multivariate Student-t, the inverse-gamma, the family of wavelet scale-space representations — all hyperbolic, for the same reason. The Fisher metric is the common thread.

The Weil-Petersson connection

The Weil-Petersson metric is the natural metric on moduli spaces of Riemann surfaces, derived in the 1960s by André Weil and Scott Wolpert and now a standard tool in algebraic geometry and string theory. It is defined by a pairing of holomorphic quadratic differentials and has a clean expression in terms of the Teichmüller deformations of the surface. On the moduli space of instantons in a sigma model, the Weil-Petersson metric has a specific algebraic-geometric construction that we will not reproduce. The key fact for us is that it coincides with the Fisher metric computed above, up to an overall constant.

The coincidence is not accidental. Both metrics measure the same thing: how distinguishable two nearby configurations in the moduli space are, using an inner product on tangent vectors that derives from the physics of the theory. The Weil-Petersson derivation starts from the Polyakov action and ends up with the Weil-Petersson pairing. The Fisher derivation starts from the normalised topological charge density and ends up with the Fisher matrix. The two starting points are different, the two bookkeepings are different, but the final inner product is the same — because both of them are measuring the second-order variation of the log-likelihood of a sample drawn from the field configuration, and that is a metric-independent quantity.

This is information geometry doing the same work that Weil and Wolpert did, but with a completely different conceptual starting point. Whether you find that surprising depends on how committed you were to the idea that Weil-Petersson is a "complex-analytic" construction. Information geometry says it is a statistical construction in disguise, and the instanton case is the cleanest example where both derivations can be carried out in full.

And AdS/CFT

The third coincidence. Maldacena's AdS/CFT correspondence (1997) asserts a duality between string theory on Euclidean anti-de Sitter space in dimensions and a conformal field theory living on the -dimensional boundary. The bulk geometry is a specific hyperbolic space; the boundary theory is a specific CFT. Everything you compute in one picture has a "dictionary" translation to the other picture. For the CP¹ nonlinear sigma model as a boundary theory, the corresponding AdS bulk is — three-dimensional Euclidean anti-de Sitter space, which is the three-dimensional upper half-space with the metric.

So we have three derivations of the same geometric object:

  1. Compute the Fisher metric on the instanton moduli space (information-geometric).
  2. Compute the Weil-Petersson metric on the moduli space (algebraic-geometric).
  3. Identify the bulk dual of the boundary CP¹ model as (string-theoretic).

All three give the same answer. The equivalence between the second and third is standard AdS/CFT lore and predates the information-geometric derivation. The equivalence between the first and the other two is the new content of the Moonshine-frontier pivot: it says that the bulk geometry of AdS₃, which string theorists interpret as a spatial dimension of a higher-dimensional theory, can be computed from the statistical structure of the boundary theory using nothing more exotic than the Fisher information matrix. The "emergence" of a holographic spatial dimension is an information-geometric phenomenon.

How much of this is speculative? The Fisher = Weil-Petersson coincidence for the CP¹ instanton case is a calculation you can do by hand. The metric equation is derivable from the instanton density in closed form; the overall prefactor depends on normalisation conventions that differ across the sigma-model literature, but the shape and the isotropic structure are robust. What is new — and labelled as such — is the suggestion that this calculation generalises to other moduli spaces relevant to Umbral Moonshine (K3 surfaces, Mathieu moonshine, and so on) and that the same information-theoretic derivation yields the Weil-Petersson metric in those cases too. That generalisation has been proposed but not verified in full, and it is the kind of "research frontier" Act III of this series is pointing at rather than proving.
