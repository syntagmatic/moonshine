Hyperbolic geometry keeps appearing because of scale parameters. A family parameterised by a scale gets a term in its Fisher metric, the defining feature of hyperbolic half-space. The Gaussian family, the CP¹ instantons, the Student-t, the inverse-gamma, the wavelet scale-space — all secretly hyperbolic.

The Weil-Petersson connection

The Weil-Petersson metric (Weil and Wolpert, 1960s) is the natural metric on moduli spaces of Riemann surfaces, defined by a pairing of holomorphic quadratic differentials. On instanton moduli, the Weil-Petersson metric coincides with the Fisher metric above up to a constant.

Both measure the same thing: how distinguishable two nearby configurations are. Weil-Petersson starts from the Polyakov action and the differential pairing; Fisher starts from the normalised charge density. Both end at the second-order variation of the log-likelihood, which is a metric-independent quantity.

How speculative is this? The Fisher = Weil-Petersson coincidence for CP¹ instantons is a hand calculation. The form is robust; only the prefactor depends on conventions. What is new is the suggestion that the same derivation generalises to K3, Mathieu moonshine, and other Umbral cases. That generalisation has been proposed but not verified.
