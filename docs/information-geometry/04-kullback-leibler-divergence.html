Kullback-Leibler Divergence

Part 4 of 15. The global companion to the Fisher metric. Non-negative, zero only when two distributions coincide, and (famously) not symmetric. The asymmetry turns out to be a feature, not a bug.

The Fisher metric is infinitesimal. We need a global version that gives a single number for how far apart two distributions are, not just in the limit of small steps. Kullback-Leibler divergence (Kullback and Leibler, 1951) is the natural candidate.

KL is the backbone of information theory, the loss behind maximum likelihood, and what you are minimising when you train a classifier or a VAE. It is also notoriously not a metric: asymmetric, violates the triangle inequality, cares which distribution comes first. Part 5 will show that locally, KL and the Fisher metric are the same object.

The asymmetry encodes a real fact: approximating a sharp thing with a blurry thing is easy; approximating a blurry thing with a sharp thing is hard. If the model is narrower than reality, the tails of reality are under-covered and the divergence is huge. If the model is wider than reality, the model wastes probability mass but the divergence stays moderate.

Mode covering vs mode seeking

The canonical example: approximate a bimodal target with a single Gaussian. The fit is bad either way; the question is how.

Minimising forward KL gives mode covering. Forward KL blows up where the target has mass and the model does not, so the model is forced to place density everywhere the target does — a broad blurry Gaussian straddling both modes.

Minimising reverse KL gives mode seeking. Reverse KL only penalises model mass that the target lacks, so the model collapses onto one mode and ignores the other.

Grey: the bimodal target. Red: the forward-KL fit, broad and straddling. Blue: the two reverse-KL optima, sharp on one mode each.

This is part of why VAEs tend to blur and GANs tend to mode-collapse. The VAE ELBO is forward-KL flavoured at convergence and pushes the decoder toward mode-covering. The non-saturating GAN heuristic behaves like a reverse-KL minimiser and shows up as mode collapse. Architecture matters too, but the KL direction is a real and well-understood ingredient.

KL is not a metric

KL has non-negativity and identity of indiscernibles, but fails symmetry and the triangle inequality. Three Bernoullis with , , and give pairwise KLs that violate . Disqualified as a metric.

The information-geometry term is divergence: a non-negative function vanishing iff the distributions coincide and smooth enough to induce a Riemannian metric via its second-order Taylor expansion (Part 5). KL, squared Hellinger, and Jeffreys' symmetrised KL are all divergences with different geometries.

The asymmetry carries real information that a metric would throw away. Information loss in the physical sense — the arrow of thermodynamic time, the second law — is asymmetric: unmixing cream from coffee is not the reverse of mixing it. Symmetrising KL via Jeffreys or Hellinger is available if you want a metric, but it discards the direction, and for most classical applications the direction is what you want to keep.
