Part 1 of 15. A probability distribution is not a list of numbers. It is a point, and the set of all Gaussians is a two-dimensional curved surface.

Concept Summary

  • Distributions are points. A parameterised statistical family is a manifold. For example, the Gaussian family is exactly the upper half-plane . The parameter values are just local coordinates (like latitude and longitude).
  • Parameters are the wrong ruler. Euclidean distance between parameters does not reflect true statistical distance.
  • Distance is distinguishability. The correct way to measure distance on this manifold is by how easily two distributions can be distinguished from their samples.
