Information Geometry

A fifteen-part interactive tour of the geometry of probability. Treat distributions as points, the Fisher information as a metric tensor, and KL divergence as a global distance, and watch the scaffolding of modern statistics reorganise itself around a single Riemannian picture.

“The space of probability distributions is a Riemannian manifold, and everything follows.”

Most statistics textbooks teach probability as algebra over a flat set of distributions. Information geometry, due mainly to Amari (1985 onward) and building on Rao (1945) and Chentsov (1972), treats that set as a curved manifold with a natural Riemannian metric and a natural divergence, and shows that the usual algorithms of statistics stop being disparate tricks and start being applications of a single framework.

Act I gets you onto the manifold and gives you a working ruler: Fisher metric, Chentsov uniqueness, KL divergence, and the Taylor expansion that glues them together. Act II introduces the dual geometric structure, the α-connections, exponential families, the Pythagorean theorem, natural gradient descent, that makes the manifold computationally useful. Act III pivots away from the usual machine-learning applications toward the unclaimed Moonshine frontier, where the Fisher metric turns out to coincide with the Weil-Petersson metric on moduli spaces of conformal field theories and Monstrous Moonshine becomes, in a precise sense, an optimisation problem in Koszul-Souriau thermodynamic geometry.

A soft prerequisite: you know what a Gaussian is and have seen a likelihood function. We introduce the small amount of differential geometry we need just-in-time. No prior exposure to Riemannian geometry is assumed.

Act I · The Manifold distributions as points, Fisher as ruler
01

Parameterised Distributions as a Manifold

A parameterised family of distributions is a curved surface, not a table. The Gaussian family is a two-dimensional manifold whose points are the densities themselves, and coordinate distance is a terrible way to measure how far apart they are.

Warm-up · Distinguishability
02

The Fisher Information Metric

The score function, its covariance, and the metric tensor that falls out. For Gaussians, the Fisher metric is the Poincaré half-plane of hyperbolic geometry, geodesics are semicircles and straight-line interpolation is a lie.

Fisher · Poincaré half-plane
03

Chentsov's Uniqueness Theorem

Why Fisher and not some other metric? Because it is the only Riemannian metric invariant under Markov morphisms, information-preserving transformations of the data. Euclidean distance on the simplex fails this test immediately.

Uniqueness · Markov invariance
04

Kullback-Leibler Divergence

The global companion to the Fisher metric. Asymmetric on purpose: forward KL gives mode-covering fits, reverse KL gives mode-seeking fits, and the divergence between them is why VAEs blur and GANs collapse.

KL · Forward vs reverse
05

Local KL Equals Fisher

The bridge. Taylor-expand KL around coincident arguments and the second-order term is exactly half the Fisher quadratic form. All of KL's rough edges (asymmetry, triangle failures) live in the third-order term and above.

Taylor expansion · Bregman
Act II · Dual Connections and Dually Flat Spaces exponential families, projections, natural gradients
06

α-Connections and Duality

A Riemannian manifold needs a connection. Information geometry comes with Amari's one-parameter family ∇^(α), and two of them (the e-connection and the m-connection) are dual under the Fisher metric. Dually flat manifolds are the exponential families.

Connections · Duality
07

Exponential Families and Dual Flatness

Natural parameters, expectation parameters, and the Legendre duality that relates them. Gaussians, Bernoullis, Dirichlets, the workhorses of statistics are dually flat, and this is why everything downstream is tractable.

Legendre · Log-partition
08

The Generalised Pythagorean Theorem

The projection theorem that powers maximum likelihood, EM, variational inference, and belief propagation. When an e-geodesic and an m-geodesic meet at a right angle, KL decomposes like squared Euclidean distance.

Pythagoras · MLE as projection
09

Bregman Divergences

Every strictly convex potential induces its own divergence and its own geometry. KL is the Bregman divergence of negative Shannon entropy, squared Euclidean is that of half the norm squared, Itakura-Saito is that of negative log, and all share the local-Fisher structure of Part 5.

Bregman · Mirror descent
10

Natural Gradient Descent

Vanilla SGD is a naive Euclidean descent on a non-Euclidean space. Pre-multiplying by the inverse Fisher matrix gives the gradient the geometry it needs, parameterisation-invariant, second-order-like, and now standard for variational quantum circuits as Quantum Natural Gradient.

NGD · Mirror descent
Act III · Visualisation and the Moonshine Frontier hyperbolic Voronoi, EM, and the unclaimed territory
11

Hyperbolic and Bregman Voronoi Diagrams

Voronoi diagrams on statistical manifolds use the divergence-induced metric, not squared Euclidean. For Gaussians the cells warp into hyperbolic geometry; for categorical distributions they become spherical; for autoregressive data they live in Siegel space.

Voronoi · Bregman clustering
12

The EM Algorithm as Alternating Projection

Dempster-Laird-Rubin's EM algorithm is, geometrically, alternating projection between two flat submanifolds: an m-projection for the E-step and an e-projection for the M-step. The Pythagorean theorem of Part 8 then forces monotone convergence.

EM · e- and m-projections
13

Information Geometry Meets Moonshine

Act III's pivot. The Moonshine ecosystem is populated by partition functions, moduli spaces, relative entropies, and modular invariants, all fundamentally statistical objects. Information geometry has barely touched this territory, and what follows is the case for why it should.

Frontier · Pivot
14

Fisher as Weil-Petersson on Moduli Spaces

The Weil-Petersson metric on the moduli space of a Calabi-Yau or K3 surface (derived via complex algebraic geometry) is mathematically isomorphic to the Fisher metric on the moduli space treated as a statistical manifold of normalised characters. For CP¹ instantons it is AdS₃.

Frontier · Weil-Petersson
15

Koszul-Souriau and the j-Function as Thermodynamic Entropy

The deep integration. The j-function of classical number theory becomes an entropic potential in a Koszul-Souriau dually flat geometry on the Lie algebra of the Monster. Monstrous Moonshine's "magical coincidences" reframed as an optimisation of information entropy bounded by Fisher curvature.
