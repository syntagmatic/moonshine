α-Connections and Duality

Part 6 of 15. A statistical manifold comes with not one "straight line" but three, and the right one depends on what you are trying to compute. Amari's α-connections are a one-parameter family that interpolates between them and exposes a deep duality between exponential and mixture geometries.

A metric alone is not enough to do calculus on a manifold. You also need a connection: a rule for parallel-transporting a vector from one point to a nearby one. Without it, no geodesics, no vector-field derivatives, no notion of "straight."

On a standard Riemannian manifold, the metric determines a unique connection — the Levi-Civita connection — by torsion-freeness and metric preservation. Part 2 implicitly used it for the Fisher geodesic. On statistical manifolds, Levi-Civita is usually the wrong choice. Statistics uses two other connections: the e-connection (exponential) and the m-connection (mixture).

Amari's α-connections (1982) are a one-parameter family with giving Levi-Civita, giving e, and giving m. The e- and m-connections are dual with respect to the Fisher metric, and that duality drives maximum likelihood, EM, variational inference, and belief propagation.

Parallel transport, in pictures

Given a tangent vector at , parallel transport says which vector at a nearby point "is the same." On flat paper, the answer is just translation; on curved surfaces, different reasonable rules give different results. The rule is the connection.

A geodesic is a curve that parallel-transports its own tangent vector. On Earth, geodesics are great circles; on the Poincaré half-plane (Part 2), semicircles.

A statistical manifold has two equally natural coordinate systems: the parameter space (physicist) and the expectation-value space (statistician). Each wants its own geodesics to be "straight lines." The e-connection and m-connection make this true respectively.

"Straight" depends on what you hold fixed. The e-geodesic linearly interpolates the natural parameters — at every point a weighted geometric mean of the endpoint likelihoods. The m-geodesic linearly interpolates the expectation parameters — moment-matching a linear mixture. The Levi-Civita geodesic minimises Fisher arc length and falls between them.

The Gaussian family is e-autoparallel but not m-autoparallel. An e-geodesic between two Gaussians stays Gaussian; an m-geodesic (a literal mixture) leaves the family as soon as you move. Mixture families are dual: m-autoparallel but not e-autoparallel.

Dually flat manifolds

A manifold is dually flat if the curvature tensors of both the e- and m-connections vanish. A flat connection has coordinates that make all Christoffel symbols zero; dually flat means two such coordinate systems, one per connection.

Dually flat manifolds are precisely the exponential families. Natural parameters are the e-flat coordinates; expectation parameters are the m-flat coordinates. The two are related by a Legendre transform (Part 7).

The Gaussian family is the canonical example: dually flat, with exponential coordinates and moment coordinates . Flip to either and the manifold looks flat.

A curvature paradox resolved. The Gaussian family has constant negative Levi-Civita curvature (Part 2), but it is dually flat. Curvature is a property of the connection, not the manifold. The Levi-Civita connection sees a hyperbolic surface; the e- and m-connections see two flat spaces stitched together by Legendre duality. Which you want depends on the algorithm.
