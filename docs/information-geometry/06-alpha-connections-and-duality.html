The Core Concept

A metric alone isn't enough to define geometry; you also need a connection—a rule for what "straight" means when moving between points. Amari's α-connections (1982) are a one-parameter family of connections:

  • α = 0 (Levi-Civita): The standard Riemannian connection that preserves the Fisher metric.
  • α = +1 (e-connection): Geodesics are linear in natural (exponential) parameters.
  • α = -1 (m-connection): Geodesics are linear in expectation (moment) parameters.

The e- and m-connections are dual to each other with respect to the Fisher metric :

A statistical manifold is often dually flat, meaning it has zero curvature under both the e- and m-connections, even if it has negative curvature under the Levi-Civita connection (like the Gaussian family). These two flat coordinate systems are related by Legendre duality.

Concept Summary

  • Multiple Straight Lines: "Straightness" depends on what you hold fixed. You can hold distance fixed (Levi-Civita), natural parameters fixed (e-connection), or moments fixed (m-connection).
  • The Duality: The e- and m-connections are dual. A path that is "straight" in exponential coordinates is curved in mixture coordinates, and vice versa.
  • Dually Flat: Exponential families (like Gaussians) are dually flat. They can be analyzed using standard flat-space tools if you choose the right connection (e or m) instead of the Fisher metric's Levi-Civita connection.
