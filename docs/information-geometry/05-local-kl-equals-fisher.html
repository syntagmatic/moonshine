The Core Concept

The Fisher metric is infinitesimal and symmetric. Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is global and asymmetric. However, a single Taylor expansion proves they are the exact same object at small scales.

Local equivalence: For any smooth parametric family at base point , the KL divergence from to is, to second order in the displacement , exactly half the Fisher quadratic form:

Because the first-order term vanishes, the asymmetry of KL vanishes at second order. Expanding in either direction gives the same quadratic coefficient. Asymmetry is strictly a large-distance phenomenon (a third-order effect).

Concept Summary

  • Local Equivalence: Locally, KL divergence is the Fisher metric. All rough edges of KL (asymmetry, triangle-inequality failure) disappear when you zoom in infinitesimally.
  • Asymmetry as a Large-Distance Effect: The differences between forward KL and reverse KL are third-order effects that only matter when taking large steps across the manifold.
  • Bregman Divergences: KL is a specific example of a broader class called Bregman divergences, all of which reduce to a Fisher-like metric locally.
