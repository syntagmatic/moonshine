de Rham: differential forms on $S^2$

Differential forms give a second construction of cohomology, with the exterior derivative $d$ in place of $\delta$ and integration in place of pairing. On a mesh the two constructions are the same linear algebra, now over $\mathbb{R}$.

A 1-form on a smooth manifold assigns a covector to each tangent space; locally $\omega = P\,dx + Q\,dy + R\,dz$. It is closed when $d\omega = 0$ and exact when $\omega = df$ for a function $f$. The quotient $\ker d / \operatorname{im} d$ is de Rham cohomology, and de Rham's theorem identifies it with singular cohomology with real coefficients through the integration pairing:

The theorem is not proved here. Every figure works over $\mathbb{R}$, not the $\mathbb{Z}/2$ of explainers 02, 03 and 05, on meshes where a 1-form is stored as one real number per oriented edge: its integral along that edge.

On a mesh, d is the coboundary

For a function, $df$ on the edge $i \to j$ is $f(j) - f(i)$. For a 1-form, $d\omega$ on a triangle is the sum of its three edge values around the boundary, which is Stokes' theorem for one triangle. At the last face the cap is the whole sphere and its boundary is empty, so both lines end at 0: summed over a closed surface, $d\omega$ vanishes for any $\omega$. For a closed form the integral can only change where $d\omega \ne 0$, which is why it depends on the loop only through its class. On $S^2$ every loop bounds a cap, so a form that is closed everywhere integrates to zero around every loop.

Is every closed form exact?

To find $f$ with $df = \omega$, set $f = 0$ at a root and integrate $\omega$ outward along a spanning tree. Tree edges then agree with $df$ by construction. Each other edge closes a loop in the tree, and its leftover $\omega(e) - (f(j) - f(i))$ is $\omega$'s integral around that loop.

On the sphere every leftover is zero to rounding, whichever root and form you pick: the closed form was $df$. On the annulus the edges whose loop goes around the hole are off by exactly $2\pi$, and moving the root only moves them. $\omega - df$ is $2\pi$ times an integer cocycle, the same shape as the cut cochain of explainer 01, and explainer 06 runs this integration backwards to turn such a cocycle into a circular coordinate.

Every sphere mesh leaves $H^1 = 0$, from the tetrahedron's 6 edges to the icosphere's 480: the closed forms and the exact forms are the same space. The projective plane and the Klein bottle show why coefficients have to be stated. Over $\mathbb{Z}/2$ both have $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}/2$; over $\mathbb{R}$ both have $H^2 = 0$, because neither is orientable, and $\mathbb{R}P^2$ loses its $H^1$ as well. De Rham cohomology sees the $\mathbb{R}$ answers.

A finer mesh does not prove that the two theories agree; that is the content of de Rham's theorem. The next explainer computes cohomology a third way, by cutting a space into pieces and gluing the answers.
