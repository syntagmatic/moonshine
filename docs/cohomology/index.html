Cohomology

Cohomology attaches algebra to the holes of a space: classes that add, multiply and pair against loops. Six essays build it from small computed examples, from a triangulated annulus to a point cloud.

Homology counts holes. Cohomology gives those holes names you can add, multiply and pair against chains. This series builds the algebra from small examples: a triangulation, a coboundary you can apply by hand, a cup product table you fill in cell by cell. Then come three other ways to reach the same groups: differential forms, gluing along a cover, and persistence on data.

The figures compute every number by linear algebra, over ℤ/2 except in two places: the de Rham essay works with real-valued forms, and the last essay works over ℤ/47 and explains why ℤ/2 is not enough there.
