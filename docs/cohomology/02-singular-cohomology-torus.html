Singular cohomology on a triangulated torus

Over ℤ/2, the cohomology of a finite complex is a matrix computation: transpose the boundary matrix that homology uses, reduce it, and read off H0, H1 and H2 for four closed surfaces.

Essay 01 motivated the quotient; this one computes it. Coefficients are $\mathbb{Z}/2$ unless a sentence says otherwise.

Where every dimension goes

Rank–nullity splits each $C^k$ three ways: coboundaries arriving from below, the classes, and a complement that $\delta_k$ maps one-to-one onto the coboundaries above. So the bars interlock, and the Betti numbers are what is left uncovered.

Why is H² = ℤ/2 without an orientation?

Every edge of a closed surface lies in two triangles, so every coboundary is 1 on an even number of triangles. A single triangle is a cocycle (there are no 3-simplices) that no coboundary reaches. Orientability never enters.

A 2-cochain and its parity. Click a triangle to toggle it, or an edge to add its coboundary (flipping its two triangles). The ℝP² star's boundary is glued antipodally. Parity and the rank test for exactness always agree.

What changes over the integers?

With signs, integer row and column operations reduce $\delta_1$ to its Smith normal form. On ℝP² and the Klein bottle one diagonal entry is 2. Over $\mathbb{Z}$ it is a pivot, so $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}/2$ with no free part. Mod 2 it vanishes: $\delta_1$ loses a unit of rank, and $H^1$ and $H^2$ each gain a dimension. That is the Klein bottle's second $\mathbb{Z}/2$ in $H^1$, which the universal coefficient theorem traces to torsion in $H_1(K;\mathbb{Z})$.

Beyond Betti numbers

Over $\mathbb{Z}/2$ the torus, the Klein bottle and $S^1 \vee S^1 \vee S^2$ all have Betti numbers $(1, 2, 1)$. The cup product of essay 03 tells them apart. Essay 04 gets the same groups over $\mathbb{R}$ from differential forms.
