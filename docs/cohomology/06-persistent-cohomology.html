Persistent cohomology and circular coordinates

The dual barcode runs from death to birth — and each long bar hands you a coordinate. On a sample of points from an annulus, a persistent H1 class is the angle around the central hole, learned from the data.

Act II · Closing essay · cross-reads with The Shape of Data

Persistent homology counts how long each hole lasts as the scale grows; the picture is the barcode you have already met in the TDA series. Persistent cohomology is the dual computation. Instead of reducing the boundary matrix ∂ left-to-right by scale, we reduce the coboundary δ right-to-left. The pivots pair up the same way; the bars are the same length. What changes is the side-effect: cohomology hands you, for each long H1 bar, an explicit 1-cocycle representative, and that cocycle integrates to a map from the data to the circle.

The series began by asking what cohomology is for. The answer, after five essays, is that cohomology names obstructions in a way that composes — and sometimes, hands the name back to you as a coordinate. A persistent H1 class on a noisy annulus is the cleanest small example: the data does not come with an angle attached, but the cohomology can fish one out. This essay closes the series by computing it live.

The dual barcode

Fix a finite point cloud in the plane and build the Rips filtration from essay 01 of TDA: a one-parameter family of simplicial complexes growing in . Each simplex enters at a definite scale (its longest edge). The persistent homology of this filtration is a finite multi-set of bars; the persistent cohomology of the same filtration is the same multi-set, but computed by working with the dualized complex of cochains.

The bars match: a 1-dimensional class born at scale and dying at scale on the homology side corresponds to a 1-cocycle in that is well-defined for . The matching is a theorem (de Silva, Morozov, Vejdemo-Johansson, 2011); the proof we skip. The practical point: the reduction algorithm on δ happens to be faster on many real data sets than the corresponding reduction on ∂. Not always faster — the speedup is data-dependent, and on small dense filtrations the two are within a constant factor — but the result is the same barcode either way.

What is genuinely new: the dual reduction names each bar with a specific cocycle. Pick a long H1 bar; at any scale inside the bar's lifetime the complex has a one-dimensional cohomology in degree 1, and the reduction yields a definite generator — a function on the edges of the Rips complex with δω = 0. That cocycle is the seed for the coordinate.

From a cocycle to an angle

The construction we use is the harmonic representative algorithm of de Silva–Morozov–Vejdemo-Johansson. Three steps:

First, the Z/2 cocycle is lifted to an integer-valued cocycle using the canonical edge orientation i < j: each edge already has a definite direction from its smaller-indexed vertex to its larger, and the Z/2 value 0 or 1 is just promoted to that integer.

What changes if you stay over Z/2. The barcode is well-defined over Z/2, but the harmonic representative algorithm needs the integer lift: the Laplace equation lives over . The lib's circularCoords handles the lift internally using the canonical orientation, so you do not have to think about it; but the integer arithmetic is doing real work, and a pure mod-2 computation would not give you the coordinate.

Six handles on one object

The series has now offered six grips on the same algebraic shadow. Cohomology is, in essay 01, the Euler-class obstruction: the reason there is no nonvanishing vector field on the sphere. In essay 02, the singular cochain construction: the transpose of the boundary, computed by hand on a 7-vertex torus. In essay 03, the cup product ring: the Alexander–Whitney move that turns graded groups into a ring distinguishing from . In essay 04, the de Rham complex: the same construction, but with differential forms instead of cochains, and the same identity doing the load-bearing work. In essay 05, the Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence: cohomology as an algorithm on a cover. And here, the persistent cohomology: a barcode dualized, with cocycle representatives attached, computing coordinates on data.

Six faces of one object. The faces are not redundant; each grips something the others cannot. The Euler obstruction is about why cohomology should exist at all; the singular ring is about what it is on a specific space; the cup product is about how to multiply; de Rham is about differential geometry's same construction; Mayer–Vietoris is about recursion; persistent cohomology is about the inverse problem from samples. The reader who can do all six has the algebra.

Beyond the series. The next ring of constructions: characteristic classes (Stiefel–Whitney, Chern, Pontryagin) — global invariants of vector bundles that take values in cohomology and detect twistings the cup product alone cannot see. Spectral sequences — the bookkeeping that organizes a filtered or fibered cohomology computation into a sequence of pages, each refining the last; the spectral sequence charts diagram is the right orientation map. Sheaf cohomology proper — derived functors of the global-sections functor, where Mayer–Vietoris becomes a Čech-resolution acceleration of a much larger machine. Equivariant and étale and motivic variants, each turning a different feature of the spaces being studied (group actions, arithmetic, mixed characteristic) into algebraic invariants the cup product alone would miss.

The cohomology of the ring on is the same algebra as on , with the dimensions of the generator scaled; the cross-series link is the octonionic projective plane dossier. That is one example of how this small ring keeps recurring in places that look unrelated.

Closing meditation. Homology counts holes. Cohomology gives those holes names you can add, multiply, and pair against chains — and, when the holes come from data, the names are coordinates. The algebra was never the point; the point was always that the algebra composes, and composing names is what gives you a coordinate, an obstruction, a ring, a long exact sequence, a barcode. Six faces, one object. The series ends. The algebra keeps going.
