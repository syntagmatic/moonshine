Persistent cohomology and circular coordinates

Data arrives as points with no triangulation. Persistent cohomology grows a complex from the points, tracks which classes last across scales, and turns a long-lived H1 class into an angle on the data.

Act II · Essay 06

Every complex so far in this series was handed to us. Data arrives as points, and each choice of scale builds a different complex from them, so the question becomes which classes survive across scales. Persistent cohomology answers it as persistent homology does, and adds a cocycle for every surviving class, which can be turned into a map from the data to the circle.

Why the field cannot be ℤ/2

Circular coordinates need a cocycle over the integers: its signed sum around every filled triangle must be exactly zero, or the angle would have to wind around a triangle with no hole in it. A cocycle read as 0s and 1s usually fails. With and the mod 2 condition holds, but over the integers

The fix, from the same paper, is to compute over for an odd prime and lift each coefficient to the integer in with the same residue. That works when the class has a representative with small coefficients, which is usual for data but not guaranteed, so the figures check every triangle.
