Persistent cohomology and circular coordinates

Data arrives as points with no triangulation. Persistent cohomology grows a complex from the points, tracks which classes last across scales, and turns a long-lived H1 class into an angle on the data.

Act II · Essay 06

Every complex in this series so far was handed to us: a seven-vertex torus, a six-vertex projective plane, an icosphere. Data does not come triangulated. It comes as points, and the first job is to build a complex from them. Each choice of scale gives a different complex, so the question changes from "what is the cohomology?" to "which classes survive across a range of scales?" That is persistence. Persistent cohomology adds one thing to persistent homology: every surviving 1-dimensional class comes with a cocycle, and a cocycle with integer values can be turned into a map from the data to the circle.

Take a finite set of points and a scale . The Vietoris–Rips complex puts in a simplex for every set of points that are pairwise within of each other:

Draw a disc of radius around each point. Two discs overlap exactly when their centres are within , so the edges are the overlapping pairs. Triangles need nothing more: three points whose discs overlap in pairs get a filled triangle, whether or not the three discs share a common point. The Čech complex, which asks for a common point, is the more faithful picture of the union of discs. The Rips complex is the one people compute, because it is fixed by its edges alone.

Slide up from zero on the annulus. At first every point is its own component and β0 is the number of points. Edges merge components, and small loops appear and are filled by triangles almost at once, so β1 flickers. Then the complex closes up around the hole, one loop stays open over a long stretch of scales, and eventually, at a scale near the hole's diameter, it fills too. Noise makes short-lived classes and the hole makes a long-lived one. The barcode records every class's birth and death so the two can be told apart.

Lifting to integer coefficients

Essays 02 to 05 worked over , where there are no signs and every nonzero coefficient is 1. For circular coordinates that is not enough. The plan is to find a function on the points whose differences along edges match an integer cocycle , up to a smooth correction, and then read as a point on the circle. For that, must be a cocycle over the integers: its sum around the boundary of every filled triangle must be exactly zero, or the angle would have to wind around a triangle that has no hole in it.

On the noisy annulus the barcode has one long H¹ bar and a scatter of short ones. Select the long bar, set ε anywhere inside it, and the hue runs once around the ring: the coordinate recovers the angle around the hole, up to direction and a rotation, from nothing but pairwise distances. Choose a short bar and the coordinate is nearly constant except around one small loop, which is what a noise class looks like when you try to use it as a coordinate. The figure-eight has two long bars, one per lobe, and each gives a coordinate that winds around its own lobe and barely moves on the other. The two separate circles give two coordinates of the same kind. The filled disk has no long H¹ bar at all.
