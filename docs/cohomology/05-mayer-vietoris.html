Mayer–Vietoris as a computation engine

Cut a space into two pieces, compute the cohomology of the pieces and of their overlap, and a long exact sequence assembles the whole.

Essays 02 to 04 computed H* of a whole complex at once. Mayer–Vietoris works from pieces: cover X by two subcomplexes U and V, compute H* of each and of the overlap U ∩ V, and a long exact sequence joins the three answers. Every group, map and rank on this page is computed over ℤ/2 by row reduction on the complex drawn.

The long exact sequence

The map π* restricts a cocycle on X to each piece. The map d = iU* − iV* compares two cocycles on the overlap (over ℤ/2 the minus is a plus). The connecting map δ* climbs one degree:

Extend a cocycle η on the overlap to any cochain ηU on U. On the overlap its coboundary equals δη = 0, so it extends by zero to a cocycle on X. Exactness (image equals kernel) means the ranks into and out of each group add up to its dimension, which gives each Betti number of X from the pieces and the maps d alone:

In the default cover, U is rows 0 to 2 of the grid and V is rows 2, 3 and 0: two annular bands meeting in two disjoint circles. The pieces only have to cover, and that can fail. On the Klein bottle, bands sideways leaves edges with one end only in U and the other only in V, because the flipped gluing puts column 1 next to column 3. Those edges lie in neither piece, so U ∪ V is a smaller complex than X. The figure then runs the sequence on U ∪ V, where it is still exact, and reports its Betti numbers (1, 3, 0) against the Klein bottle's (1, 2, 1).

Where does a new loop come from?

In degree 0 everything is components. H0 of a piece has one generator per component, and d0 records which component of U and which of V contain each component of the overlap. Draw a node for each component of U or V and an edge for each component of U ∩ V. Then d0 is the incidence matrix of that graph, and its cokernel, which δ* carries into H1(X), has one dimension per independent cycle.

Figure 2. The degree-0 end of Figure 1's current cover. Left: components of U and V, one edge per overlap component, with a spanning forest in violet and each edge that closes a cycle in red. Right: d0, a row per overlap component, reduced over ℤ/2. Repaint Figure 1 to change it.

For the two annuli the graph is two nodes joined by two edges, one cycle. That cycle is the class in H1(T2) that counts, mod 2, how often a loop crosses one of the two overlap circles. The star of a vertex meets the rest of the torus in one hexagon, a single edge, and gets nothing in H1 this way.

Same groups, different bookkeeping

The formula splits each Betti number of X into a part carried in from the overlap by δ* and a part restricted from the pieces. Different covers of one space split it differently.

On the torus the two annuli get one H1 class from each source, while the star of a vertex gets both from the punctured torus. In every preset H2 comes entirely from the overlap, since no piece is a closed surface.

The connecting map by hand

On the octahedron split into hemispheres, U is the north pole n with the four equator vertices, V the south pole with the same four, and U ∩ V is the equator, a 4-cycle with H1 = ℤ/2. Any 1-cochain η on it is a cocycle, and its class is its sum around the loop.

Figure 4. Flip an equator edge to change η, or a spoke to change how η is extended into U. Northern triangles show δηU, red where it is 1; the four outer triangles are the southern cap, where the cocycle is extended by zero.

On a surface every 2-cochain is a cocycle. Each edge lies in two triangles, so a coboundary is 1 on an even number of them, and a 2-cochain's class in H2(S2; ℤ/2) is its triangle count mod 2. Flipping a spoke flips the two northern triangles beside it and keeps that parity, which is why the extension doesn't matter.

More than two pieces

Čech cohomology extends this to covers with any number of pieces and any pattern of overlaps; for two pieces its complex reduces to the Mayer–Vietoris sequence. Every complex so far came already triangulated. Part 6 starts from a cloud of points, where the complex has to be built.
