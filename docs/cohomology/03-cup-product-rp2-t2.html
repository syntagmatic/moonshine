The cup product on ℝP² and T²

Part 3 of 6. Multiplying cocycles makes $H^*$ a ring, and the ring tells apart spaces that Betti numbers cannot.

The torus $T^2$ and the wedge $S^1 \vee S^1 \vee S^2$ both have Betti numbers $(1, 2, 1)$, yet they differ, and multiplying cocycles tells them apart. The Alexander–Whitney formula needs only a total order on the vertices: split each $(p+q)$-simplex at its $p$-th vertex, read $\alpha$ on the front face and $\beta$ on the back, and multiply. All coefficients here are in $\mathbb{Z}/2$, so there are no signs and the product of two values is an AND.

Only the $H^1 \otimes H^1$ cell says more than the Betti numbers. On $\mathbb{RP}^2$ the generator squares to the generator $\gamma$ of $H^2$ while $\alpha^3$ lands in $C^3 = 0$, so the ring is $\mathbb{Z}/2[\alpha]/(\alpha^3)$. On the torus $\alpha\beta = \gamma$. On the wedge the $H^1$ generators live on the circles and every triangle on the sphere, so that cell is all zeros.

Why doesn't the choice of cocycle matter?

The product is well defined on classes only if replacing $\alpha$ by $\alpha + \delta f$ moves $\alpha \smile \beta$ by a coboundary. Since $\delta\beta = 0$, the Leibniz rule gives

On a closed connected surface a 2-cochain is a coboundary exactly when it is 1 on an even number of triangles: $\delta$ of one edge is the two triangles containing it, and $H^2 = \mathbb{Z}/2$. So the class of a product is the parity of its violet count.

Torus or Klein bottle?

The Klein bottle also has Betti numbers $(1, 2, 1)$ over $\mathbb{Z}/2$. Write the $H^1 \otimes H^1$ cell as a matrix $G$, with $G_{ij}$ the coefficient of $\gamma$ in $\alpha_i \smile \alpha_j$. The square of $x = \sum x_i \alpha_i$ is $x^{\top} G x$; the cross terms cancel in pairs, so squaring is linear. On the torus it is zero, on the Klein bottle it is not, so no change of basis matches the two rings.

Each matrix is the cup pairing on $H^1$, computed from the cocycles the library returns (violet for 1). Pick a class to outline the entries of $G$ that add up to its square. Poincaré duality says $G$ has full rank on a closed surface; the wedge's $G$ is zero.

Does the vertex order matter?

It changes the cochain but not the class. Reversing the order swaps front and back faces, so the formula computes $\beta \smile \alpha$. The two cochains differ by a coboundary $\delta c$, found here by solving a linear system. Over $\mathbb{Z}$ the same argument gives $\beta \smile \alpha = (-1)^{pq}\,\alpha \smile \beta$ in cohomology.

The third row is where they differ, and a dot marks each triangle where $\delta c$ is 1 for the 1-cochain $c$ found by Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

The ring of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ has a large relative: the 16-dimensional Cayley plane $\mathbb{OP}^2 = F_4/\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ has $H^*(\mathbb{OP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}[\alpha]/(\alpha^3)$ with $|\alpha| = 8$. Part 4 replaces cochains by differential forms and $\delta$ by the exterior derivative.
