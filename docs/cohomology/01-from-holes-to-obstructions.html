From holes to obstructions: why dualize at all

A cohomology class is a way of evaluating on holes that ignores exact pieces, and a nonzero class is an obstruction. Every number below is computed in the page over $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

What does counting holes leave out?

A continuous tangent vector field on the 2-sphere has to vanish somewhere: the hairy ball theorem. The sphere's Betti numbers, $(1, 0, 1)$, say nothing about vector fields. Homology can prove the theorem through the degree of the antipodal map, but its natural form is an obstruction: the Euler class of the tangent bundle lives in $H^2(S^2; \mathbb{Z})$, evaluates to $2$ on the fundamental class, and would be $0$ if a nowhere-zero field existed. This essay does not prove that. It builds the language on complexes small enough to see.

Cochains, and δ as the transpose of ∂

Over $\mathbb{Z}/2$ a $k$-chain is a set of $k$-simplices. A $k$-cochain is a function on them,

and the two meet in the Kronecker pairing, which counts the simplices where both are $1$ and keeps the parity:

The coboundary $\delta$ is fixed by one rule, Stokes' theorem in combinatorial form. It forces the matrix of $\delta_k$ to be the transpose of the matrix of $\partial_{k+1}$, so $(\delta\alpha)(\sigma)$ adds up $\alpha$ over the faces of $\sigma$.

Pairing on the annulus

COH.tri.annulus(8, 16) has $24$ vertices, $48$ edges and $24$ triangles, and $H_1(\text{annulus}; \mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{Z}/2$. The inner loop $\gamma_\text{in}$ and outer loop $\gamma_\text{out}$ together bound the strip, so any cocycle pairs the same way with both. The pairing is $1$ for the generator and stays $1$ through any number of $\delta f$ steps. Over a field the pairing between $H^1$ and $H_1$ is perfect, and the loop around the hole generates $H_1$.

Where does integrating a cocycle get stuck?

If $\alpha = \delta f$, you can recover $f$ by walking: fix $f = 0$ at one vertex and cross edges setting $f(v) = f(u) + \alpha(uv)$. A spanning tree reaches every vertex exactly once, so the tree edges never complain. Each left-over edge closes a loop through the tree, and it agrees with $\delta f$ exactly when $\alpha$ pairs to $0$ with that loop. A nonzero class is what stops $\alpha$ from being a coboundary.

How many cocycles are there, and how many classes?

Rank-nullity splits each cochain space into the part $\delta$ kills and a part it carries one-to-one onto $\mathrm{im}\,\delta$ in the next degree. What is killed but not hit is the cohomology.

Figure 4. Each bar is a cochain space over $\mathbb{Z}/2$, its length its dimension, split by ranks computed with Gaussian elimination on the matrices of $\delta_0$ and $\delta_1$. Grey is carried forward by $\delta$, the ribbon lands it on the red $\mathrm{im}\,\delta$ below, and the colored remainder is $H^k$. Refining the annulus grows every bar and leaves $H^1$ at $1$. Coning off the hole with one new vertex makes $\gamma_\text{in}$ a boundary, and $H^1$ drops to $0$.
