Simulating on a Lattice

Fifteen explainers on the twin mathematical pillars underneath every physical simulation that runs on a finite computer: a lattice that discretises space, and a group that encodes the symmetries the lattice preserves or breaks.

A simulation always begins with a choice of stage. The stage cannot be the continuum ℝn, because a finite machine cannot store an uncountable set of points. So the continuum gets replaced by a lattice, a discrete subgroup of ℝn spanned by integer combinations of a basis. Every cell of a cellular automaton, every voxel of a molecular dynamics box, every link of a lattice gauge theory, every pixel of a convolutional network, they are all lattice sites.

The second pillar is harder to see but more consequential. Every law of physics that the simulation is trying to approximate has a symmetry group: translation gives momentum conservation, rotation gives angular momentum, time-translation gives energy, gauge invariance gives charge. When space is discretised, the continuous symmetry group gets replaced by a finite point group, and Noether's theorem survives only if the discretisation was chosen carefully. A square lattice kills isotropy. A forward-Euler integrator kills energy conservation. The FHP hexagonal lattice keeps the right tensor symmetries and recovers Navier–Stokes; HPP does not.

The series walks across simulation paradigms, crystallography, molecular dynamics, cellular automata, lattice Boltzmann, the Ising model, multi-agent networks, ecology, traffic, spatial partitioning, additive manufacturing, equivariant neural networks, and shows, in each case, which group the discretisation honours and what goes wrong when it doesn't.

Two pillars, one substrate

Lattice
A free abelian group of rank n spanning ℝn: all integer combinations of a basis. In 2D: five Bravais types. In 3D: fourteen. Periodic boundary conditions turn a lattice into a torus ℝn/ℤn. Choice of lattice fixes the achievable rotation symmetries and thus the isotropic tensors the simulation can represent.
Group
A set with an associative product, an identity, and inverses. Noether: every continuous symmetry of an action corresponds to a conserved current. Discretise carefully and the discrete analogue survives, symplectic integrators conserve a shadow Hamiltonian exactly. Discretise carelessly and energy drifts, isotropy dies, gauge charge leaks.
Where they meet
The point group of a lattice is the subgroup of the lattice's orthogonal group that maps sites to sites. Cellular automata inherit it as the symmetry of their rule. Lattice Boltzmann uses its representation theory to build discrete velocity sets. Equivariant neural networks hard-code it into their layers.
Where they fight
Spontaneous symmetry breaking: the Ising model's Z₂ above Tc, the boid swarm's rotational symmetry collapsing into a single velocity vector, the predator-prey lattice's directed-percolation extinction invisible to the mean-field ODE. Symmetry sets the rules; symmetry breaking produces the interesting structure.
