Lattice Boltzmann & the D2Q9 Stencil

Part 5 of 6. Nine velocities on a square lattice, weighted so the fluid they make looks the same in every direction and in every moving frame, to the order that matters.

Part 4 ended with HPP failing because four axis velocities cannot build an isotropic rank-4 tensor. Lattice Boltzmann keeps the square lattice with real-valued populations fi on nine velocities (McNamara and Zanetti 1988; Qian, d'Humières and Lallemand 1992), weighted so the fluid respects two continuum symmetries: rotations and Galilean boosts.

What do the weights have to satisfy?

The nine velocities form three orbits of the point group D4, so there are three weights, one fixed by normalisation. The rank-4 moment Σwi(ci·n)4 must not depend on the direction n, and must equal the Maxwellian's 3cs4, where cs2 = Σwicix2.

Figure 1. On the purple line the rank-4 moment is isotropic; on the orange curve its average over directions equals 3cs4. The polar plot is the moment over 3cs4; the Maxwellian is the dashed circle. Drag the point or use arrow keys.

The axes-only set matches along the axes and has half the moment on the diagonals. The eight moving vectors are isotropic at r = 1/4 but too small; the rest weight supplies the size, and the two curves cross only at 4/9, 1/9, 1/36.

Collide, then stream

Each step relaxes every population toward a local equilibrium with one time τ (the BGK collision), then shifts it one cell along its velocity. A Chapman–Enskog expansion turns this into incompressible Navier–Stokes with the viscosity below.

Figure 2 sums the equilibrium's moments. Through rank 2 they match the Maxwellian. At rank 3 they cannot, because cx3 = cx when every component is −1, 0 or 1.

The eight-moving set carries the wave at 5/9 of U, the radius of its circle in Figure 1. The axes-only set has no channel that moves y-momentum along x, so the wave stays put. D2Q9 drifts at exactly U, but its viscosity in the moving frame falls as U grows: the cubic error of Figure 2, and the reason runs are kept at low Mach number.

Flow past a cylinder

Walls are bounce-back: a population entering a solid site returns the way it came, so the wall velocity is zero. Figure 5 runs a 300 × 90 channel with a cylinder of diameter 22. Everything is mirror-symmetric about the centreline except a seeded random perturbation of 1% of U in the initial velocity.

How does the mirror symmetry break?

Figure 6 tracks the wake's mirror defect, the RMS difference between the flow and its reflection through the centreline, divided by U. It grows exponentially, then saturates, a Hopf bifurcation seen from inside: a complex pair of eigenvalues of the symmetric wake has crossed into the unstable half-plane.

No snapshot of the street is symmetric, but reflection plus a shift of half a period maps it onto itself, so the Z2 symmetry survives as a space-time symmetry. A new seed changes only the phase of the street.

Where D2Q9 stops

Practitioners keep the Mach number U/cs near 0.1; the slider here reaches 0.21. Compressible and thermal flows need larger velocity sets such as D2Q17 or D2Q37. Part 6 turns to the Ising model, where a Z2 symmetry breaks with no flow at all.
