The Grammar of a Dead Show

Dead shows followed an unwritten order: a first set of shorter songs, a long second set with Drums and Space, a short encore. Opener and closer counts show how firmly songs held each slot.

The Touring Life mapped where the band played. This part looks at what one night contained. A Dead show followed conventions that nobody wrote down but fans could recite. Set 1 was the warm-up, with shorter songs. Set 2 held the long improvisations, with Drums and Space in the middle. The encore was short, and often it was "U.S. Blues." The question here is how firmly each slot belonged to a handful of songs, and when that order settled.

The Shape of a Show

Averaged across the database, Set 1 ran 9.1 setlist entries, Set 2 ran 10.1 and the encore 1.7. Somewhere in Set 2 the band dissolved into Drums (a percussion duet between Mickey Hart and Bill Kreutzmann) and then Space (electronic, ambient improvisation), and both count as entries in that 10.1. After Space the band reconvened for the rest of Set 2, then closed; the encore followed, usually one or two songs.

Shows before 1975 were looser (see below), though two sets and an encore were already the rule. After the 1975 hiatus the middle of Set 2 settled in. Drums arrived first: it appears at 34 of the 41 shows in 1976. Space took longer, turning up at 44 of 81 shows in 1978, 57 of 75 in 1979 and 79 of 87 in 1980. From 1980 on, the pair was in essentially every show.

Openers

Set 1 usually opened with something uptempo and familiar. "Jack Straw" led with 191 opens, "Bertha" was second at 157, and a Chuck Berry cover, "Promised Land," was third.

Set 2 openers led into jams. "China Cat Sunflower" led with 198 opens, almost always flowing into "I Know You Rider" (Deadheads wrote it "China > Rider"). "Scarlet Begonias" (179 times) typically segued into "Fire on the Mountain."

Figure 1: Opener Frequency. The top 15 opening songs for Set 1, Set 2 and the encore, colored by song type. "Encore" here is the third set the database records for a show. For most shows that is the encore, but a few dozen early shows had a true third set, and some shows list four sets.

"U.S. Blues" started the encore 265 times. Only one song held a single slot more often: "Sugar Magnolia," which closed Set 2 359 times.

Closers

Set 1 closers had firm endings. "Deal" led with 230, a Jerry Garcia song whose guitar solo could stretch or compress to fit the night. "Don't Ease Me In" and "The Music Never Stopped" filled the same role.

Set 2 closers were anthems. "Sugar Magnolia" closed Set 2 359 times, 112 more than the runner-up, and its coda "Sunshine Daydream" made a natural finale. "Not Fade Away" (247 times) did the same job on a Bo Diddley beat. "Turn On Your Lovelight" (201 times) was the early-era equivalent, an R&B workout that could run 30 minutes.

Figure 2: Closer Frequency. Top closers for Set 1, Set 2 and the encore.

Encore openers and closers overlap because most encores were one or two songs, so the same song often both opened and closed it. "U.S. Blues" was usually a one-song encore.

Songs by Slot

The database behind this series holds full setlists, split into sets, with every song in order. The summary used here keeps only the ends of each set: the song that started it and the song that ended it, tallied slot by slot. Because each slot is counted separately, it cannot say which Set 1 opener went with which Set 2 closer on the same night. Side by side, though, the six slot tallies show that most songs belong to one of them.

Each cell counts how often the song held that slot. The source keeps only the top fifteen songs per slot, so an empty cell means the song fell below that slot's fifteenth-place count, which may still be more than zero. "Jack Straw" appears in one column only: it opened Set 1 191 times and never made the top fifteen anywhere else. "Deal" closed Set 1 230 times and Set 2 only 16. "U.S. Blues" appears only in the encore columns. "Sugar Magnolia" closed Set 2 359 times and ended the encore another 37.

A few songs worked several jobs. "Promised Land" opened Set 1 121 times and closed it 134 times. "Playing in the Band" closed Set 1 67 times, opened Set 2 45 times and closed it 34 times. "Casey Jones" shows up in five of the six columns. "One More Saturday Night" closed Set 2 143 times and started or ended the encore more than a hundred times each.

Songs in the same slot could stand in for each other. "Bertha" (157 Set 1 opens) did the job of "Jack Straw," and "Not Fade Away" the job of "Sugar Magnolia."

How the Format Evolved

The two-set show did not exist at the beginning. Acid Tests had no sets at all, and early San Francisco ballroom shows ran three or four sets, or one long unbroken performance. By 1969 two sets appeared regularly, but some nights paired an acoustic set with an electric one and others were a single long set. As the band moved from theaters to arenas in the early 1970s, longer shows with an intermission pushed toward Set 1, break, Set 2, encore. The acoustic sets stopped in 1971, and by the end of that year the two-set shape was the norm.

Mickey Hart's full-time return in 1976, five years after he left, made Drums nightly, and once Space joined it Set 2 had three parts: the exploration before Drums, the percussive and electronic interlude, and the return to songs after Space. By the 1980s the encore was just as fixed.

Songs per Show Over Time

The summary file does not break each year's shows down by set, but it does record how many entries each setlist has. Averaged by year, that count climbs from about 12 per show in 1969 to 26 in 1973 and 1974, the years of the longest shows. After the hiatus it settles near 20, and it drifts down to about 18.5 by 1992-95.

Read the early years with care. Only 32 of the 113 shows in 1966, and 41 of 139 in 1967, have a setlist in the database, and the ones that survive are probably not typical. From 1971 on, coverage is complete or missing a single show.
