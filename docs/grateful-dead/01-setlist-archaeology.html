Setlist Archaeology

How the Grateful Dead's repertoire grew and settled across thirty years and 2,358 shows.

The numbers on this page come from gdshowsdb, an open database of Grateful Dead concerts. It lists 2,358 dated shows between May 1965 and July 1995. Of those, 2,076 have a recorded setlist. The other 282 are known dates with no surviving song list, and all but one of them fall before 1971. From the setlists, the page counts how many times each of the 60 most-played titles appears in each year. A reprise within one show counts as a second entry, which is why Playing in the Band reaches 750.

Two of the 60 titles are not songs in the usual sense. Drums and Space are the percussion and free-form segments at the center of the second set, and they top the list with 1,777 and 1,247 entries.

Six Eras

The band's thirty years fall into six stretches, marked by changes in how often it toured and what a show contained. In the Primal years (1965 to 1969) the Dead played ballrooms and Acid Tests, often built whole sets around a few long jams. After Workingman's Dead and American Beauty in 1970, the Golden Road years (1970 to 1974) filled the setlist with new, tightly written songs. Debts from the Wall of Sound, the band's enormous PA, and plain exhaustion stopped the touring in 1975, a year with just four shows, all in San Francisco. The band came back in 1976. The Arena years (1978 to 1986) settled the show into the two-set format with Drums and Space in the middle of the second. In the Dark and the single "Touch of Grey" made 1987 to 1990 the band's most popular stretch. The Final Years ran until Jerry Garcia's health gave out: the last show was at Soldier Field in Chicago on July 9, 1995, and Garcia died a month later.

The era totals are 539 shows in the Primal years, 424 in the Golden Road, 105 across the hiatus and return, 634 in the Arena years, 312 in the Touch of Grey years and 344 in the Final Years. Only 268 of the 539 Primal shows have a setlist, and some of those lists are partial, so every count on this page undercounts the late sixties. The Touring Life charts the shows year by year.

From 1971 on, every show but one has a setlist. How long those setlists were, and how they changed from era to era, is taken up in The Grammar of a Show.

The Full Repertoire

Stacking every year's counts for the 60 staples shows where the repertoire came from. Each layer below is one song, colored by the era in which it was first played.

The stream is widest in 1972, with 1,780 entries from the 60 staples, pinches almost shut in 1975, and holds a steady width from 1978 onward. Most of its thickness comes from songs that were already in the repertoire by 1974: 53 of the 60 debuted by then.

The songbook was built in five years. Of the 60 staples, 35 were first played between 1969 and 1973, and 17 of them debuted in 1971 alone: Playing in the Band, Jack Straw, Bertha, Deal, Sugaree, Wharf Rat, Loser, Brown Eyed Women, Tennessee Jed and Ramble On Rose among them. Uncle John's Band and Casey Jones reached the stage in 1969, a year before their albums came out; Sugar Magnolia, Truckin' and Friend of the Devil followed in 1970. By the end of 1973, 50 of the 60 existed. The later additions are few: Scarlet Begonias, U.S. Blues and Cassidy in 1974, Samson and Delilah in 1976, Estimated Prophet and Terrapin Station in 1977, then one a year in 1978, 1979, 1980 and 1982, the last being Throwing Stones.

Song Lifecycles

Each song had its own shape. Some arrived early and never left; others burned for a few years and became rarities.

Sorted by first appearance, the grid reads as a sequence of cohorts: the 1966 to 1968 originals such as Me and My Uncle, I Know You Rider and The Other One; the 1969 and 1970 songs; the large 1971 wave; and a short tail from 1974 on. Sorted by peak year, 16 of the 60 peak in 1972, the busiest year in the data. Dark Star peaks in 1969 and Good Lovin' in 1970. Only seven titles peak after 1979: Space, Wharf Rat and Althea in 1980, Little Red Rooster and Don't Ease Me In in 1981, Throwing Stones in 1983 and the catch-all Jam in 1993.

Truckin' shows the steady kind. It entered in 1970 and was played at least eight times in every year from 1977 through 1995; the only gap is the hiatus, with one entry in 1975 and none in 1976.

Drums and Space

A drum segment was part of the show long before the Arena years. Drums has 130 entries in 1969 and 123 in 1970. It drops to 52 in 1971 and 8 in 1974, the years when Mickey Hart was away from the band, and climbs back to 36 in 1976 after his return. Space is different. It appears only 14 times before 1978 and then becomes Drums' partner: 74 Drums and 44 Space entries in 1978, and 88 and 84 across 87 shows in 1980. From 1982 until the end, the two counts never differ by more than three in any year. At that point they were fixed slots in the show, and their totals mostly measure how many shows the band played.

Disappearances and Returns

Some songs vanished and came back. Dark Star, the long improvisation of the late sixties, has 70 entries in 1969, declines through the early seventies, and appears only six times between 1975 and 1988. It returned in 1989, and by 1991 the band was playing it 18 times a year.

Cryptical Envelopment was a centerpiece of 1968 to 1970 (73 entries in 1968, 92 in 1969), played once in 1972, revived for eight shows in 1985 and never again. Casey Jones was a staple from 1970 to 1972 at around 70 entries a year, fell to 10 in 1974, and after the hiatus came back only a few times a year from 1977 to 1984; it then disappeared until three performances in 1992 and one in 1993. Turn On Your Lovelight, Pigpen's showcase, has 132 entries in 1969 and 4 in 1972, the year before he died. It was absent until 1981 and became a regular again from 1985, now sung by Bob Weir.

What Sixty Songs Can Show

A common story about the late Dead is that the repertoire narrowed. This data cannot test that. It holds only the 60 titles with the most entries across the whole career, and a list chosen that way favors songs with long careers. Within those 60, the late band was not narrowing: 45 of them were played in 1972, while every year from 1979 to 1995 used between 55 and 59. Whether the full catalog shrank, and whether one-off covers and new songs got rarer, needs every title from every setlist.

What the data does measure is shorter shows. Counting only shows with a setlist, a Golden Road show averaged 21.9 entries and a Final Years show 18.8. Fewer songs per night meant each staple came around a little less often, even as nearly all of them stayed in rotation.
