MA Plot

How to visualize differential expression by separating magnitude of change from the average intensity of the signal.

When comparing gene expression between two conditions, a raw difference is misleading: genes with low expression show high variability and produce large fold changes by chance. The MA plot separates real biological signal from intensity-dependent noise.

The MA plot transforms the data into two components:

\[ M = \log_2(C_1) - \log_2(C_2) \] \[ A = \frac{1}{2}(\log_2(C_1) + \log_2(C_2)) \]

\( M \) (minus) is the log fold change; \( A \) (average) is the mean log intensity. The rotation shows how change behaves across expression levels.

Intensity and variation

In a typical experiment, most genes don't change and cluster around \( M = 0 \). As \( A \) decreases, the spread increases, producing a funnel shape. A given fold change is more significant in a highly expressed gene than in a rarely expressed one.

Figure 1. An MA plot of simulated gene expression. Points are genes; highlighting marks those above a fold-change threshold.

The funnel is a sequencing artifact. Low-intensity measurements are noisier, so visualizing them prevents over-interpreting rare transcripts.

Comparing perspectives

A direct scatter of two conditions also shows differences but obscures outliers. The MA transformation flattens the diagonal, making deviations easier to see.

Figure 2. Direct comparison of control vs. treated expression. The diagonal represents unchanged genes.
