Volcano Plot

Combining effect size and statistical significance to reveal the most influential genes in a biological system.

The volcano plot summarizes a differential expression analysis. The MA plot shows intensity vs. change; the volcano plot adds statistical confidence, so a gene is judged on both effect size and reliability.

Two axes:

  • Horizontal: \(\log_2(\text{fold change})\) — magnitude and direction of change.
  • Vertical: \(-\log_{10}(p\text{-value})\) — higher means more significant.

Most genes cluster at the base; interesting ones rise into the upper corners, hence the volcano shape.

Significance

A candidate must usually pass two filters: a minimum fold change and a maximum p-value. The pair eliminates both noisy low-intensity changes and statistically significant but trivially small differences.

Figure 1. Volcano plot of 800 simulated genes. Upper-right: significantly upregulated; upper-left: significantly downregulated.

High fold change without significance usually means measurement noise. A very low p-value with tiny fold change may be statistically real but biologically irrelevant.

Comparing filters

Each filter alone is insufficient: fold change alone captures noisy outliers; p-value alone may include thousands of genes with negligible expression changes.

Figure 2. Three filtering strategies. The combined approach (right) isolates genes that satisfy both criteria.

Top hits

The most significant genes are usually labeled directly. They become the first candidates for follow-up experiments.
