Dimensionality Reduction

How to compress thousands of genetic measurements into a two dimensional map of cell identity.

Single cell RNA sequencing allows us to measure the activity of thousands of genes in individual cells. A typical experiment might profile tens of thousands of cells, creating a high dimensional dataset where each cell is a point in a 20,000 dimensional space. To make sense of this complexity, we use dimensionality reduction to project these points onto a two dimensional plane.

The goal is to preserve the relationships between cells: those with similar gene expression profiles should remain close together, forming clusters that represent distinct cell types or states.

Algorithms and Interpretations

Different algorithms emphasize different aspects of the data structure. PCA is a linear method that preserves global variance. t-SNE and UMAP are non-linear methods that prioritize local neighborhoods, often resulting in clearer separation between clusters.

Figure 1. Comparison of methods. UMAP is widely preferred for single cell data because it tends to preserve more of the global structure than t-SNE while maintaining distinct local clusters.

No single projection is perfectly accurate. Each method introduces its own distortions. PCA may fail to separate distinct groups that overlap in the primary dimensions of variance, while t-SNE can sometimes create artificial clusters from random noise.

Markers and Identity

Clusters alone do not tell us which cell types are present. Researchers identify clusters by looking for "marker genes" that are uniquely expressed in specific groups. By correlating these markers with known biological functions, we can assign identities to the clusters.

Figure 2. Exploring cell identity. Clicking a cluster reveals genes that are significantly upregulated in those cells compared to the rest of the population.

Stability and Variation

Non-linear dimensionality reduction is stochastic. Starting with a different random seed will produce a different visual layout. While the absolute coordinates change, the relative groupings should remain stable if the signal in the data is real.

seed: 42

Figure 3. Sensitivity to initialization. Repeatedly regenerating the embedding shows that while the clusters may rotate or shift, the membership of each cluster is generally preserved.

A stable cluster is a robust biological finding. If a group of cells only appears under specific algorithmic settings or seeds, it may be an artifact rather than a true biological state.
