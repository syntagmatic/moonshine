Oncoprint

How often is TP53 altered across patients, and what happens to CDKN1A when it is?

Cancer genomics produces many layers per patient. The oncoprint compresses these into a single dense grid. Columns are patients, rows are genes, and colors encode alteration types.

TP53 is the most frequently altered gene in human cancer. It encodes the transcription factor that activates cell-cycle arrest via CDKN1A.

Figure 1. Oncoprint grid. Standard colors: missense, truncating, amplification, deletion.

Functional Consequence

Linking the oncoprint to expression data shows the result of mutations. Patients with TP53 alterations typically show lower CDKN1A expression.

Figure 2. CDKN1A expression by TP53 status.
