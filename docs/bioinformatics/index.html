Bioinformatics Visualization

Fifteen interactive explanations of the visual language used to decode life's complexity.

Modern biology is a data science. From profiling thousands of individual cells to comparing millions of DNA bases, the challenge is no longer just generating data, but making sense of it. Bioinformaticians have developed a distinctive visual vocabulary to navigate these high-dimensional landscapes.

This series begins by introducing that core vocabulary. Then, we follow a single biological story: the "two-gene detective story" of TP53 and CDKN1A. Each visualization adds a new layer of evidence (mutations, expression, methylation, and networks), revealing how a single broken connection can drive cancer.

The Vocabulary

Across the nineteen techniques, five semantic colors recur. Learning the palette once makes every chart in the series easier to read.

gene
A genomic feature being measured, a locus, transcript, or variant.
gene bodies · exons · SNPs · protein domains
sample
An individual patient, cell, or replicate, one observation in the cohort.
heatmap columns · oncoprint tracks · single cells
expression
Measured signal intensity, how much of something there is.
read counts · log-fold-change · heatmap cell values · peak height
significance
Statistical thresholds and called hits, what rises above the noise floor.
p-values · FDR cutoffs · volcano hits · GWAS peaks
pathway
Structural or functional connections, the wiring between features.
pathway edges · PPI networks · ChIP-seq binding · chord links
