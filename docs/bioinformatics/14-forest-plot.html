Forest Plot

Measuring the consistency of the TP53→CDKN1A link across independent studies.

We have seen that TP53 mutations reduce CDKN1A expression in synthetic cohorts. But any single study can be misleading. A meta-analysis pools results from multiple independent sources to ask if the effect holds up across different tissues and laboratories.

The forest plot is the standard way to visualize meta-analytic results. Each row represents a study, reporting an effect size and a confidence interval. A diamond at the bottom shows the pooled estimate, confirming the overall biological signal.

Figure 1. Forest plot of eight studies reporting CDKN1A log₂ fold change. Squares are proportional to study weight. The dashed line marks the null. The pooled diamond confirms a consistent reduction of expression in TP53 mutant tumors.

The I² statistic measures heterogeneity. Values above 50% suggest that the variation across studies is due to real biological differences rather than sampling error alone.

Sensitivity Analysis

How robust is the pooled estimate? In a leave-one-out analysis, we remove each study in turn to see if any single observation is driving the entire conclusion.

Figure 2. Leave-one-out explorer. Watch how the diamond and I² statistic shift as you include or exclude outliers. A robust biological effect should persist even after removing the largest study.

Publication Bias

Journals prefer significant results, which can bias the published literature. The funnel plot helps detect this by plotting effect size against precision. In an unbiased collection, the points should scatter symmetrically around the pooled estimate.

Figure 3. Funnel plot for detecting bias. The dashed line is the pooled estimate. Asymmetry suggests missing studies, often smaller ones with non-significant findings.
