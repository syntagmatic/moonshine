Lollipop Mutation Plot

Mapping mutations onto a protein's linear sequence to see where damage clusters.

A protein is a long chain of amino acids. The lollipop mutation plot unfolds this chain into a line: the \(x\)-axis is amino acid position, colored blocks mark functional domains, and vertical stems show where mutations occur.

TP53 is the most frequently mutated gene in human cancer. Its protein product, p53, is 393 amino acids long. When p53 carries the R175H mutation in its DNA-binding domain, it loses the ability to bind DNA and activate its targets like CDKN1A.

Figure 1. TP53 mutation hotspots. Lollipop height encodes mutation frequency. The R175H mutation is clearly visible in the DNA-binding domain.

The Target That Doesn't Need Mutating

If TP53 is the general, CDKN1A is the soldier it sends to halt the cell cycle. Cancer silences p21 by breaking TP53 upstream rather than mutating CDKN1A directly.

Figure 2. CDKN1A lollipop plot. Note the significantly lower mutational burden compared to TP53.
