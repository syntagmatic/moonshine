Genome Browser

A genome browser lines up gene models, expression, conservation and variants on one coordinate axis, so that evidence at the same stretch of DNA can be read together.

Part 1 placed methylation at CpG sites along the CDKN1A promoter. A genome browser does the same for many kinds of data at once: it shows a stretch of DNA with several tracks of information at the same coordinates, such as gene models, regulatory regions, expression, conservation and variants.

The horizontal axis is genomic position in base pairs. In a browser search box, coordinates are usually displayed as 1-based, fully closed intervals, like chr7:55,000,001-55,500,000. Many file formats underneath the browser, especially BED and bedGraph, use 0-based, half-open intervals, where the same 500 kb span is written as chr7 55000000 55500000. The one-base shift at the left edge is easy to miss and can break an analysis.

The browser does not interpret the tracks. It places them on the same coordinate ruler, so that an expression peak lining up with a gene, a promoter feature, a conserved segment and a variant can be seen together. Figure 1 is a browser at the EGFR locus.

The label above uses browser-style 1-based coordinates; intervals are stored internally as half-open spans. Gene models (Ensembl canonical transcripts) and CpG islands (UCSC) are real GRCh38 annotations; the expression, conservation and variant tracks are simulated.

Reading the tracks

  • Gene models (blue arrows): Blocks are exons and thin lines are introns. The track draws untranslated ends the same as coding exons; Figure 2 separates them. Exons are kept in the mature RNA, and only their coding part becomes protein.
  • Expression (area chart): RNA-seq coverage across the locus. A peak over a transcript is evidence that its RNA is abundant in the sample. It says nothing direct about protein.
  • CpG islands (green): CG-rich regions, often near promoters. Methylation of a promoter island often goes with lower transcription.
  • Conservation (purple): Similarity across species. High conservation is evidence that selection has constrained a sequence, which hints at function without identifying it.
  • Variants (brown ticks): Sample or population differences at precise positions. The browser shows where they land. Interpreting them needs allele frequency, predicted effect and context.

Figure 2. Anatomy of a gene model. Coding exons (thick) become protein. UTRs (medium) are exon sequence that is transcribed but not translated. Introns (thin lines) are spliced out. The arrow shows the direction of transcription.

Genome browsers show one chromosome at a time. Inter-chromosomal events (translocations, fusions, long-range contacts) need the circos plot.
