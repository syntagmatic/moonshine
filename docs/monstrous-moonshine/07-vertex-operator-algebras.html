Vertex Operator Algebras

Part 7 of 10. The Conway–Norton conjecture asks for a graded Monster module whose graded traces are Hauptmoduls. To build one, we need the algebraic structure that turns a graded vector space into a consistent quantum field theory: the vertex operator algebra.

Act II assembled the ingredients — the Leech lattice, the bridge from E8 to the Monster, the graded-module formalism. Now Act III must construct V♮ and prove that its graded traces are Hauptmoduls. Both steps rest on a single algebraic framework: the vertex operator algebra (VOA). This explainer introduces the axioms, the key examples, and the conceptual point that makes VOAs indispensable: they are the rigorous mathematics of two-dimensional conformal field theory.

The idea: states that talk to each other

A graded vector space V = ⊕ Vn is inert — it has no multiplication, no dynamics. A VOA endows V with a way for every state v ∈ V to act on every other state, not by a single linear map but by a formal power series of linear maps indexed by a complex variable z. This is the vertex operator:

Each coefficient v(n) is a linear endomorphism of V. Taken together, Y(v, z) is a "field" in the physicist’s sense: an operator-valued distribution on the punctured complex line. The variable z plays the role of a coordinate on the worldsheet of a string — which is why VOAs are sometimes called "mathematical string theory".

The four axioms

A vertex operator algebra is a graded vector space V = ⊕n≥0 Vn equipped with a vertex operator map Y, a vacuum vector |0⟩ ∈ V0, and a conformal vector ω ∈ V2, satisfying four axioms.

1. Vacuum. The vacuum is the identity for the vertex operator: Y(|0⟩, z) = idV, and for every v ∈ V, the operator Y(v, z) applied to |0⟩ converges to v as z → 0. In physical language: creating a state v at the origin and letting the insertion point collapse is the same as just having v.

2. Translation. There is an operator T: V → V satisfying [T, Y(v, z)] = ∂z Y(v, z) and T|0⟩ = 0. This says moving an insertion point along the worldsheet is an infinitesimal symmetry — it is translation covariance.

The number c is the central charge of the VOA. It measures the "size" of the conformal symmetry — or, in string-theoretic terms, the number of free bosonic dimensions. For V♮, the central charge is c = 24: the Leech lattice lives in 24 dimensions, and the VOA records that fact in its Virasoro algebra.

Why VOAs matter for moonshine. A VOA is not just a graded vector space with multiplication. The Virasoro structure fixes the grading (L0 is the energy operator), locality controls the singularities, and the vacuum pins the identity. Together these axioms are rigid enough that the automorphism group of V♮ is forced to be a finite simple group — the Monster — rather than an arbitrary matrix group.

The free boson: a toy example

The simplest nontrivial VOA is the free boson (or Heisenberg VOA) of central charge c = 1. Its underlying vector space is the polynomial algebra in infinitely many variables α−1, α−2, α−3, … applied to a vacuum vector. The grading is by total degree, where α−n has degree n. From free bosons to lattice VOAs

A single free boson gives a VOA of central charge 1. Taking d independent free bosons gives central charge d. But to get a VOA with interesting symmetry, we compactify: we identify the bosonic fields with a lattice Λ ⊂ ℝd. The resulting lattice VOA VΛ has central charge d and graded dimension equal to the lattice’s theta series divided by the Dedekind eta function to the d-th power. In physical terms, this is a bosonic string compactified on the torus ℝd/Λ.

For the Leech lattice Λ24, the lattice VOA VΛ24 has central charge 24. Its grade-1 space V1 has dimension 24 — one boson per lattice dimension. Those 24 states generate a 24-dimensional abelian Lie algebra acting on VΛ24 by continuous symmetries. But the Conway–Norton specification demands dim V0♮ = 0 and no continuous symmetries at all. So VΛ24 is not V♮ — it has too much symmetry. The next explainer shows how to fix this by an orbifold construction.

VOAs as mathematical string theory. A VOA axiomatises the chiral half of a 2D conformal field theory. The vertex operators are the OPE, the Virasoro algebra is the conformal symmetry, the central charge counts target-space dimensions. For V♮, c = 24 is dictated by the Leech lattice's dimension — the same 24 that appears as the critical bosonic-string dimension (26 minus the 2 longitudinal degrees removed by Virasoro constraints). Borcherds imported the no-ghost theorem from 26-dimensional bosonic string theory as a lemma in pure algebra. The moonshine proof uses the substance of physics, not merely the language.

What we take with us

  • A VOA is a graded vector space V with a vertex operator map Y(v, z), a vacuum |0⟩, and a conformal vector ω, satisfying vacuum, translation, locality, and Virasoro axioms.
  • The Virasoro algebra [Lm, Ln] = (m−n)Lm+n + (c/12)(m3−m)δm+n,0 encodes conformal symmetry. The central charge c for V♮ is 24.
  • Lattice VOAs VΛ arise from compactifying free bosons on a torus ℝd/Λ. For Λ = Λ24 (Leech), the lattice VOA has c = 24 but dim V1 = 24 ≠ 0 — too much symmetry.
  • Next step: orbifold VΛ24 by the involution x ↦ −x to kill the weight-1 states and produce V♮.
