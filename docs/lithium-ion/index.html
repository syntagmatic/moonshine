The Lithium-Ion Cell

A lithium-ion cell is a small metal cylinder with a voltage — 3.7 V new, useful down to about 2.7 V. Wire thousands of them together and you get an EV pack; add a BMS and you get something that won't catch fire. Open one up and the voltage is a chemical-potential derivative; push current through it and that voltage sags; leave it alone and an SEI creeps across the anode and eats the lithium.

Butler-Volmer and Nernst share the log-sum structure of the Fisher-information metric used in the information geometry series: overpotential η as a dual coordinate, current as the conjugate response, activation-energy landscape as close kin to a KL divergence.
