The Lithium-Ion Cell

A lithium-ion cell stores energy as lithium shuttled between two host materials, and almost everything you can measure from outside is a voltage. These five essays start from that voltage. The first two explain where the resting voltage comes from and what a current subtracts from it. The next two follow a cell as it ages, and show how an impedance sweep reads that aging back through the terminals. The last puts cells together into a pack, where charging, cell-to-cell spread, state-of-charge estimation and thermal runaway become engineering problems.

Part of the Moonshine explainer project. Standard references: Newman & Thomas-Alyea, Electrochemical Systems; Bard & Faulkner, Electrochemical Methods; Doyle, Fuller, Newman (1993) P2D model.
