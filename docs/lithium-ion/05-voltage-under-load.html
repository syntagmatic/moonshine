Voltage Under Load: The Stack of Losses

A real cell's terminal voltage is never its OCV, it's OCV minus a stack of losses (ohmic, charge-transfer, concentration), each with its own magnitude, current law, and timescale.

Draw current and a cell's terminals sag below the thermodynamic OCV. Three mechanisms in series subtract from that baseline, each with its own magnitude, current law, and timescale.

The cell equation

The additive form is not an approximation. Three processes sit in series along the current path, each dropping its own voltage.

VOCV is the thermodynamic target (Part 4). IR is the ohmic drop across electrolyte, separator, and collectors, linear in current and vanishing the instant it stops. ηct is the Butler-Volmer price of forcing ions across the interface faster than the exchange current density i₀. ηdiff is the concentration overpotential: surface lithium depletes faster than bulk diffusion replenishes it, and Nernst charges you for the gap. Three distances from equilibrium, paid in electron transport, interfacial kinetics, and solid-state mass transport.

Where each loss comes from

IR is Ohm's law; the other two are worth a picture. ηdiff lives inside the particle: draw current and a gradient opens between the full centre and the depleted surface that Nernst actually reads. ηct lives at the interface: applying ηct tilts the transition-state barrier by αFη forward and (1−α)Fη backward, and Butler-Volmer is the difference of the two tilted Arrhenius rates.

Figure 1. Left — ηdiff. Radial lithium concentration in a spherical particle at SOC = 0.5; C-rate scales the surface depletion. The gap between average (dashed) and surface (dot) is ηdiff in concentration units. Right — ηct. The activation barrier tilts with η: reactant side rises by αFη, product side falls by (1−α)Fη. Below, the Butler-Volmer curve i/i₀ = exp(αFη/RT) − exp(−(1−α)Fη/RT). At α = 0.5, 298 K the Tafel slope 2.303·RT/(αF) ≈ 118 mV/decade. The third loss, IR, is just a resistor (right).

Peeling the voltage, SOC by SOC

Hold current constant, sweep SOC, and the three sources stack into Vcell. The nested curves below peel them off in order; each shaded band is one term's budget at every SOC.

Figure 2. VOCV (black), minus ηdiff (purple), ηct (orange), IR (red), landing on Vcell (blue). Auto-sweeps 1 → 0; drag the cursor to inspect, pause to hold. Changing C-rate or chemistry re-arms the sweep. Low C-rate: bands hair-thin, Vcell hugs VOCV. 5 C: ηct and ηdiff inflate, and ηdiff diverges at end-of-discharge as i → ilim. NMC's sloping OCV and LFP's 3.42 V plateau share the same loss stack — it is indifferent to the thermodynamic shape underneath.

Three current laws. IR ∝ i. ηct ∝ ln(i/i₀) once i ≫ i₀ (BV's inverse-sinh is near-log). ηdiff ∝ −ln(1 − i/ilim), singular at i = ilim. That is why the three bands in Figure 2 inflate at different rates when you drag C-rate, and also why the cell's timescales separate: IR is instantaneous, ηct settles on the double-layer RC (≈ 1 s), ηdiff drags out to minutes as diffusion fronts propagate.

Regimes in a triangle

Every operating condition reshuffles the three losses. Low current → ηct dominates (BV is already log-steep at i ≈ i₀). High C-rate → IR gains ground. End-of-discharge → ηdiff explodes as i → ilim. Cold → ηct takes off as i₀ shrinks Arrhenius-style. Plot the fractional composition (IR%, ηct%, ηdiff%) in a ternary triangle; any parameter sweep traces a trajectory.

Slider moves a cursor along the active trajectory; status bar reports recipe, mV budget, and dominant regime.

Corners are pure regimes, sides drop one term, the interior is where real cells live. Fast-charging protocols avoid the ηdiff corner (lithium plating) while keeping ηct bounded (heat). A BMS that knows where it sits in this triangle knows more about what limits the cell than any single-number state estimate.
