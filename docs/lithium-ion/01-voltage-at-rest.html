The Voltage at Rest

What sets a cell's resting voltage, and why some voltage curves are easier to read back than others.

Put a multimeter on a cell with nothing connected and let it settle. The reading is the open-circuit voltage (OCV), and once current flows, every loss is subtracted from it. OCV is the difference in lithium's chemical potential between the two electrodes, divided by the Faraday constant. The chemistry sets its value. Its shape against state of charge decides whether a battery management system (BMS) can work out the state of charge from a voltmeter.

A layered host

Graphite is a stack of sp² carbon honeycombs, covalently bonded in-plane and held to their neighbours above and below only by van der Waals attraction between π-clouds. The few-ångström gap between adjacent sheets is a gallery. On charge, Li⁺ squeezes from the electrolyte into those galleries; the carbon sheets themselves are off-limits. Fully lithiated graphite is LiC₆, one lithium per six carbons, all parked in galleries.

Filling does not proceed uniformly. At low lithium content, Li occupies every n-th gallery and fills it completely, leaving the rest empty. The state with every n-th gallery full is stage n; a charging electrode walks empty (dilute) → stage 4 → stage 3 → stage 2 → stage 1 (LiC₆). Opening a gallery costs a fixed elastic energy (the two bounding sheets must push apart by about 10%, straining their van der Waals bonds) and that cost scales with the number of opened galleries, not how full they are. Once open, each additional Li is nearly free. The system therefore opens as few galleries as it can. Entropy pushes the other way, and elasticity wins until the current stage saturates.

Because opening costs a fixed energy and filling an open gallery is cheap, every gallery is either fully open or fully closed. The transitions from one stage to the next are first-order phase transitions, and that discreteness is what produces voltage plateaus.

From lattice to voltage

OCV is a derivative of the host's Gibbs free energy. The chemical potential of lithium in the host is μ(x) = ∂G/∂x. Moving a mole of Li from a reference electrode (at μ_ref) into the host at composition x performs reversible work (μ − μ_ref) per mole; for a single-electron transfer the cell does −FV of electrical work per mole. Setting the two equal:

For a half-cell against lithium metal, μ_ref = μ_Li°, and V(x) encodes the shape of G(x). A convex G with a smooth minimum gives a smoothly sloping V. When G(x) has two competing wells, say stage 2 and stage 1, the equilibrium free energy between them follows the common tangent joining the wells instead of the curve. Between the tangent points the system is a mechanical mixture. Adding lithium converts one phase to the other while μ, the slope of G, stays fixed, so V stays fixed too. Every plateau in a half-cell curve is a region where two phases coexist, and the plateau voltage is set by the tangent's slope.

The cathode menu

Graphite is the standard anode, so most chemistry decisions are made at the cathode. A cathode can phase-separate like graphite or form a continuous solid solution, and two common chemistries sit at either end. LFP (lithium iron phosphate, LiFePO₄) phase-separates into one long flat plateau near 3.42 V that spans almost its entire capacity. NMC (nickel-manganese-cobalt oxide) is a solid solution whose voltage slopes smoothly from about 4.2 V down to about 3.4 V on discharge. Why the shape matters

At rest the BMS sees one number, a voltage, and wants the hidden state x. Given a known V(x), it computes x = V⁻¹(V), an inverse that behaves well numerically only where V(x) is strictly monotonic with a slope that stays away from zero. On a plateau the slope is near zero and its reciprocal is huge, so a millivolt of noise can move the estimate by tens of percent of SOC. The cause is the chemistry, fixed by the common-tangent construction above, and no BMS design removes it.

Graphite is steep only at low x and flat across its staging plateaus, so in an LFP/graphite cell both electrodes are flat over most of the range, and the small steps left in the cell curve come from the graphite.

On the plateau, V⁻¹ of the green curve has no useful value, and no amount of filter tuning recovers information the voltage does not carry. An NMC pack can track its SOC from voltage alone. An LFP pack has to lean mostly on coulomb counting, which Part 5 takes up.

The chemistry that makes LFP flat also makes it durable. Olivine is thermally stable and its lattice barely deforms across charge and discharge, which is where LFP gets its safety and cycle life. The price is that its voltage says little about its state of charge, so LFP systems make up for it in software: coulomb counting, relaxation behaviour after a load, temperature compensation, and regular full charges that re-anchor the count at the steep end of the curve.
