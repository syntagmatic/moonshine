Crystal bases

A crystal graph is a diagram attached to a crystal basis. In Kashiwara's construction, one starts with a representation of a quantum group . The parameter is not set to zero in the algebra itself. Instead, a suitable lattice and basis have a well-behaved limit as , and what remains is discrete enough to draw.

The vertices are basis elements in that limit. Each vertex has a weight. For every simple root index , there are partial maps , usually called Kashiwara operators. When , the graph has a directed edge of color from to . The operator goes back along the same edge.

A weight diagram records where weight spaces sit. A crystal graph also records how the simple-root operators move individual basis elements between those weights. The difference shows up in representations with multiplicities, where several vertices can share a weight but have different incoming and outgoing arrows.

A crystal graph is a deterministic colored graph. For a fixed color , each vertex has at most one outgoing edge and at most one incoming edge.

Highest and lowest weights

A highest-weight crystal has a distinguished vertex killed by all raising operators:

From that vertex, the rest of the connected crystal is generated by lowering operators. For an integrable highest-weight module, this graph is finite in finite type and has a lowest-weight vertex when the representation is finite-dimensional. The lowest vertex is killed by all lowering operators. In the string, highest and lowest are the two ends of the line.

"Highest" depends on a choice of positive roots. Once that choice is fixed, the highest weight determines the irreducible finite-dimensional representation. The crystal graph also gives a combinatorial model of the basis and of tensor products.

Tableaux, words, Littlewood-Richardson coefficients and character calculations all fit into this operator picture, which is why crystal graphs appear in combinatorics.
