Cayley Graphs: Groups as Geometry

Part 3 of 23. A group's multiplication rules, drawn as a directed graph. One node per element, one coloured edge per generator. The geometry depends on the generators, but it exposes the algebra you can walk through.

The Core Concept

A multiplication table hides geometry. A Cayley graph turns chosen generators into moves, and structure that is hard to see in the table becomes visible: cyclic subgroups are loops, cosets are repeated clusters, and commuting generators close coloured squares.

A group is a set G together with an associative binary operation, an identity element e, and inverses. A set S of generators is a subset of G such that every element of the group can be written as a product of elements of S and their inverses.

Given a group G and a generating set S, the Cayley graph Cay(G, S) is a directed graph whose vertices are the elements of G. For each element g and each generator s in S, there is a directed edge from g to gs, coloured by s.

Vertex-transitivity: Every node in a Cayley graph looks the same: from any vertex you see the same pattern of outgoing edges, since left multiplication by any group element is a graph automorphism. The graph has no special vertex until you pick one as the identity.

Cosets as clusters: If H is a subgroup of G, the left cosets gH partition the group into equal-sized blocks. In the Cayley graph, each coset forms a cluster of nodes connected internally by the generators belonging to H.

A gallery of small groups: The Cyclic groups (Zn) form directed polygons. The Klein four-group (V4) forms a square where every pair of paths commutes. The Symmetric group (S3) forms a triangular prismatic shape. The Dihedral group (D4) connects two 4-cycles. The Quaternion group (Q8) looks similar to D4 but has a different edge pattern, reflecting its different algebraic structure.

Concept Summary

  • Multiplication: Following a coloured edge from g lands on gs. Every product is a path.
  • Subgroups: A subgroup generated by a subset of the generators appears as a connected subgraph through every node.
  • Cosets: Left cosets of a subgroup partition the graph into isomorphic clusters.
  • Commutativity: Two generators commute if and only if every blue-red square closes.
  • The word metric: Shortest-path distance in the graph gives a left-invariant metric on the group.
  • Generator choice: The same group can draw different Cayley graphs. The graph records the group together with the chosen generating set.
