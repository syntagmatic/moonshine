The Associahedron

An associahedron is a polytope whose vertices represent fully parenthesized products. Two vertices are connected when one pair of parentheses is changed by the elementary move . This is the smallest move that associativity allows.

There are several indexing conventions in the literature. In this article, an -gon gives an associahedron of dimension . Its vertices are the triangulations of the polygon. Its edges are flips of one diagonal. With that convention, the pentagon gives an actual pentagon, and the hexagon gives a three-dimensional polytope with 14 vertices.

Parenthesizations and Triangulations

A full parenthesization of a product of factors can be drawn as a triangulation of a convex polygon with vertices. One way to see the correspondence is recursive. The last multiplication splits the product into a left block and a right block. In the polygon that split is a diagonal, and the two subproducts become the smaller polygons on either side of it.

For example, five factors correspond to triangulations of a hexagon. The product ((ab)c)(de) makes one split between abc and de, and then further splits inside those pieces. Drawing all those splits as noncrossing diagonals gives a triangulation.

The number of triangulations of a convex -gon is the Catalan number

This is why Catalan numbers count both polygon triangulations and parenthesized products. The associahedron also records which states are one move apart.

Flips and the Exchange Graph

In a triangulated convex polygon, every internal diagonal belongs to exactly two triangles. Together those two triangles form a quadrilateral. A flip removes the shared diagonal and inserts the other diagonal of the quadrilateral, leaving everything else alone.

The exchange graph has one vertex for each triangulation and one edge for each flip. For the convex -gon, this graph is the 1-skeleton of the associahedron. Distance in the graph is the number of flips needed to pass from one triangulation to another.

Cluster Algebra Connection

Cluster algebras use a similar language. A cluster is a set of mutually compatible variables. Mutation replaces one variable in a cluster by another one, according to an exchange relation. The exchange graph records clusters as vertices and mutations as edges.

For finite type , this picture has a concrete polygon model. Cluster variables correspond to diagonals of a convex -gon, clusters correspond to triangulations, and mutation is a diagonal flip. Thus the type exchange graph is the pentagon, and type is the hexagon triangulation graph shown above.

The exchange relation has a geometric form in a quadrilateral. In the coefficient-free surface model, the two diagonals of a quadrilateral satisfy a Ptolemy-type relation. The polygon does not prove the cluster algebra structure, but it is a faithful combinatorial model of the type A exchange graph. Quiver Mutation

The polygon model exists only in type A. What carries over to other cluster algebras is a quiver, a directed graph that records how the variables of one cluster interact. A triangulation gives one directly. Put a vertex on each diagonal. Inside every triangle with two diagonal sides, draw an arrow between them, turning the same way around every triangle.

Flipping diagonal changes that quiver by a rule stated without the polygon. Fomin and Zelevinsky's quiver mutation at adds an arrow for every path , reverses every arrow at , and then deletes opposite pairs of arrows two at a time. Because the rule needs only the quiver, it applies to quivers with no polygon behind them, and mutating twice at the same vertex returns the quiver you started from. Fomin and Zelevinsky proved in 2003 that a cluster algebra built from a quiver has finitely many clusters exactly when its mutation class contains an orientation of a Dynkin diagram of type A, D or E. Type gives the associahedron of the -gon above, and types D and E give the generalized associahedra of Chapoton, Fomin and Zelevinsky. The same ADE list turns up in Gabriel's 1972 theorem, which says a connected quiver has finitely many indecomposable representations exactly when its underlying graph is an ADE Dynkin diagram.
