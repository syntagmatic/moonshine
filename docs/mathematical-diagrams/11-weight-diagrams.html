The Core Concept

Every finite-dimensional representation of a semisimple Lie algebra decomposes into simultaneous eigenspaces for the Cartan subalgebra. The eigenvalues are weights, and they live on a lattice. A weight diagram plots every weight as a dot, sized by its multiplicity (the dimension of the eigenspace).

Weights are eigenvalues: The Cartan subalgebra is the maximal commuting part of the algebra. Its simultaneous eigenvalues on a representation are called weights. The weights of the adjoint representation (where the algebra acts on itself) are called roots.

sl₂ (rank 1): Weights are integers on a line. An irreducible representation of highest weight λ has weights λ, λ−2, …, −λ, each with multiplicity 1, and dimension λ+1.

sl₃ (rank 2): Weights can be plotted on a two-dimensional weight lattice, often using Dynkin-label coordinates. The fundamental representation 3 is a triangle, and the adjoint 8 is a hexagon with a double point at the origin.

Highest weight theorem: Every irreducible finite-dimensional representation is uniquely determined by its highest weight. The Weyl dimension formula computes the dimension from the highest weight alone.

Weyl symmetry: The Weyl group acts by reflections on the weight diagram. Irreducible diagrams are symmetric under that action, and multiplicities often grow toward the interior. In type A, the same multiplicities can be computed through semistandard tableaux and Kostka numbers.

Concept Summary

  • Dimension: The total number of dots (counting multiplicity) equals the dimension of the representation.
  • Symmetry: The Weyl group acts by reflections, so every diagram has the symmetry of the root system.
  • Highest weight: The unique extremal dot in the dominant chamber determines the entire representation.
  • Multiplicity: Dot sizes reveal the combinatorial depth, growing toward the interior.
  • Root action: Simple roots connect adjacent weights, encoding how raising and lowering operators move between eigenspaces.
  • Tensor products: To decompose a tensor product, shift one diagram by each weight of the other and collect the resulting multiplicities.
