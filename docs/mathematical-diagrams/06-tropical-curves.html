Tropical Curves: Where Linear Pieces Tie

Part 6 of 11. Tropical geometry replaces a polynomial by a maximum of linear functions, which turns algebraic curves into graphs of straight segments. The curve is where the maximum is attained at least twice.

Tropical arithmetic

Tropical arithmetic replaces addition by maximum and multiplication by addition. This article uses the max-plus convention; the min-plus convention common in the literature negates every coefficient and draws the same curves reflected through the origin.

Curves in the plane

The one-variable picture carries over. Lift each exponent to height ; the upper hull projects to a subdivision of the Newton triangle, and the curve is dual to it. Each region belongs to an exponent point, each edge crosses a subdivision edge at right angles, each vertex sits over a cell, and an edge's weight is the lattice length of its dual edge.

Amoebas

A nomogram (Part 11) puts quantities on logarithmic scales so that multiplying them becomes adding lengths. Tropical geometry pushes the same move to its limit, where adding the quantities themselves becomes taking a maximum.

Apply it to a whole curve. Send each point of the complex line to ; the image is the line's amoeba. A point is in it exactly when can be the sides of a triangle, and as grows the amoeba shrinks onto the tropical line .

Balancing

Each edge of a plane tropical curve has a rational slope and an integer weight. At every vertex, the primitive direction vectors of the outgoing edges, each multiplied by its weight, sum to zero. Duality is the reason: each weighted vector is a side of the vertex's dual cell turned a quarter turn, and the sides of a polygon close up.
