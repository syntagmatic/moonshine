Maxima of linear functions

To define a plane tropical curve, take the pointwise maximum of finitely many affine-linear functions on , which gives a convex piecewise-linear surface. The tropical curve is the projection of the creases of that surface down to the -plane.

Amoebas

Tropical curves also arise as limits of ordinary complex curves in the algebraic torus. Send a point to the logarithms of the absolute values of its coordinates. The image is called an amoeba. When that logarithmic picture is rescaled, many families of amoebas approach piecewise-linear spines.

Balancing and the Newton Polygon

The edges of a tropical curve have rational slopes, integer weights and a local conservation law. Near a vertex, take each outgoing edge direction as a primitive integer vector and multiply it by the edge weight. The sum of those weighted vectors is zero. This is the balancing condition.

The balancing inspector draws the outgoing weighted rays of a chosen vertex type on the left, and places the weighted primitive vectors head to tail on the right, where a balanced vertex closes up.

Balancing inspector. Smooth trivalent vertices close the vector polygon with three unit directions. Higher weights and higher valence still use the same conservation law.

For a plane tropical curve coming from a polynomial, the balancing law is reflected in the Newton polygon, the convex hull of the exponent lattice points . Coefficients lift those points to heights , and for the max-plus convention the upper convex hull projects back to a regular subdivision of the Newton polygon.

The tropical curve and that subdivision are dual to one another. A region of the plane corresponds to an exponent point. An edge of the curve corresponds to an edge in the Newton subdivision, with perpendicular direction. A vertex of the curve corresponds to a polygonal cell in the subdivision.

A tropical curve is called smooth when the subdivision is a unimodular triangulation, meaning the local Newton cells are as small as the lattice allows. The polyline itself still has corners.

Degree, Ends, and Genus

The Newton polygon also controls coarse topological data. For ordinary plane curves of degree , the Newton polygon is the triangle with vertices . Its boundary lattice points cut the boundary into unit segments, and for a smooth curve each segment is dual to one unbounded end. Its interior lattice points count the genus of a smooth curve with that Newton polygon.

A plane tropical curve is drawn as a balanced graph in , but an abstract tropical curve is a metric graph with ends. The embedding records slopes and Newton data, and the abstract graph records lengths, cycles and genus.

For higher genus, not every metric graph appears as a smooth plane tropical curve with a chosen Newton polygon. The polygon and its regular unimodular subdivisions constrain which graph shapes and edge lengths can occur, because a plane tropical curve is a graph with lattice geometry attached.
