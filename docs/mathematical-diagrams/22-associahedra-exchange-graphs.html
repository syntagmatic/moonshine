Core Concept

An associahedron is a polytope whose vertices represent fully parenthesized products. Two vertices are connected when one pair of parentheses is changed by the elementary move . This is the smallest move that associativity allows.

There are several indexing conventions in the literature. In this article, an -gon gives an associahedron of dimension . Its vertices are the triangulations of the polygon. Its edges are flips of one diagonal. With that convention, the pentagon gives an actual pentagon, and the hexagon gives a three-dimensional polytope with 14 vertices.

The point of the diagram is not only to count bracketings. It shows how bracketings sit next to one another. The graph remembers adjacency: which choices differ by one local change, and which require several changes.

Parenthesizations and Triangulations

A full parenthesization of a product of factors can be drawn as a triangulation of a convex polygon with vertices. One way to see the correspondence is recursive. The last multiplication splits the product into a left block and a right block. In the polygon, that split is a diagonal. The two subproducts become the two smaller polygons on either side.

For example, five factors correspond to triangulations of a hexagon. The product ((ab)c)(de) makes one split between abc and de, and then further splits inside those pieces. Drawing all those splits as noncrossing diagonals gives a triangulation.

The number of triangulations of a convex -gon is the Catalan number

This is why Catalan numbers appear both in polygon dissections and in parenthesized products. They count the states. The associahedron adds the local geometry between states.

Flips and the Exchange Graph

In a triangulated convex polygon, every internal diagonal belongs to exactly two triangles. Together those two triangles form a quadrilateral. A flip removes the shared diagonal and inserts the other diagonal of the quadrilateral. Nothing else changes.

The exchange graph has one vertex for each triangulation and one edge for each flip. For the convex -gon, this graph is the 1-skeleton of the associahedron. The term "exchange" is useful because it emphasizes replacement: one compatible piece is exchanged for the unique other piece inside the same quadrilateral.

Distance in this graph is a practical notion. It asks how many local flips are needed to pass from one triangulation to another. Even in small polygons, the graph makes clear that there is usually no single global reassociation. One moves through a sequence of local choices.

Cluster Algebra Connection

Cluster algebras use a similar language. A cluster is a set of mutually compatible variables. Mutation replaces one variable in a cluster by another one, according to an exchange relation. The exchange graph records clusters as vertices and mutations as edges.

For finite type , this picture has a concrete polygon model. Cluster variables correspond to diagonals of a convex -gon, clusters correspond to triangulations, and mutation is a diagonal flip. Thus the type exchange graph is the pentagon, and type is the hexagon triangulation graph shown above.

The algebraic exchange relation has a geometric shadow in a quadrilateral. In the coefficient-free surface model, the two diagonals of a quadrilateral are related by a Ptolemy-type relation. The diagram does not prove the cluster algebra structure, but it gives a faithful combinatorial model for the type A exchange graph.

Concept Summary

  • States: Vertices of the associahedron can be read as parenthesizations, binary trees, or triangulations of a convex polygon.
  • Local move: An edge is one reassociation, or equivalently one diagonal flip inside a quadrilateral.
  • Catalan count: The number of triangulations of an -gon is .
  • Exchange graph: The 1-skeleton of the associahedron records which triangulations differ by one flip.
  • Type A clusters: In finite type , diagonals are cluster variables, triangulations are clusters, and flips are mutations.
  • Conventions: Authors index associahedra in different ways. The polygon dimension rule is the convention used here.
