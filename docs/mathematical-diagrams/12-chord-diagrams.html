The Core Concept

Trace along a knot, recording each crossing as you go. You meet every crossing twice, once over and once under. Mark these 2n encounters as points on a circle and connect each pair with a chord. The result is a chord diagram: a combinatorial shadow that forgets the 3D embedding but remembers which strands cross. This encoding is the right language for Vassiliev's finite-type invariants.

Gauss and chord diagrams: A Gauss diagram records a knot's crossings as directed, signed chords on an oriented circle. A chord diagram is the undirected, unsigned version: just n chords connecting 2n points. Two chords intersect when their endpoints interleave on the circle.

Vassiliev invariants: A knot invariant is of finite type n if it vanishes on every singular knot with more than n double points, when extended via the Vassiliev skein relation.

The four-term (4T) relation: Not every linear combination of chord diagrams corresponds to a knot invariant. The four-term relation is the single algebraic constraint that defines the relevant quotient space. It arises from sliding one endpoint of a chord past an endpoint of another.

Weight systems and the Kontsevich integral: A weight system is a linear function on chord diagrams that satisfies 4T. Every Vassiliev invariant determines a weight system, and conversely. The Kontsevich integral is a universal Vassiliev invariant that assigns to each knot a formal series of chord diagrams — making chord diagrams modulo 4T the universal receiver for finite-type invariants.

Concept Summary

  • Crossings: Each chord records one crossing of the knot, abstracting away over/under information.
  • Interleaving: Two chords cross iff their endpoints alternate around the circle.
  • Intersection number: The count of crossing chord pairs, the simplest finite-type invariant beyond the trivial.
  • 4T relation: The single algebraic constraint that carves out the space dual to Vassiliev invariants.
  • Weight systems: Linear functions on chord diagrams that respect 4T, each one a finite-type knot invariant.
  • Universality: Via the Kontsevich integral, chord diagrams are the universal target for all finite-type invariants.
  • Lie structure: The 4T relation is the chord-diagram shadow of the Jacobi identity, linking knot theory to representation theory.
