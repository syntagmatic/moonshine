Chord Diagrams: From Knots to Finite-Type Invariants

Part 12 of 12. A chord diagram distils a knot to its crossings — points paired on a circle. The algebra of these diagrams, governed by the four-term relation, is dual to the space of Vassiliev invariants.

Trace along a knot, recording each crossing as you go. You meet every crossing twice, once over and once under. Mark these 2n encounters as points on a circle and connect each pair with a chord. The result is a chord diagram: a combinatorial shadow that forgets the 3D embedding but remembers which strands cross. This encoding is the right language for Vassiliev's finite-type invariants.

Gauss diagrams and chord diagrams

A Gauss diagram records a knot's crossings as directed, signed chords on an oriented circle representing the knot's parameter. Each chord connects the two preimages of a crossing, with an arrow from the overcrossing pass to the undercrossing pass and a sign () recording the local writhe.

A chord diagram is the undirected, unsigned version: just n chords connecting 2n points on a circle. Two chords intersect (or cross) when their endpoints interleave on the circle. For chords and , they cross if and only if exactly one of c, d lies in the arc from a to b.

Weight systems

A weight system of degree n is a linear function on the space of chord diagrams with n chords that satisfies the 4T relation. Every Vassiliev invariant of type n determines a weight system (by evaluating the invariant on singular knots), and conversely every weight system extends to a Vassiliev invariant. Connections

Chord diagrams sit at a crossroads. The circle represents a knot traversed once; the chords mark its crossings. The crossings themselves can be generated by braid moves. Weight systems often come from Lie algebras: given a metrized Lie algebra , one builds a weight system by placing the Lie bracket at each chord and contracting with the metric. The resulting invariants recover the coloured Jones polynomial and its relatives.

Chord diagrams also connect to weight diagrams through representation theory. The weight system of a simple Lie algebra encodes how representations interact at each crossing, and the 4T relation mirrors the Jacobi identity for the Lie bracket.

The connection to string diagrams is structural: chord diagrams can be interpreted as morphisms in a category of "Jacobi diagrams," with composition governed by the same topological principles as string-diagram calculus.

What the diagram sees

  • Crossings — each chord records one crossing of the knot, abstracting away over/under information.
  • Interleaving — two chords cross iff their endpoints alternate around the circle, capturing the combinatorial essence of linked crossings.
  • Intersection number — the count of crossing chord pairs, the simplest finite-type invariant beyond the trivial.
  • 4T relation — the single algebraic constraint that carves out the space dual to Vassiliev invariants.
  • Weight systems — linear functions on chord diagrams that respect 4T, each one a finite-type knot invariant.
  • Universality — via the Kontsevich integral, chord diagrams are the universal target for all finite-type invariants.
  • Lie structure — the 4T relation is the chord-diagram shadow of the Jacobi identity, linking knot theory to representation theory.
  • Finiteness — the graded vector space of chord diagrams modulo 4T is finite-dimensional in each degree, making computation tractable despite the combinatorial explosion of knot types.
