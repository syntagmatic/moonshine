Where It Appears

Coxeter diagrams first feel like a notation for reflection symmetry, and that remains their home base. Finite real reflection groups, including symmetry groups of regular polytopes, are classified by Coxeter diagrams. The type gives symmetric groups, gives signed permutation groups, and exceptional types such as are tied to regular four-dimensional polytopes.

In Lie theory, the Weyl group of a semisimple Lie algebra is a Coxeter group. The earlier Dynkin-diagram article used roots and Cartan matrices; here the same simple reflections are seen through their pairwise product orders. Passing from Dynkin data to Coxeter data forgets some root-length information but keeps the reflection group.

In combinatorics and geometric group theory, Coxeter systems organize reduced words, weak order, Bruhat order, Hecke algebras, hyperplane arrangements, buildings, and CAT(0) complexes. The diagram is often the first line of a paper because it fixes the local reflection rules before any larger geometry is constructed.
