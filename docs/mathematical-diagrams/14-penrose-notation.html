The Core Concept

In 1971, Roger Penrose proposed a graphical notation for tensor calculus. Each tensor becomes a node, its indices become lines, and contraction (the summing operation) is just connecting lines. The structure of an algebraic expression becomes visible in the shape of its diagram.

Tensors and type: A tensor of type (p,q) has p contravariant (upper) indices and q covariant (lower) indices. In Penrose notation, upper indices extend upward from the top of the node, lower indices extend downward. A scalar is (0,0), a vector is (1,0), a covector is (0,1), a matrix is (1,1).

Contraction = connecting wires: When an upper index of one tensor matches a lower index of another, we sum over that index. In Penrose's diagrams, this summation is simply connecting the corresponding wires. Matrix–vector multiplication connects the lower wire of A to the upper wire of v.

Trace = self-loop: The trace connects an upper wire back to a lower wire of the same tensor, forming a loop. Every closed loop represents a summation; a closed wire is a contraction with nothing left free.

Special tensors: The metric tensor is drawn as a cup with two lower wires (it lowers an index). The Kronecker delta is a straight wire with no node at all (the identity map). The Levi-Civita symbol is an antisymmetrizer node where swapping any two wires changes the sign.

Topology over geometry: Node positions carry no algebraic information; only connections matter. Dragging a tensor node changes the picture but not the expression. Two diagrams with the same connectivity are the same tensor expression.

Concept Summary

  • Nodes: Tensors, each labelled with a name and drawn as a rounded box. The number of ports on top and bottom gives the type.
  • Upper wires: Contravariant indices, extending upward. Free ones end in open circles and represent output slots.
  • Lower wires: Covariant indices, extending downward. Free ones end in open circles and represent input slots.
  • Connected wires: Contracted indices, drawn as curves between tensors. Each connection is a summation.
  • Self-loops: Trace operations, where a tensor's upper wire curves back to its own lower wire.
  • No wires at all: A node with no free wires is a scalar. The tensor has been fully contracted.
  • Topology over geometry: Dragging a node changes the picture but not the algebra. The meaning lives in the connections.
