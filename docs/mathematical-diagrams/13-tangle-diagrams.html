Tangle Diagrams: Open-Ended Knots and Rational Tangles

Part 13 of 14. A tangle is a portion of a knot confined to a ball, with strands escaping through four boundary points. Rational tangles — built by twisting — are classified by a single fraction, turning topology into arithmetic.

Cut a knot diagram with a circle that crosses it in exactly four points. The portion inside the circle is a tangle: a pair of arcs (strands) running between four boundary points on the circle's equator, labelled NW, NE, SW, SE. Where knot diagrams are closed curves and braid diagrams have many endpoints along two edges, tangles occupy a middle ground — open-ended, with just four free ends, yet rich enough to build every knot and link by closing them up.

The elementary tangles

Two tangles contain no crossings at all. The 0-tangle connects NW to SW and NE to SE with two vertical strands. The -tangle connects NW to NE and SW to SE with two horizontal strands. These are the two seeds from which all rational tangles grow.

Every other rational tangle is obtained from these by twisting: rotating one pair of adjacent endpoints around each other, creating crossings.

Twisting and rational tangles

A twist takes two adjacent boundary points and winds them around each other. Horizontal twists rotate the bottom pair (SW, SE), adding crossings at the bottom of the diagram. Vertical twists rotate the right pair (NE, SE), adding crossings on the right side. A rational tangle is any tangle built by alternating horizontal and vertical twists, starting from the 0-tangle.

Conway's classification theorem

John Horton Conway proved the definitive result about rational tangles in 1970:

Conway's theorem. Two rational tangles are ambient isotopic (can be deformed into each other without cutting strands) if and only if they have the same fraction . The fraction is a complete invariant: it classifies rational tangles up to equivalence, turning a topological problem into an arithmetic one.

Closing tangles into knots

A tangle becomes a knot (or link) when its four boundary points are connected pairwise. There are two natural ways:

The numerator closure connects NW to NE and SW to SE with arcs across the top and bottom. The denominator closure connects NW to SW and NE to SE with arcs on the left and right. For a rational tangle with fraction , the numerator closure yields a knot (or link) whose properties are governed by p, while the denominator closure is governed by q.

For example, the [3] tangle (fraction 3/1) gives the trefoil knot under numerator closure. The [2, 3] tangle (fraction 11/3) gives a more complex knot. This is how Conway's tangle calculus generates the knot tables systematically.

From tangles to braids and knots. Tangles unify braids and knots into a single framework. A braid is a tangle with many boundary points along two edges; a knot is a tangle with zero boundary points (fully closed). Rational tangles sit between: four boundary points, enough to encode the arithmetic of continued fractions, yet simple enough to classify completely. The chord diagrams of finite-type invariants also arise naturally from tangle operations.

Tangle arithmetic

Tangles support algebraic operations. The tangle sum places two tangles side by side, connecting the NE of the first to the NW of the second. For rational tangles, the fraction of the sum is not simply the sum of the fractions — tangle addition is more subtle than ordinary addition, and the sum of two rational tangles need not itself be rational.

However, for the special case of integer tangles (fraction ), tangle addition does correspond to integer addition. This is the simplest case of Conway's tangle calculus, which extends to a powerful tool for studying knots by decomposing them into tangle building blocks.

What the diagram sees

  • Boundary points — four fixed points NW, NE, SW, SE where strands exit the tangle, the interface between inside and outside.
  • Crossings — over/under strand intersections inside the ball, generated by twist operations.
  • Twist sequence — the Conway notation recording how many twists at each stage, alternating horizontal and vertical.
  • Fraction — the continued-fraction invariant that completely classifies each rational tangle.
  • Closure — connecting boundary points pairwise transforms a tangle into a knot or link, the bridge from open to closed topology.
  • Rationality — a tangle is rational iff it can be unwound to the 0-tangle by a sequence of twists, iff its fraction is a rational number or .
