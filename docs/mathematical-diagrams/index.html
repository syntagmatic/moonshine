The Visual Language of Algebra

Eleven interactive essays on diagrams that mathematicians compute with. In each one, changing the picture changes a quantity computed from it: a polynomial, a path count, a contraction cost, a genus.

Many diagrams in mathematics are calculations as well as pictures. Read a knot diagram by its crossings and you can compute the Kauffman bracket from it. Count paths through a Bratteli diagram and you get the dimensions of representations. Pick the order in which to contract a tensor network and you have set the cost of the computation. Each essay below takes one kind of diagram, says what it encodes, and gives a figure where changing the picture updates the computed quantity.

Dynkin and Coxeter diagrams live in the Exceptional Atlas: see simple roots and Dynkin diagrams and the build-your-own Dynkin sandbox in pruning and the Diophantine threshold. The cohomology series is the background for the spectral sequence chart. Standard references include Mac Lane, Kauffman, Kassel and Turaev, Aitchison, Bump and Schilling, McCleary, Maclagan and Sturmfels, Lando and Zvonkin, Goodman, de la Harpe and Jones, Fomin and Reading, and Orús.
