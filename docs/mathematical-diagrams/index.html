The Visual Language of Algebra

Every good mathematical notation is also a diagram. Fourteen interactive explainers on the diagrams mathematicians actually think with: from Hasse diagrams and Young tableaux through braids, knots, and tangles, to Dynkin diagrams, quivers, dessins d'enfants, weight diagrams, chord diagrams, and Penrose tensor notation.

Mathematical diagrams are not illustrations of algebra; they are algebra, rendered in a visual language where position, connection, and crossing carry meaning. A Hasse diagram encodes a partial order; a Young tableau encodes a partition and, simultaneously, an irreducible representation of the symmetric group; a Cayley graph turns a finite group into a walkable geometry; a commutative diagram states a theorem about morphisms; a braid diagram records a topological object whose algebra satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation.

Each explainer takes one diagram type, explains what it encodes, and makes it interactive. You do not watch — you build, rearrange, and discover the structure the diagram reveals.
