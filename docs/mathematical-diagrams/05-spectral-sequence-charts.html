Spectral Sequence Charts

Part 5 of 10. A spectral sequence computes homology in stages. Its chart draws classes as dots on a grid and differentials as arrows that cancel them in pairs, until the surviving dots give the pieces of the answer.

Part 4 drew a representation as dots joined by operator arrows. A spectral sequence chart uses dots and arrows to track a homology computation done in stages. The chart says where each class lives and where a differential could go. It does not say which differentials are nonzero, and settling that is most of the work.

Cohomological spectral sequences and Adams charts point their arrows other ways, so the same algebra can look reflected or sheared.

The first chart computes its differentials instead of decreeing them. The page a class dies on is the gap in level between it and its partner, so a late differential is a class that took a long time to fill in. The dots alone cannot predict the pairs. Choosing the differentials

In a real computation nobody hands you the complex. A theorem supplies , and the arrows are the hard part: whether one is zero is a fact about the underlying topology or algebra, and pinning it down can take a paper. The sandbox lets you decree them. Everything is a vector space over , so a cell holds a dimension, and a nonzero differential of rank removes from both its source and its target.
