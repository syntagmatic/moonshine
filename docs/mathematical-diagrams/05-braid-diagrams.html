Braid Diagrams: Crossings and the Braid Group

Part 5 of 7. Strands twist around each other with a careful accounting of over and under, and the result is an algebraic structure richer than the permutation it projects onto.

Take a handful of strings, pin them at the top, pin them at the bottom, and let them cross each other on the way down. If you keep track of which strand passes over which, you have a braid. If you forget the over/under information, you have a permutation. The difference between those two things is the entire subject of this explainer.

Braids are geometric algebra. You compose them by stacking one on top of another. You invert them by reflecting top-to-bottom and swapping every crossing. They form a group, the braid group, that maps onto the symmetric group but is infinite and far more interesting. The braid group shows up in knot theory, quantum groups, algebraic geometry, and the theory of configuration spaces.

The braid group and its generators

The braid group on n strands, written Bn, is generated by elementary crossings. The generator swaps strand i over strand i+1, with strand i passing in front. Its inverse performs the same swap but with strand i+1 passing in front.

A braid word is a sequence of generators: you read it top to bottom and each generator contributes one crossing to the diagram. The multiplication in Bn is concatenation: given braids a and b, the product ab stacks a on top of b.

From braids to permutations

Every braid determines a permutation of the strands: just forget which strand passes over which. This defines a surjection Bn → Sn. Its kernel, the pure braid group Pn, consists of braids where every strand returns to its starting position. Pure braids wind around each other but never trade places.

The symmetric group as a shadow

The permutation displayed beneath the braid diagram is the image of the braid under the quotient Bn → Sn. Two very different braids can project to the same permutation. The braid is not the identity in B3, but its image in S3 is the identity permutation. This is why braid theory is strictly richer than the theory of permutations: it remembers the winding.

Braids and configuration spaces. The braid group Bn is the fundamental group of the configuration space of n unordered points in the plane. A braid is a loop in that space: the points move, possibly trading places, and return to their starting configuration (as a set). The pure braid group corresponds to ordered configurations where each point returns to its own starting position.

Where braids appear

Braid groups surface well beyond the study of literal woven strands. In knot theory, Alexander's theorem guarantees that every knot or link is the closure of some braid, making braids a universal encoding of knots. Markov's theorem then characterises when two braids produce the same link. In quantum groups and statistical mechanics, the Yang-Baxter equation is the consistency condition for exactly solvable lattice models, and the braid group provides its algebraic backbone. In algebraic geometry, braid groups control the monodromy of polynomial equations: as the coefficients vary, the roots braid around each other, and the resulting braid records the topology of the parameter space.

Pure braids form a normal subgroup of Bn, and the quotient is exactly Sn. The closure of a pure braid is a link with multiple components, one for each strand.

Garside normal form. Every braid can be written as a power of the "fundamental braid" (the half-twist of all strands) times a positive braid. This gives a solution to the word problem -- an algorithm to decide whether two braid words represent the same element of Bn -- and is the basis for efficient braid simplification far beyond the local cancellation shown above.

What the diagram sees

  • Strands are elements being permuted; their colours track identity through the crossings.
  • Crossings are generators of the braid group; over/under encodes the sign of the generator.
  • Vertical stacking is group multiplication: composition reads top to bottom.
  • The Yang-Baxter move is the fundamental equivalence: a local rearrangement that preserves the braid.
  • Closure converts a braid to a knot or link, connecting the algebra of Bn to the topology of embedded curves.
  • The permutation is the shadow on the symmetric group: the braid diagram remembers strictly more than its image in Sn.
