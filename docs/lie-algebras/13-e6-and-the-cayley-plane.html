E₆ and the Cayley Plane

Part 13 of 15. The (3, 3, 2) simply-laced Diophantine solution. Rank 6, dimension 78, 72 roots. E₆ is the isometry algebra of the octonionic projective plane 𝕆P², the largest projective plane constructible with division-algebra coordinates, and its Weyl group permutes the 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface.

We have now climbed to the simply-laced half of the exceptional list. The Diophantine inequality 1/p + 1/q + 1/r > 1 from explainer 10 has three "branched" solutions: (3, 3, 2), (4, 3, 2), and (5, 3, 2), giving E6, E7, and E8. This explainer is the first of two on these three algebras. We start with E6, the smallest, and walk through its root system, its connection to the octonionic projective plane, and the remarkable fact that its Weyl group is the symmetry group of the 27 lines on a cubic surface, one of the most celebrated classical configurations in all of algebraic geometry.

Where E₆ sits

The (3, 3, 2) Dynkin diagram has three arms of lengths 3, 3, 2 emanating from a central branch node. Counting all nodes (the branch plus 2 nodes on each length-3 arm plus 1 node on the length-2 arm): 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 6. So E6 is a rank-6 algebra.

In Bourbaki labelling, the main chain is α1, α3, α4, α5, α6, and α2 branches off α4 (the central node). The Cartan matrix is a 6 × 6 integer matrix with the standard ADE pattern, all −1 off-diagonal entries where an edge is present, 0 elsewhere:

Because E6 is simply-laced, all roots have the same squared length, and the diagonal entries are 2 with no asymmetric off-diagonal entries (−2 or −3) anywhere. The structural numbers: rank 6, dim 78, |Δ| = 72, Coxeter number 12, Weyl group order 51,840.

E₆ as the isometry algebra of 𝕆P²

The classical definition of E6 builds on the exceptional Jordan algebra h3(𝕆) from the last explainer. Recall: h3(𝕆) is the 27-dimensional algebra of 3 × 3 Hermitian octonionic matrices, and its derivation algebra is F4. E6 arises as the next algebra up in the tower.

Specifically, F4 derivations preserve the trace of an element of h3(𝕆) (since D(1) = 0 and the trace is the coefficient of the identity). E6 comes from enlarging F4 to the full reduced structure algebra of h3(𝕆), the Lie algebra of all linear maps that preserve the cubic form

where det is a well-defined cubic function on h3(𝕆) despite the non-associativity of the octonion product (one of the small miracles that make h3(𝕆) an exceptional structure). The Lie algebra of linear maps preserving det is E6, with dimension 78, a 26-dimensional enlargement of F4 that accounts for the 52 dimensions of F4 plus a 26-dimensional "non-derivation" piece that scales and reshapes h3(𝕆).

Geometrically, the projectivisation of h3(𝕆), that is, the set of 1-dimensional subspaces of h3(𝕆), contains a distinguished 16-real-dimensional subvariety called the octonionic projective plane:

This is the largest honest-to-goodness projective plane that can be built with coordinates from a normed division algebra. Projective planes over ℝ, ℂ, ℍ exist in all dimensions, but projective spaces over 𝕆 only exist up to dimension 2; the non-associativity prevents you from going any higher. The Cayley plane 𝕆P² is therefore a unique object in the landscape of projective geometry (see the Cayley plane chapter of the octonions series for the projective-geometry side), and its group of motions, the "isometry group" relative to the octonionic metric, is precisely E6.

More precisely: the real compact form of E6, called F4-then-E6 in the Tits–Freudenthal magic square (coming in explainer 15), acts on 𝕆P² by octonionic collineations. F4 sits inside E6 as the stabiliser of a chosen point of 𝕆P², and the quotient E6 / F4 is homeomorphic to the 16-dimensional real manifold 𝕆P². This is directly analogous to SO(3) / SO(2) = S2 and PGL3(ℂ) / (stabiliser) = ℂP², with the octonionic exception replacing the standard setups.

The 27 lines on a cubic surface

Here is where E6 enters classical algebraic geometry. A smooth cubic surface in three-dimensional complex projective space ℂP³ is the zero set of a homogeneous polynomial of degree 3 in four variables. Any such surface (assuming the coefficients are generic) contains exactly 27 complex lines, a famous 19th-century count due to Cayley and Salmon (1849). The 27 lines come with a rich combinatorial structure: each line meets exactly 10 of the other 26, and is disjoint from the remaining 16.

The intersection graph of the 27 lines, with a node per line and an edge whenever two lines meet, is a specific 10-regular graph on 27 vertices that can be described combinatorially in several equivalent ways. One particularly clean description uses the six distinguished points used to construct a cubic surface by blowing up ℂP² at those points. The 27 lines split naturally into three groups of sizes 6 + 15 + 6:

  • 6 exceptional lines E1, …, E6, one for each of the blown-up points.
  • 15 proper lines Lij for 1 ≤ i < j ≤ 6, the strict transform of the line in ℂP² through blown-up points i and j.
  • 6 conic lines C1, …, C6, the strict transform of the conic through the five points other than i.

The intersection rules are a short combinatorial table, derivable from the blow-up picture:

  • Ei meets Ej: never (i ≠ j).
  • Ei meets Ljk: iff i ∈ {j, k}.
  • Ei meets Cj: iff i ≠ j.
  • Lij meets Lkl: iff {i, j} ∩ {k, l} = ∅.
  • Lij meets Ck: iff k ∈ {i, j}.
  • Ci meets Cj: never (i ≠ j).

Counting for any single line: each has exactly 10 intersecting partners, a direct verification from the rules above. The Weyl group of E6, which has order 51,840, is the full automorphism group of this 27-vertex, 135-edge graph, it acts by symmetries that preserve the intersection pattern. This is why the Weyl group of E6 is sometimes called the group of the 27 lines, and the connection was understood by Cayley, Jordan, and Klein in the late 19th century, long before the classification of Lie algebras was complete.

E₆ and particle physics

E6 makes a cameo in the physics literature as a candidate grand unified theory (GUT) gauge group. The Standard Model of particle physics has gauge group SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1), a 12-dimensional product group describing the strong, weak, and electromagnetic interactions. Many attempts to unify these three forces into a single simple group use larger simple Lie algebras: SU(5) (Georgi–Glashow 1974), SO(10) (Fritzsch–Minkowski, Georgi 1975), and E6 (Gürsey–Ramond–Sikivie 1976). E6 is the smallest simple Lie algebra whose complex adjoint representation contains the Standard Model group with the right fermion content, specifically, one generation of quarks and leptons (including the right-handed neutrino) fits into the 27-dimensional fundamental representation of E6, which is exactly the Jordan algebra dimension.

This numerological match, 27 quarks-and-leptons ↔ 27 dimensions of h3(𝕆) ↔ 27 lines on a cubic surface, is the reason E6 keeps turning up in both physics and algebraic geometry despite being a "small exception." Whether any of these connections is physically meaningful remains open, but the mathematical structure is rigid enough that it deserves a second look.

Three 27s in a row. (1) The exceptional Jordan algebra h3(𝕆) has dimension 27. (2) A smooth cubic surface has 27 lines. (3) The fundamental representation of E6 has dimension 27. All three are the same object seen from three angles: the Jordan algebra's 27 coordinates, the cubic surface's 27 lines, and the E6-module's 27 weights. Knowing one tells you the other two, once you know how to translate.

What we take with us

  • The Dynkin shape. E6 has arms of lengths (3, 3, 2) emanating from a branch node. Six simple roots, five edges, one branch. Simply-laced: all edges are single, all roots have the same length.
  • Structural numbers. Rank 6, dim 78, |Δ| = 72, Coxeter number 12, Weyl group order 51,840.
  • E6 and h3(𝕆). E6 is the reduced structure algebra of the exceptional Jordan algebra, the Lie algebra of linear maps on h3(𝕆) preserving the cubic determinant form. It enlarges F4 (the derivation algebra) by 26 dimensions.
  • E6 and 𝕆P². The octonionic projective plane is the 16-real-dimensional subvariety of ℙ(h3(𝕆)) consisting of trace-1 idempotents. Its isometry algebra is E6, and the coset E6 / F4 is 𝕆P² itself.
  • 27 = 27 = 27. The fundamental representation of E6 is 27-dimensional, equal to dim h3(𝕆) and equal to the number of lines on a smooth cubic surface. The Weyl group of E6 is the automorphism group of the incidence graph of the 27 lines.
  • Grand unified theories. E6 is a candidate GUT gauge group, containing the Standard Model and accommodating one generation of fermions in the 27.
