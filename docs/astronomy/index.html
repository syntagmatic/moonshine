Astronomy

Everything astronomy knows about the stars arrived as light at one point on Earth. Four articles on what that light carries, and how each measurement rests on the one before.

Starlight carries four things we can measure: the direction it comes from, how bright it is, its colour, and how it changes with time. The first article works with direction and time alone, the geometry of the sky seen from where you stand. The second turns angles and brightness into distance, one calibrated rung at a time, out to the Hubble constant. The third uses those distances to put stars on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram and read their physics from it. The fourth uses what we then know about stars to find the planets around them.

The articles are written for someone who knows calculus, mechanics and a little thermodynamics but has never taken an astronomy course. Every figure computes what it shows, from real catalogue data wherever the claim is about the real sky.
