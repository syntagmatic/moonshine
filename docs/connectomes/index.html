Connectomes

A connectome is the wiring diagram of a nervous system. Eight articles on why it is a measurement rather than a blueprint: what was counted, what it is compared against, how it is drawn, and what it leaves out.

The word connectome covers graphs whose edges mean very different things: synapses counted one by one in electron micrographs, axons labelled by a tracer injection, streamlines traced through diffusion MRI. Each is an estimate with its own errors, and every claim made about one, that it is small-world, modular, hierarchical or wired at random, is a comparison with something else. The series works through those comparisons on real data, computed in the page.

The nematode C. elegans anchors it: 302 neurons, small enough that every null model, eigenvector and layout runs in the browser. The fly, the mouse and the macaque come in where they are the best example. The articles are written for someone comfortable with linear algebra, graphs and probability who has never studied neuroscience. Parts 3 to 8 are written so far; the first two are listed so the shape of the series is visible.
