Atlas of Atlases

Eight poset views of the Moonshine series. Each article takes one recurring theme, groups, graphs, spectra, manifolds, time, data, visual grammar, and lays out the concepts that theme touches as a Hasse diagram. The leaves are the explainers that use them, sometimes across five or six different series at once.

Most of the Moonshine series share more than they seem to. The Fiedler vector appears in lattice-simulation, tda, bioinformatics, algorithms-ml. Group actions organise exceptional-atlas, modular-forms, noether, lattice-simulation, type-systems. Random walks reappear as Markov chains in autoresearch, as Metropolis-Hastings in lattice-simulation, as anomaly detection in japan-earthquakes.

This series makes the overlap legible. Each article is a single rich Hasse diagram, concepts as nodes, a partial order of generality or dependence as edges, and small badges on each node pointing to the explainers (across whichever series apply) that give that concept its weight. The up-cone / down-cone interaction you have seen on 5 posets of the lattice series carries through here. Hover a node, see its supergroups lit in purple and its subgroups in amber. That is the same semantics every figure in this series uses.

One invariant across every article
A node is a concept, not an explainer. Edges run up toward more general, down toward more specific. Hover lights the two cones. Click a node to see the badge-list of explainers (possibly drawn from many series) that live in it. When the same Fiedler-vector node lights up with a tda badge, a bioinformatics badge, and a lattice-simulation badge at once, the cross-series overlap is not an observation, it is the figure.
