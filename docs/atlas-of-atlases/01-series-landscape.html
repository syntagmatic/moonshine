Series Landscape

All 21 Moonshine series at once. One DAG ordered roughly by prerequisite flow, one tag-overlap matrix, and one spine picker for zooming in on any single series and its two cones.

Moonshine is not a flat list of notebooks. Some series are foundational: you really do want to read type systems or lattice simulation before modular forms, and TDA makes more sense after algorithms & ML. Some series are applied stories whose machinery comes from elsewhere. The rest of the articles in this atlas are theme-sliced; this one is series-sliced, a map where you see which series exist and how they reach each other.

Figure 1 · The prerequisite DAG

Series as nodes; edges mean "reading this first is likely to help"
↑ supergroups, more foundational series ↓ subgroups, series that build on this one hover a node to light its two cones
Rank 0 at the top is the most foundational (type systems, parallel coordinates, visual language of algebra). Rank grows downward toward applications. Many edges are arguable — this is a map of my reading order, not a theorem. Click a node to open that series; a click does not navigate when the cursor is over the lit cone indicator.

The DAG is laid out in layers: each series sits at rank 1 + max(rank of prerequisites). Foundational visual-language and geometry series sit near the top. Domain stories sit near the bottom. The long right-hand chain, type systems → noether → lattice simulation → modular forms, is the main spine of the mathematics arc. The left chain, parallel coordinates → emergence → algorithms & ML → TDA, is the main spine of the data / method arc.

Figure 2 · Tag-overlap matrix

Shared-tag count between every pair of series
0 shared tags 2 shared 3+ shared hover a cell to see which tags are shared
The diagonal is dropped. Dark cells mark pairs that share a lot of machinery — lattice simulation / exceptional atlas / modular forms cluster on groups; tda / bioinformatics / algorithms-ml cluster on graphs, data; information geometry reaches across both clusters. This is the overlap structure the rest of the atlas unpacks theme by theme.

Figure 3 · Spine view

Pick a series; see its two cones in the prerequisite DAG
The up-cone is everything the selected series can legitimately reach for — its transitive set of prerequisites. The down-cone is everything that, downstream, reaches for it. Both cones come straight from the DAG of Figure 1; the visualisation just zooms in on one series at a time.

What this view buys

The tag matrix says these two series share machinery. The DAG says the dependency runs this way. The spine view says for any one series, here is the half-atlas that meets it. The rest of the atlas-of-atlases picks a specific piece of shared machinery (groups, graphs, spectra, manifolds, dynamics, data, visual grammar) and goes deeper on which concepts inside that theme live where.
