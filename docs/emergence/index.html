Emergence: Simple Rules, Complex Worlds

Simulations of systems where large-scale behavior comes from simple local rules.

A flock of starlings turns together with no bird in charge. A traffic jam travels backward through cars that are all trying to move forward.

These fourteen essays are grouped by mechanism rather than subject, because many famous examples come down to a few processes: local copying and alignment, a density threshold, a slowly driven system that relaxes in bursts, growth fed by a field that piles up at the tips, agents that write into their surroundings and read it back, and oscillators or chemicals coupled across space. Each essay follows one mechanism through one or two models, with simulations you can run in the browser.

The Ising ferromagnet, Conway's Game of Life and lattice Boltzmann flow past a cylinder are in the companion series Simulating on a Lattice.

The Vocabulary

Five ideas that recur across the series.

agent
An individual following simple local rules.
boids, cells, particles, nodes, grains, walkers
rule
The local interaction law that couples agents together.
neighborhood averaging, reaction kinetics, repulsion, copying
pattern
What emerges when many agents follow the same rules at once.
flocks, spirals, clusters, fractals, coherent oscillations
critical
The threshold where behavior snaps from one regime to another.
density tipping points, phase boundaries, percolation thresholds
feedback
Positive loops that amplify small differences into large ones.
growth at tips, avalanches, pheromone reinforcement
