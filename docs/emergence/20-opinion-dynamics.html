Opinion Dynamics

Social influence rules produce either agreement or entrenched camps, depending on a single threshold of confidence.

Imagine a population where each person holds an opinion on a scale from 0 to 1. Zero is one extreme, one is the other. People interact in pairs and nudge each other toward compromise. But there is a catch: people only listen to those they already mostly agree with. If the gap is too wide, they ignore each other entirely.

This is the Deffuant model. It has one free parameter: the confidence threshold. This number determines whether a society ends in harmony or deadlock.

1. The Deffuant Model

Below, each dot is an agent. At each step, two agents are chosen at random. If their opinions differ by less than the confidence threshold, they move halfway toward the other.

The threshold 0.25 is critical. Near this value, small random differences in initial opinions can lead to vastly different outcomes: a single united group or multiple isolated camps.

2. The Phase Transition

The transition from consensus to fragmentation is sharp. As the threshold drops, the number of surviving clusters jumps from one to many. This sudden shift is a classic phase transition.

3. Network Effects

Real societies have structure. Restricting interactions to a network of neighbors creates echo chambers. Tightly connected clusters reinforce their own views. Adding random long-range connections breaks these chambers open.
