Crystal Growth

Hexagonal symmetry and branching complexity emerge from water molecules attaching one at a time.

A snowflake starts as a tiny hexagonal prism. Water molecules latch on one by one. There is no blueprint for the final shape. The symmetry comes from the hexagonal lattice of ice, and the complexity comes from the competition between diffusion and surface energy.

1. The Hexagonal Lattice

The bonding geometry of water favors a hexagonal arrangement. In the simulation below, vapor diffuses inward and freezes on contact with the crystal. Cold temperatures produce branches; warm temperatures produce compact plates.

2. Diffusion vs. Faceting

Tips that poke into the vapor field grow faster because they intercept molecules first. This instability creates branches. Surface energy works against this, trying to keep faces flat.
