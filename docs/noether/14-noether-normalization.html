Noether Normalization

Part 14 of 15. Every finitely generated algebra over a field sits as a finite cover of a polynomial ring. The number of variables in the polynomial ring is the dimension of the variety, and that is how algebraic geometry learned to count dimensions.

The previous explainer guaranteed that every ideal in is finitely generated, so every quotient is a sensible "coordinate ring" of an affine variety. Noether normalization (1926) is the next step: it says that the coordinate ring of any such variety can be presented as a finite extension of a polynomial ring in fewer variables, and the number of those variables is exactly the geometric dimension of the variety.

"Finite as a module" means: there exist finitely many elements such that every element of can be written as with in the polynomial ring. Both Noetherian setup work, the polynomial ring is Noetherian, the variety has a definite dimension, and in this final structural theorem the two come together.

Why a generic linear change works

The construction in the proof is a piece of linear algebra. Given and a polynomial in the defining ideal, choose a generic linear change of variables

For generic constants , the leading term of after substitution becomes a pure power with nonzero coefficient. That makes integral over , it satisfies a monic polynomial equation in with coefficients in the smaller ring, which is exactly what "finite as a module" needs. Iterate the process for every generator of the ideal, eliminating one variable at a time, and what remains is a polynomial subring over which is a finite module.

The number is the Krull dimension of , the length of the longest strictly ascending chain of prime ideals in , or equivalently (for finitely generated ) the transcendence degree of the fraction field of over . Both definitions return the same number, and Noether normalization is the structural fact that makes them equivalent.

The pattern is consistent across all of these: there is a polynomial subring with equal to the dimension of the variety, and the original algebra is a finite module over that subring of some rank that depends on the embedding. Dimension is determined by the polynomial subring; "rank" measures how many sheets cover each point.

Normalization as dimension theory

The reason Noether normalization is more than a structural curiosity is that it makes algebraic geometry's notion of dimension agree with the geometric one. For a smooth variety over , the Krull dimension of its coordinate ring equals the topological dimension of the variety as a real manifold (divided by 2, since each algebraic dimension corresponds to two real dimensions). The agreement is not automatic: it is a theorem, and the theorem is Noether normalization plus some bookkeeping.

The framing also makes computations possible. Want to know the dimension of the variety ? Find a Noether normalization. Count the variables in the polynomial subring. That number is the dimension. No clever geometry required, only the ability to do generic linear substitutions and check what eliminates.

Noether normalization closes the foundational loop of Act III. Hilbert basis (#13) tells you that ideals are finitely generated, so quotient rings are well-defined coordinate rings. Noether normalization tells you that those coordinate rings are not exotic, each one is a finite cover of a perfectly ordinary polynomial ring, and the dimension of the polynomial ring is the dimension of the variety. From this point on, the techniques of multivariable calculus on affine space lift to any variety, by passing through the finite cover. That lift is what made the second half of twentieth-century algebraic geometry possible.
