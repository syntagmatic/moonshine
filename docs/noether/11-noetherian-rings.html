Once the staircase touches both axes, the monomials outside are finite and each strict step fills one, so their count is a budget. Before that there is none: is as long as you like. Every chain stops, but nothing says when.

Stopping is the same as finite generation

Three conditions on a ring are equivalent: (a) every ascending chain of ideals stabilizes; (b) every ideal is finitely generated; (c) every nonempty set of ideals has a maximal element.

For (b) ⇒ (a): the union of a chain is an ideal with finitely many generators, each appears at some step, and after the last one the chain cannot move. For (a) ⇒ (b): if has no finite generating set, pick , , , and so on forever. (a) and (c) are one statement read two ways: a set with no maximal element lets you climb forever, and an endless chain is such a set.
