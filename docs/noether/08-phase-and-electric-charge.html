Phase and Electric Charge

Part 8 of 13. Rotating the phase of a wave function is a symmetry that moves nothing in space, and its conserved quantity is charge. Making the rotation local leads to gauge theory.

Every symmetry so far moved something. The Schrödinger wave function has one that moves nothing: multiply it everywhere by one unit complex number,

and nothing measurable changes. Noether's theorem turns this internal symmetry into conservation of total probability, or of electric charge when the field is charged.
