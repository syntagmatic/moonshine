Noether's Second Theorem

Part 9 of 13. A symmetry whose parameter varies from point to point gives an identity among the equations of motion, not a new conservation law. That identity is gauge freedom, and it is why energy in general relativity is hard to pin down.

Noether's 1918 paper has two theorems. The first (Parts 5 to 8) takes a global symmetry, with a constant parameter, and gives a conserved current. The second takes a local symmetry, whose parameter is an arbitrary function, and gives an identity that the equations of motion satisfy whether or not they are solved.

How the second theorem came about

Hilbert and Klein brought Noether to Göttingen in the spring of 1915 for her invariant theory, while Hilbert worked toward the variational field equations he presented that November. Energy in the new theory stayed puzzling; Klein published his letters with Hilbert on it in 1918. Noether, refused a formal appointment because she was a woman and lecturing under Hilbert's name, answered it in Invariante Variationsprobleme (Nachr. Ges. Wiss. Göttingen, 1918): a local symmetry gives an identity among the field equations, not a conserved energy.

Charge conservation still comes from the first theorem, through the global part of the phase symmetry (Part 8); the local part makes it a consistency condition on sources.
