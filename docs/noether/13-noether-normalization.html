Noether Normalization

Part 13 of 13. Every finitely generated algebra over a field is a finite extension of a polynomial ring. The number of variables in that ring is the dimension of the variety, which gives a way to compute it.

Part 12 showed that every ideal in is finitely generated, so a quotient is the coordinate ring of an affine variety cut out by finitely many equations. Noether normalization (1926) says that such a coordinate ring is a finite extension of a polynomial ring in at most variables (fewer unless the ideal is zero), and the number of those variables is the dimension of the variety.

Why a generic linear change works

The construction in the proof is a piece of linear algebra. Given and a polynomial in the defining ideal, choose a generic linear change of variables

For generic constants , the leading term of after substitution becomes a pure power with nonzero coefficient. That makes integral over : it satisfies a monic polynomial equation in with coefficients in the smaller ring, which is what "finite as a module" needs. Generic constants exist when is infinite. Over a finite field the proof substitutes powers instead, such as for a large integer , and the theorem still holds. If the remaining variables still satisfy a polynomial relation in , repeat the substitution with that relation. Each round removes one variable, and the process stops when the remaining variables are algebraically independent. They generate a polynomial subring over which the algebra is a finite module.

The number is the Krull dimension of , the length of the longest strictly ascending chain of prime ideals in , or equivalently (for finitely generated ) the transcendence degree of the fraction field of over . Both definitions return the same number, and Noether normalization is the structural fact that makes them equivalent.

In every row there is a polynomial subring with equal to the dimension of the variety, and the algebra is a finite module over it. The rank depends on the choice of subring and counts how many sheets lie over a generic point.

Normalization as dimension theory

Noether normalization helps make the algebraic notion of dimension agree with the geometric one. For a smooth variety over , the Krull dimension of its coordinate ring is half the dimension of the variety as a real manifold, since each complex dimension is two real ones, and normalization is a main tool in proving this.

It also makes dimension computable. To find the dimension of the variety , find a Noether normalization and count the variables in the polynomial subring. That needs only generic linear substitutions and elimination.

Together with Hilbert's basis theorem (Part 12), this completes Act III. Ideals in polynomial rings are finitely generated, so quotients are coordinate rings of varieties, and each coordinate ring is a finite cover of an ordinary polynomial ring whose number of variables is the dimension of the variety.
