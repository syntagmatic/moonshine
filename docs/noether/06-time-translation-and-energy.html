Energy, Momentum and Angular Momentum

Part 6 of 13. The three oldest conservation laws in physics, read off a Lagrangian. If , energy is conserved, and the theorem says which quantity "energy" is. Shifts and rotations of space give momentum and angular momentum.

Energy is older than Noether's theorem. Leibniz argued for vis viva, Lagrange and Hamilton built mechanics around it, and a bouncing ball visibly trades kinetic energy for potential and back. What the theorem adds is a test: if a Lagrangian does not mention explicitly, some energy is conserved. It also names the conserved quantity. That quantity is the Hamiltonian, which is often but not always.
