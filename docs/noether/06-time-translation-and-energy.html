Time Translation and Energy

Part 6 of 15. The oldest conservation law in physics, read off a Lagrangian. If , energy is conserved — and Noether tells you what "energy" actually means.

Energy is older than Noether's theorem. Leibniz argued for vis viva, Lagrange and Hamilton built a whole mechanics around it, and every high-school student knows that a bouncing ball swaps kinetic for potential and back. What Noether added is a diagnostic: you can look at a Lagrangian and decide, by inspecting whether it mentions explicitly, whether an energy conservation law exists at all. And when it does, her theorem tells you the exact quantity that is conserved — not always , sometimes something subtler, always the Hamiltonian.

The symmetry and what it says

Time translation is the transformation . It is not a configuration-space transformation like the ones in explainer 5 — it shifts the parameter of the path, not the path's values. That changes the bookkeeping enough that the cleanest derivation goes directly through the Euler-Lagrange equations, without dressing the calculation up as a Noether formula.
