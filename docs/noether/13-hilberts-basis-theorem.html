Gordan's lament

Hilbert's 1890 paper proved the basis theorem (in a slightly different form, framed in invariant theory) and immediately deduced that the ring of invariants of any binary form under is finitely generated. This was the subject Paul Gordan — Noether's doctoral advisor — had spent his career on, computing generators by hand for forms of low degree and hoping for explicit formulas in higher degrees. Hilbert's proof produced no formulas, no generators, no bounds; it just announced that finitely many existed.

Gordan is reported (in Constance Reid's 1970 biography of Hilbert, and earlier accounts) to have said:

"Das ist nicht Mathematik, das ist Theologie."
"This is not mathematics, this is theology."

The complaint was about non-constructivity: Hilbert proved the existence of a finite generating set without producing one. Gordan's school had been computing invariants by hand for decades, and what Hilbert showed was that the entire research program had a uniform answer. The community was slow to accept it. Within a few years, however, Hilbert had also given an alternative, more constructive treatment, and by 1900 the basis theorem was a foundational result.

The historical irony: Emmy Noether, whose 1907 dissertation was written under Gordan on exactly the kind of explicit invariant-theory calculation Gordan favored, would spend her career building the abstract framework Hilbert had pioneered. Her 1921 Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen — which gave the modern formulation of the basis theorem in terms of the chain condition — is the moment the abstract method became the working language of algebra.

Hilbert's basis theorem is the theorem that turns "Noetherian" from an abstract condition into a productive one. By itself, "every chain of ideals stabilizes" is a definition; in the presence of the basis theorem, it becomes a feature you can iterate, lift, and depend on. Every Noetherian ring you meet in algebraic geometry — coordinate rings of varieties, localizations of polynomial rings, formal power series — is Noetherian because the basis theorem says so, applied to a base ring like or .
