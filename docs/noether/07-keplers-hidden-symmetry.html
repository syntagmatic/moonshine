Kepler's Hidden Symmetry

Part 7 of 13. Inverse-square orbits conserve a vector that no rotation or translation explains. The symmetry behind it, a four-dimensional rotation group, also explains the degeneracies in the hydrogen spectrum.

The Kepler problem, a particle pulled toward a fixed center by an inverse-square force, conserves one more quantity than the symmetries of Part 6 account for: the Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector. In January 1926 Pauli used it to derive the hydrogen spectrum from matrix mechanics, submitting ten days before Schrödinger submitted his wave-equation solution.

The vector is old: Jakob Hermann and Johann Bernoulli found it around 1710, Laplace used it in 1799, Hamilton rediscovered it in the 1840s. The symmetry stayed hidden because it does not act on positions alone.
