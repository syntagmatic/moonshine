Invariants of a Binary Form

Part 2 of 13. A linear change of variables scrambles the coefficients of a binary form, but its discriminant only picks up a power of the determinant. Noether's doctoral thesis studied invariants like this.

In 1907 Emmy Noether finished her doctoral dissertation at Erlangen under Paul Gordan, Über die Bildung des Formensystems der ternären biquadratischen Form, on the complete system of invariant forms of a quartic in three variables. This part works through the smallest cases of that subject: forms in two variables.

What the discriminant detects

Over the complex numbers a binary form splits into linear factors, and the discriminant vanishes exactly when two of them coincide. Since , a real substitution also cannot change the sign of a cubic's discriminant, and that sign counts the real zero lines: three when positive, one when negative.

Gordan and Hilbert

Gordan proved in 1868, by explicit construction, that the invariants of any binary form are generated by a finite list; for the quartic that list is just and . In 1890 Hilbert proved finite generation for forms in any number of variables with an argument that produced no list at all, and Gordan distrusted it; Part 12 tells that story.

Hilbert's argument rests on an ascending chain condition for ideals in a polynomial ring. In 1921 Noether made that condition the axiom of a general theory of ideals, the subject of Part 11. The rest of Act I turns to physics, where the object a group acts on is a Lagrangian.
